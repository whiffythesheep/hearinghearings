Summary

Meeting Overview

The Committee on Disabilities, chaired by CM Shahana Hanif, held a joint hearing with the Committee on Parks and Recreation on November 24, 2026, examining the accessibility of city parks, playgrounds, and recreation centers. The hearing considered four bills and a resolution honoring disability rights advocate Becky Ogle. The administration's testimony was led by Juliana Titus, Assistant Commissioner for Diversity, Equity, Inclusion and Belonging at Parks, and Eric Borsten, Deputy Commissioner for Capital Projects, joined by representatives from the Mayor's Office for People with Disabilities.

The opening statements established a damning baseline: the Parks Department was required to complete an ADA transition plan by January 1995. It did not finalize one until June 2024, more than thirty years late. The 2023 State Comptroller audit found 64% of previously identified barriers remained uncorrected, that the transition plan lacked every federally required element including a list of barriers, a remediation schedule, and a named responsible official, and that Parks' own website listed only 22 of 63 facilities that auditors independently verified as accessible. A federal lawsuit over Van Cortlandt House Museum, filed by the U.S. Attorney for the Southern District, eventually settled in a consent agreement requiring the site to become accessible within five years - precisely the kind of site-by-site enforcement the committee said should be unnecessary. The administration's response throughout the hearing oscillated between genuine candor about the scale of the problem and somewhat evasive answers about accountability structures, with Deputy Commissioner Borsten being notably honest about the depth of deferred maintenance across the portfolio.

The four bills drew mixed responses. Int 0212-2026, requiring an annual accessibility assessment and public map of every park facility, was met with resistance from Parks on capacity grounds, claiming it would amount to a perpetual survey of 30,000 acres that would divert staff from actual improvements. Committee members pushed back, arguing that perpetual assessment was precisely the point. Int 1193-2026, requiring at least two accessible swings in playgrounds by 2033 and 2036 respectively, was largely supported in principle, with Parks noting nearly half of its approximately 900 playgrounds with swing sets already have accessible swings, but raising a drafting concern that the bill as written would require accessible swings even at playgrounds with no swing set at all. Int 1177-2026, extending reduced recreation center fees to active military personnel and their minor children, received favorable comments from the administration, though a fiscal impact assessment has not been completed. Parks confirmed the current anomaly whereby a 30-year-old veteran pays $25 for annual membership while a 30-year-old active duty Marine pays $150. The resolution honoring Becky Ogle was not substantively debated.

Questioning by committee members revealed a series of structural weaknesses in the department's accessibility governance. The Parks ADA advisory committee has been inactive since COVID, now approximately six years. The department has a single ADA coordinator embedded in the capital division whose role is not formally documented, who does not sign off on capital projects, and whose primary function is to advise designers rather than to survey existing facilities. There are two disability service facilitators who handle complaints across all 30,000 acres, receive between 20 and 50 accommodation requests per year, and track them on a spreadsheet - a figure that Council members found implausibly low given the volume of accessibility complaints reaching their own offices. The department confirmed it has no unified accessibility survey of its facilities, that inspection staff are not trained in granular ADA compliance, and that there is no formal mechanism for recording when a project sought an exception to accessibility standards. Borsten's willingness to describe the capital maintenance funding gap as "staggering" and historically insoluble was notable, if somewhat deflecting, given that the institutional failures catalogued by auditors over two decades are not purely a function of money.

Numbers

- The Parks Department was legally required to complete its ADA transition plan by January 1995; it did not do so until June 2024, more than 30 years late.
- The 2023 State Comptroller audit found 64% of accessibility barriers previously identified by Parks remained uncorrected 12 or more years after identification.
- The Comptroller's auditors found 63 of 85 facilities accessible on inspection; Parks' own website listed only 22 as accessible.
- Parks manages over 30,000 acres of land, representing 14% of New York City's landmass.
- Parks stewards over 5,000 individual properties, including approximately 1,000 playgrounds, 14 miles of public beaches, 66 pools, and dozens of recreation centers.
- The Parks ADA transition plan identifies 908 of 1,016 playgrounds as having the accessibility features Parks wants, though this is not equivalent to federal compliance.
- Parks has fully evaluated 175 of its 622 public restroom buildings, 17 of 36 recreation centers operating at the time of the plan, and 11 of 48 pools - fewer than half in each category.
- The Parks capital budget averages over $500 million per year for facility improvements over the past decade; the ten-year capital plan is approximately $12 billion.
- Six new recreation centers are under construction or recently opened, with an estimated total investment of $500 million.
- Of 37 recreation centers currently operated by Parks, 5 lack an accessible route between floors for the public; 2 of those 5 (Almitz in Manhattan and Owen Dolan in the Bronx) are slated for active capital repair.
- Approximately 900 of Parks' playgrounds have swing sets; nearly half currently have accessible swings.
- Of Parks' outdoor pool portfolio, 41 of approximately 53 pools have working lifts; all 14 Olympic and 22 intermediate pools have lifts; 5 of 17 mini pools have lifts; 9 of 14 Olympic pools have ramp entries into the pool basin.
- Parks conducts up to 6,000 park inspections per year through its Parks Inspection Program; a typical property is inspected 2 to 3 times annually.
- Parks disability service facilitators receive between 20 and 50 accommodation requests or complaints per year across all parks properties, tracked on an Excel spreadsheet.
- In fiscal year 2026 to date, Parks received approximately 50 accommodation requests or complaints; 27 in 2025; 49 in 2024.
- Annual recreation center membership for adults aged 25 to 61 costs $150, with a 10% discount for ID card holders; veterans and people with disabilities pay $25 per year; active duty military currently pay $150.
- Of 57 capital projects with accessibility improvements anticipated in the 2024-25 Accessibility Progress Report, 47 have been completed and one remains in active construction.
- The Parks ADA advisory committee has been inactive since COVID, approximately six years.
- Parks employs a capital division of approximately 450 staff; there is one dedicated ADA coordinator in that division.
- The Brownsville Recreation Center, the last of the six new centers, is scheduled for completion in 2030.
- All 8 of Parks' public beaches have mobility mats in multiple locations; beach wheelchairs are available at no cost with a new online reservation system.

Action Points

- Parks to provide the committee with the list of 108 playgrounds referenced in Comptroller findings, including schedule and funding status for each.
- Parks to provide the committee with accommodation requests and complaints data for the last three fiscal years, broken down by year.
- Parks to provide the committee with a status update on the reasonable accommodation requests received in the current fiscal year, including which have been resolved and which remain pending.
- Parks to share with the committee the capital needs assessment rankings for recreation centers by condition and neighborhood.
- Parks to investigate and provide the committee with a formal description of the ADA coordinator's duties, including whether these are attached to a civil service title description.
- Parks to provide the committee with a status update on the inventory completion progress for each asset class, using the Historic House Trust study as a model.
- Parks to provide the committee with a breakdown of the yearly expense-side repair budget related to accessibility and park maintenance.
- Parks to report back on how many recreation centers and major parks have accessible drop-off spots and accessible routes from the nearest accessible subway station.
- Parks to calculate and share the average cost of installing a pool chairlift and confirm how many facilities that should have chairlifts are in compliance.
- Parks to report back on how many of the 49 capital projects anticipated in the 2024-25 progress report have now been completed.
- Parks to review and report back on the 60-day complaint filing deadline for people with disabilities, with a view to extending it to cover ongoing accessibility barriers.
- Parks to clarify how the March to May 2024 public comment period for the ADA transition plan was advertised, and to commit to more robust outreach for future comment periods.
- Parks to establish a timeline to reactivate the Parks ADA advisory committee, and to identify which division will lead it.
- Parks and MOPD to provide the committee with the feedback MOPD gave on Parks' Local Law 12 accessibility plan and confirm whether that feedback was adopted.
- Parks to assess the fiscal impact of extending the reduced recreation center membership fee to active duty military personnel and their minor children, in consultation with OMB and the Department of Veterans Services, and report back to the committee.
- Parks to confirm whether jointly operated school playgrounds are covered by the ADA transition plan and by the assessment that Int 0212-2026 would require.
- Parks to work with the committee to revise the drafting of Int 1193-2026 to address the concern that the bill as written would require accessible swings at playgrounds with no swing set.
