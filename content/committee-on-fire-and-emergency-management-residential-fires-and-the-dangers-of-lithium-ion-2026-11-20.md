Summary

Meeting Overview

The joint Committee on Fire and Emergency Management and Committee on Housing and Buildings held an emergency oversight hearing on November 20, 2026 on residential fires and lithium-ion battery dangers, prompted by a sharp rise in fire fatalities and injuries in 2026. Speaker Julie Menin called the hearing explicitly because deaths and injuries are accelerating despite years of Council legislation, and the tone throughout was one of genuine alarm rather than routine oversight. Two fires in particular drove the urgency: an October 28 Bronx fire that killed a child, two teenagers, and a 33-year-old mother, caused by an e-bike charging in a kitchen doorway; and a November 5 Sunset Park fire that killed six people, including a recently arrived immigrant family personally known to the sponsoring Council Member, in a building with illegally subdivided apartments. Both fires occurred within days of each other.

FDNY Commissioner Lillian Bonsignore testified alongside First Deputy Commissioner Daniel Flynn and senior FDNY officials, plus representatives from the Department of Housing Preservation and Development and the Department of Buildings. The Commissioner outlined the causes of the 90 fire fatalities recorded in 2026 to date: 19 from incendiary fires, 18 from electrical issues, nine from lithium-ion batteries, seven from smoking, five from candles, and four from cooking. She noted aggravating structural factors including older buildings with pipe chases and cockloft spaces, non-compliant self-closing doors, heavy clutter, and illegally subdivided apartments. The Sunset Park fire in particular was complicated by apartment subdivisions, locked bedroom doors, and window bars that required firefighters to physically remove them to rescue two survivors. The fire's cause remains under investigation, and the Commissioner explicitly cautioned against assuming it was battery-related.

Seven bills were heard. Int 0920 (Ariola/De La Rosa) would remove the Fire Code exemption allowing up to five mobility devices to be stored and charged inside residential units; FDNY expressed concern about enforceability and the lack of safe outdoor alternatives for many residents, but indicated willingness to work toward a balanced version. Int 1174 (Sanchez) would create a public FDNY fire data dashboard; FDNY broadly supported the concept but flagged resource and data-completeness concerns. Int 1175 (Sanchez) would require a study of electrical capacity in older residential buildings; the Department of Buildings acknowledged the importance of the issue but noted significant access and implementation challenges. Int 0582 (De La Rosa/Gutiérrez) would require commercial bicycle operators to be provided fireproof battery containers; FDNY noted no UL-certified product currently exists that can contain a lithium-ion battery fire, making the bill unenforceable as written. Int 1189 (Gennaro) would increase penalties for selling uncertified devices and create civil penalties for possession; FDNY and the administration opposed the possession element, arguing it would disproportionately harm low-income delivery workers who may not know their batteries are uncertified and that post-sale certification is practically impossible to verify. The sealing-threshold bill (Zhuang) lowering the trigger from three violations in three years to two violations in two years received explicit FDNY support. The outreach and enforcement plan bill (Ariola) was broadly welcomed by the administration.

A significant flashpoint was the discontinued DOT battery trade-in pilot program. The Speaker demanded to know why a program the administration described as effective and valuable had been terminated; no one on the administration panel could answer. It eventually emerged that the program was a legislatively mandated two-year pilot that concluded in 2025, but no one had made a decision to renew it. The Speaker directed the administration to return an answer by end of day and noted, pointedly, that no Council legislation is needed for a commissioner to continue a successful program. The hearing also surfaced a troubling intelligence gap: FDNY could not say what proportion of e-bikes in use are uncertified, cannot determine post-fire whether a battery was certified, and acknowledged uncertainty about whether the main source of uncertified devices is now informal or black-market sales rather than storefronts. Council Member Feliz pressed this point at length, arguing that the City's enforcement efforts have been aimed at the wrong target if the problem has migrated away from retail. The passage of a federal lithium-ion battery safety bill the previous evening was cited by FDNY as potentially significant in preventing uncertified devices from entering the country.

Numbers

- 90 fire fatalities in New York City in 2026 as of the date of the hearing, compared to 63 in 2025, 77 in 2024, 100 in 2023, and 97 in 2022.
- 25,000-plus structural fires in Fiscal Year 2026, of which more than 21,000 occurred in residential occupancies, an increase of nearly 1,300 over the prior fiscal year.
- More than 2,200 of the 2026 fires were serious fires requiring at least 60 response personnel.
- 82 civilian fatalities from fires in 2026 as of the hearing date, described as nearly double the number at the same point in 2025, plus 54 life-threatening or serious civilian injuries.
- 421 lithium-ion battery fires in 2026 as of November, up from 292 in all of 2025, 279 in 2024, and 268 in 2023.
- Nine fatalities attributed to lithium-ion battery fires in 2026 to date, compared to one in 2025, six in 2024, and 18 in 2023.
- 83 injuries from lithium-ion battery fires in 2026, up from 51 in 2025.
- 100 lithium-ion battery fires in 2026 occurred in the back of sanitation trucks.
- 19 of 2026 fire fatalities resulted from incendiary fires; 18 from electrical issues including wiring and power strips; nine from lithium-ion batteries; seven from smoking; five from candles; four from cooking.
- FDNY conducted 471 lithium-ion-related inspections in 2026 to date, issuing 255 summonses, five violation orders, four criminal summonses, and identifying eight locations meeting the threshold for sealing.
- Inspection totals by year: 467 in 2023, 585 in 2024, 843 in 2025, 871 in 2026 to date.
- FDNY received 301 lithium-ion-related complaints in 2026, conducting 117 complaint-driven inspections, with a target response time of 12 hours.
- FDNY has approximately 140 fire marshals conducting approximately 5,000 fire investigations per year, of which around 2,000 involve called fires.
- Since 2022, FDNY received over 900 complaints related to lithium-ion battery charging and storage.
- FDNY conducted 3,242 fire safety presentations in 2026 reaching an audience of approximately 433,000 people, plus 970 school events reaching over 100,000 students.
- Six certified outdoor battery cabinet models are in use across 22 sites in New York City; all new cabinets must now meet UL Standard 9487, introduced in early 2025.
- The administration previously committed funding for the DOT battery trade-in program, described as targeting replacement of more than 400 unsafe devices in 2025; that pilot ran for two years and was concluded in 2025.
- DCWP issued at least 86 cease-and-desist letters to online retailers selling non-compliant products; 26 came into compliance; at least 41 summonses were issued to non-compliant online sellers.
- 270 Dyckman Street, the site of a May 4 fatal fire killing four people, had 117 open violations and had been placed in the Alternative Enforcement Program; inspectors cited broken self-closing doors three days before the fire.
- Electrical wiring has caused approximately one in six fires investigated by FDNY, making it the second leading cause after arson; lithium-ion batteries account for 6.7 percent of fires in 2026.
- The NYC Department of Buildings Chapter 33 construction safety provisions run to 167 pages, compared to seven pages in the International Building Code.
- FDNY's Fire Safety Friday app provides conversational translation in approximately 28 languages.
- The DOT Irrevocable Consent Program for outdoor battery charging stations is reportedly being extended following recent discussions.

Action Points

- FDNY and DOT officials directed by Speaker Menin to report back to the committees by end of day on why the DOT battery trade-in pilot program was not renewed after its two-year conclusion in 2025.
- FDNY to provide the committees with data on the typical response and resolution process for lithium-ion battery complaints, including whether and how complainants are notified of outcomes.
- FDNY to provide the date of the most recent inspection of e-bike retailers in the Sunset Park area, in response to a question from Council Member Zhuang.
- FDNY to follow up with the committees on data showing how many of its 471 inspections this year resulted in confirmed closure of dangerous conditions, to address the Speaker's question about inspection effectiveness.
- FDNY Commissioner to pursue with DOT the possibility of reinstating or extending the battery trade-in program without requiring new Council legislation.
- FDNY Chief of Fire Prevention to share data with the relevant Council Members on average timelines for approvals of outdoor battery storage cabinet applications under the letter-of-no-objection process.
- Department of Buildings First Deputy Commissioner to keep the Housing and Buildings Committee informed of the timeline and results of the third-party engineering investigation into the 121 Flatbush Avenue construction collapse.
- FDNY to follow up with Council Member Wang on the specific enforceability challenges with Int 0920 and to work with the sponsor to develop language that eliminates indoor charging and storage without creating unenforceable mandates.
- FDNY to work with Council Member Sanchez on refining the scope of Int 1174 to define covered incidents in a way that is both publicly useful and operationally manageable.
- HPD to review and report on current practices for distributing lithium-ion battery safety materials to tenants during building inspections, following Chair Ariola's criticism that outreach is not reaching residents at a sufficient level.
- FDNY to follow up on whether there is an existing mechanism to require sellers to notify buyers of non-compliant products that were erroneously sold, and to report back to the committee on the city's existing legal authority in this area.
- FDNY to share its social media recruitment toolkit with committee members and Council Members willing to amplify FDNY Fire Safety Friday messaging and firefighter recruitment outreach on social media.
- DOT to be invited separately by both committee chairs and the Speaker to discuss the trade-in program, the status of the Irrevocable Consent Program for outdoor charging stations, and the timeline for planned funding of outdoor storage facilities currently scheduled for 2028.
- DCWP to meet separately with both committee chairs and the Speaker, following their inability to attend the hearing, to discuss enforcement authority and the remaining legislative package.
- FDNY to continue and, if possible, expand its forthcoming five-week paid media campaign on lithium-ion battery safety, with particular attention to ethnic media and non-English-language outlets, and to report to the committees on reach achieved.
