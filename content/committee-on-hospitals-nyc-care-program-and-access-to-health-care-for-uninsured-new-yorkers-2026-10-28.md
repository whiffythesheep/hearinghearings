Summary

Meeting Overview

The Committee on Hospitals held an oversight hearing on the NYC Care Program and access to health care for uninsured New Yorkers. The hearing was convened against an urgent backdrop: federal cuts under H.R. 1 (the One Big Beautiful Bill) have already caused roughly 197,000 New Yorkers to lose Essential Plan coverage, and upcoming Medicaid changes taking effect January 1, 2027, including six-month recertification requirements and new work requirements, could affect as many as 900,000 New Yorkers. Chair Mercedes Narcisse framed the hearing as an attempt to understand how NYC Care is currently performing, what operational gaps exist, and what additional investment or policy action may be needed as the City braces for a significant expansion of the uninsured population.

Testimony was provided by Dr. Ted Long, Chief Medical Officer for Clinical Services and Population Health at NYC Health and Hospitals, and Dr. Jonathan Jimenez, Executive Director of NYC Care and a family physician at H+H Gotham Health. Both witnesses offered detailed accounts of the program's operations, outcomes, and preparedness strategy. NYC Care, the nation's largest and most comprehensive program guaranteeing health care regardless of immigration status or ability to pay, currently has approximately 120,000 members. It launched in the Bronx in 2019, expanded citywide in 2020, and initially surpassed its enrollment goal of 100,000, peaking at around 150,000 members before declining as some members successfully transitioned to Medicaid. The witnesses emphasized that this decline is partly a success story, not a failure, though they acknowledged that federal policy changes will now reverse that dynamic, pushing people off insurance and back toward NYC Care.

The most substantive policy tension in the hearing concerned whether the program has the capacity and funding to absorb a surge in demand. The witnesses confirmed that NYC Care operates on a budget of $100 million per year, and that current primary care capacity could absorb approximately 15,000 to 20,000 new patients if all existing provider panels were filled. They described a range of operational improvements designed to expand effective capacity without simply adding providers: team-based care models, e-consult systems that allow one in five specialty referrals to be resolved without an in-person visit, time-released appointment scheduling to reduce no-show rates, and a contact center that has unlocked 4,000 new behavioral health appointments. On language access, the program offers telephonic interpretation in over 300 languages and dialects through its 646-NYC-CARE number. Council Member Epstein pressed hard on the Bronx specifically, noting its 7.1 percent unemployment rate and its vulnerability to Medicaid work requirements; witnesses confirmed that New York State has applied for a work requirement exemption for the Bronx, though it has not yet been approved.

Council Member Farías raised the most pointed financial question of the hearing: H+H is pursuing the absorption of Maimonides Medical Center, whose financial stability is explicitly premised on collecting enhanced Medicaid reimbursement rates. If hundreds of thousands of patients lose Medicaid eligibility and become uninsured, the financial plan for Maimonides is materially undermined, and NYC Care reimbursement does not replace Medicaid revenue. The witnesses acknowledged this concern directly and candidly, committing to follow up in writing with financial projections. No Council member pushed for a specific legislative or budgetary fix during the hearing, though the witnesses confirmed that City Hall is actively engaged on the fiscal implications of H.R. 1 for the NYC Care program. The witnesses also highlighted supplementary H+H programs including the SHOW street medicine program (being expanded by 50 percent), Safety Net Clinics at Bellevue, Lincoln, Woodhull, and Elmhurst, and the Housing for Health initiative, which has housed 1,800 patients and built 400 units on H+H land.

Numbers

- NYC Care currently has approximately 120,766 members as of October 23, 2026.
- The program peaked at approximately 150,000 members before declining; the decline of roughly 25,000 members is primarily attributable to successful transitions to New York State Medicaid for members aged 65 and above.
- An estimated 197,000 New Yorkers have already lost coverage following the enactment of H.R. 1.
- City officials estimate that as many as 900,000 New Yorkers could be affected by Medicaid changes taking effect January 1, 2027.
- Approximately 450,000 New Yorkers lost Essential Plan coverage when it ended on July 1, 2026.
- As of September 4, 2026, approximately 59 percent of former Essential Plan enrollees had found a new insurance plan, meaning roughly 40 percent had not.
- H+H has sent nearly 400,000 emails to patients about upcoming Medicaid changes; more than half were opened.
- NYC Care's annual budget is $100 million, unchanged from a couple of years after the program's inception.
- Current primary care capacity across H+H could absorb approximately 15,000 to 20,000 new patients if all provider panels were filled to the standard panel size of 1,750 patients per full-time provider.
- More than 90 percent of NYC Care enrollees since 2019 have been offered a primary care appointment within two weeks of calling.
- Three quarters of NYC Care members complete a primary care appointment within a year of enrollment; 80 percent complete a specialty visit within the first year.
- NYC Care has had over one million community outreach interactions via contracted community-based organizations and members have completed over one million primary care appointments since 2019.
- 73 percent of new NYC Care enrollees diagnosed with hypertension at their first visit show significant improvement within six months; 53 percent of those newly diagnosed with diabetes show significant improvement within six months.
- A peer-reviewed study published in the Journal of General Internal Medicine in 2025 found identical chronic disease outcomes for new NYC Care enrollees versus new Medicaid enrollees, with a signal (not statistically significant) toward lower hospital admission rates for NYC Care members.
- The NYC Care Program is estimated to have saved 1,569 lives to date.
- 77 percent of NYC Care members have completed colorectal cancer screening, compared to 78 percent for the general H+H population.
- Over 20,500 NYC Care members have utilized the durable medical equipment benefit in its first year since launching in 2025.
- 71 percent of NYC Care members are Hispanic or Latino; 13 percent are Black or African American.
- The SHOW street medicine program is being expanded by 50 percent in units deployed daily in 2026.
- H+H's Housing for Health program has housed 1,800 patients and built 400 housing units on H+H land.
- The contact center optimization for behavioral health has unlocked approximately 4,000 new appointments that would not otherwise have existed.
- Approximately one quarter of H+H's highest-risk patients screen positive for a social need such as food insecurity or housing instability.
- The Bronx has an unemployment rate of 7.1 percent, the highest in New York City, raising particular concern about Medicaid work requirements.
- H+H Morrisania clinic achieved the best blood pressure control rate in New York State; hypertensive control at that clinic is 84 percent.
