Summary

Meeting Overview

The Committee on Education held an oversight hearing on Portrait of a Graduate, the New York State framework adopted by the Board of Regents in July 2025 that will replace the Regents exam graduation requirement beginning with students graduating in the 2027-28 school year. The hearing examined how New York City Public Schools is preparing to implement the framework across the nation's largest school district, on a timeline the state itself has not fully defined. Chair Dinowitz, a former special education teacher, framed the core tension clearly: the Regents system was a poor measure of what students actually know and can do, but removing it without a rigorous, consistent and well-communicated replacement risks turning graduation into a compliance checklist driven by pressure to inflate rates rather than deepen learning.

The Department of Education's panel, led by First Deputy Chancellor and including supervising superintendent of high schools Alan Chang, chief of student pathways Jane Martinez Dowling and senior executive director of state and federal policy Sharon Wrencher, described a multi-year implementation effort branded as New York Inspires. They testified that all superintendents and principals have been briefed on the timeline and six Portrait attributes, that over 1,600 educators attended a summer institute, and that personal finance education - the first concrete Phase 2 requirement, effective this school year for grades 5 through 12 - is now scheduled for roughly 50 percent of middle and high school students this fall. However, under sustained questioning from Dinowitz, CM Krishnan, CM Joseph, CM Narcisse, CM Wong and CM Brewer, the DOE struggled to answer basic questions: there is no published implementation plan, no announced professional development budget, no inter-visitation structure yet stood up to spread portfolio-based practice from schools that already do it to those that do not, and families and counselors have received little usable guidance on what students currently in grades 9 through 11 need to do to graduate. The DOE's most honest moment came when a senior official acknowledged there is "a little ambiguity" in what principals are expected to do right now, a characterization Brewer pushed back on sharply, describing the ambiguity as large rather than small. Dinowitz warned explicitly about the risk of repeating COVID-era patterns in which administrative pressure translated into passing students who had not met standards, and pressed for normed, centrally reviewed rubrics rather than school-by-school improvisation.

The hearing also featured extensive testimony from affinity network operators and consortium schools, including the New York Performance Standards Consortium, Internationals Network, New York City Outward Bound Schools, Urban Assembly and New Visions for Public Schools. A common theme across all of them was that the expertise to implement the Portrait well already exists within the city's own school networks, has been developed over decades and is available to the DOE, but is not being systematically leveraged. Brooklyn South supervising superintendent Michael Prayer provided the hearing's most concrete example of what Portrait-aligned learning looks like at scale, describing the HEAT program in which cohorts of students from across Brooklyn South work with partners including the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, Cisco and Maimonides Hospital to conduct research and defend findings before expert panels. Roughly 400 of approximately 10,000 eleventh graders in Brooklyn South participate, illustrating the scale challenge vividly. The panel of affinity network leaders was direct: the DOE superintendent accountability structure is organized around operational compliance rather than innovation, and that needs to change for the Portrait to be implemented well.

A large and highly effective panel of student journalists from schools including Murrow High School, Brooklyn Emerging Leaders Academy, Bard High School Early College Manhattan, the High School for Environmental Studies, Medgar Evers College Preparatory School, Bronx High School of Science and others testified in support of integrating journalism education into the Portrait framework. Their testimony was among the hearing's most substantively compelling, demonstrating in real time the critical thinking, research, civic engagement and communication skills the Portrait calls for. The Youth Journalism Coalition and Press Pass testified alongside them, noting that 73 percent of city high schools lack a journalism program, that the DOE has not provided updated statistics on this despite a legal mandate to do so, and that the recently launched Journalism for All initiative - funded at $280,000 by the City Council across 30 schools - is a model for scaling access. Students with disabilities, multilingual learners and students in foster care were highlighted as groups requiring specific attention, with foster care agency New York Foundling reporting that its Road to Success program achieved an 88 percent graduation rate for students in care compared to the citywide rate of 42 percent for that population, underscoring how much targeted support matters. Skeptical testimony from a Manhattan Institute fellow and from Trust in New York called for a delay in decoupling the Regents from graduation until the DOE can demonstrate it has a rigorous, comparable replacement in place - a concern the chair acknowledged even as he expressed support for the direction of the reform.

- Full Portrait of a Graduate implementation, including the new transcript requirement, is targeted for 2029-30.
- New York State has approximately 20-plus states nationally engaged in similar graduation requirement reviews.

- Approximately 190 schools across the city participate in Future Ready New York City programming.
- More than 150 schools are affiliated with one of the city's affinity network partnerships.
- The DOE's summer institute for Portrait of a Graduate implementation reached over 1,600 educators.
- Approximately 50 percent of middle and high school students have personal finance instruction scheduled for the fall 2026 semester.
- Personal finance education is required for grades 5 through 12 starting in the 2026-27 school year and for grades K through 4 in 2027-28.
- Climate education requirements take effect for grades 5 through 12 in 2027-28 and for grades K through 4 in 2028-29.
- Roughly 400 eleventh graders out of an estimated 10,000 in Brooklyn South participate in the HEAT program annually.
- The DOE reports approximately 90,000 students currently receiving work-based learning experiences citywide.
- Students have earned approximately $85 million over the past five years through career-connected learning, combining tuition savings and direct wages.

- Career Ready has provided paid summer work experiences to approximately 23,000 students across 230 high schools.

- New York Foundling's Road to Success program achieved an 88 percent graduation rate for students in foster care, against a citywide foster care graduation rate of 42 percent.

- The City Council has invested $280,000 in the Journalism for All initiative, currently operating in 30 schools.
- Journalism for All's summer newsroom internship program placed 36 students in paid internships across 11 local newsrooms.

- 90 percent of Journalism for All summer participants said they would recommend the program to a classmate.
- Internationals Network supports 17 schools in Queens, Brooklyn, the Bronx and Manhattan.

Action Points

- DOE to publish a written Portrait of a Graduate implementation plan and release it publicly, per CM Joseph's request.
- DOE to develop and release a comprehensive communications plan for students, families and counselors, with particular urgency for the classes of 2027 and 2028.
- DOE to establish a structured inter-visitation program enabling principals and teachers from non-consortium schools to observe portfolio-based assessment practice in consortium, international and Outward Bound schools.
- DOE to develop and publish centrally reviewed, normed rubrics for Portrait-aligned assessments to ensure consistency across schools, per sustained questioning from Dinowitz and Brewer.
- DOE to confirm and publish specific professional development plan and associated budget for Portrait implementation.
- DOE to provide updated statistics on journalism program access across high schools, as required by Local Law 272, correcting the incomplete report identified by the Youth Journalism Coalition.
- DOE to refine methodology for tracking school journalism offerings to accurately capture clubs, publications and specific course names, not only standalone courses.
- DOE to confirm research as a mandatory assessed component of Portrait-aligned student work, not merely an optional feature of individual school programs, following Dinowitz's direct question and DOE's affirmative answer.
- DOE to ensure asset mapping of school capacity and existing Portrait-aligned practices is completed and used to inform scaling strategy, with Dinowitz noting it should already be done.
- DOE to treat affinity network organizations including the Performance Standards Consortium, Internationals Network, Outward Bound and Urban Assembly as active partners in developing rubrics, professional development and implementation guidance, not merely as service recipients.
- DOE to resist awarding large professional development contracts to outside private vendors where equivalent or superior expertise exists within the city's own educator networks, per Dinowitz's explicit request.
- DOE to protect Affinity network contract funding in future budget cycles, per Dinowitz's request, given the network's centrality to Portrait implementation.
- DOE to ensure students with disabilities are held to the same Portrait standards as other students through multiple assessment modalities rather than a reduced standard, per the DOE's own stated intention confirmed during questioning.
- DOE to scale career-connected and work-based learning opportunities with particular attention to equitable geographic access, ensuring schools in under-resourced neighborhoods have the same employer partnership opportunities as those near major business districts.
- DOE to coordinate more directly with the Performance Standards Consortium to ensure that centrally reviewed rubrics and norming practices from the Consortium's 30-year model inform citywide Portrait assessment design.
- Chair Dinowitz to hold a follow-up hearing on civics education and the Seal of Civic Readiness in October, as announced during the hearing.
- Chair Dinowitz to share chronic absenteeism data follow-up with CM Wong, per DOE commitment to follow up given they did not have the data available at the hearing.
