Summary

Meeting Overview

The Committee on Small Business, jointly with the Subcommittee on Early Childhood Education, held an oversight hearing on October 29, 2026, examining child care providers as small businesses and considering Introduction 445, a bill from CM Stevens that would require the Department of Small Business Services to expand the Business Pathways program to include creative fields, retail, and cosmetology. The administration was represented by Emily List, Executive Director of the Mayor's Office of Child Care and Early Childhood Education; Harris Con, Chief of Staff at the Department of Small Business Services; and Shauna Castillo from NYCHA, which currently administers the Business Pathways program. The hearing was substantive and well-attended, touching on systemic financing failures, private equity encroachment, universal child care expansion logistics, and the specific mechanics of supporting home-based providers in NYCHA housing.

The Mamdani Administration positioned universal child care as a central policy priority, citing a day-eight commitment of $1.2 billion toward that goal jointly with the Governor. Emily List outlined a series of early operational reforms: a new child care permitting portal consolidating application requirements, a $43 million investment to overhaul contracting and payment processes at New York City Public Schools, a $3 million emergency micro-grant fund for home-based providers, a $40 million rate increase in the executive budget (5% for family child care, 2% for center-based providers), and the launch of a voucher waitlist drawdown beginning with 3,000 invitations, targeting 10,000 families by fiscal year end. The administration also described a nascent cooperative model being developed with home-based providers, partly as a structural counter to private equity expansion in the sector. Council members pressed repeatedly on whether these investments were sufficient given the scale of the problem, with CM Morano noting the IBO data showing average contract registration delays growing from 83 days in FY2021 to 247 days in FY2024, and questioning whether the city was effectively forcing small businesses to subsidize its own inefficiency.

Several recurring problems dominated questioning. Late and non-payment of contracted providers was the most urgent: multiple council members described individual providers going unpaid for a full year or more, with one member citing a phone call received the previous day from teachers unable to be paid. The administration acknowledged a historic disinvestment in early childhood contracting infrastructure under the prior administration, claiming staffing had been nearly halved. Council members were skeptical that the new $43 million investment and staffing hires would be sufficient, and the administration was unable to provide a specific figure for how much of that investment targets payment timelines or what measurable delay reduction is expected. Liability insurance was flagged as an acute and worsening crisis for providers, with the number of insurers willing to cover child care in New York City described as dwindling. The administration acknowledged it is exploring using government scale to address this but has no concrete solution yet. The potential displacement effects of the 2K expansion on existing small providers, particularly family child care operators, were also raised, with the administration noting it had learned from the pre-K rollout's unintended harms to family child care providers and is placing no 2K seats in public school buildings, working exclusively through community-based and home-based providers.

The Business Pathways program, administered by NYCHA since pandemic-era grant funding expired, was examined in detail by CM Hanif. The program has produced 365 food business graduates and 149 child care graduates since 2014 and 2015 respectively, with 57 child care businesses launched and 20 still operating as of this year. Each cohort costs $100,000 and enrolls 25 participants over 15 weeks. The program is hybrid since COVID, runs twice weekly in evenings, and includes a physical walkthrough of prospective participants' homes to assess viability. CM Hanif raised the particular difficulty facing NYCHA residents trying to run home-based child care when living conditions require constant repairs, an issue the program attempts to address through maintenance ticket guidance. Introduction 445's proposal to expand Business Pathways into cosmetology, creative fields, and retail was received cautiously by the administration, which said it had not yet conducted a cost analysis for new sectors and noted that resident surveys consistently identify food and child care as primary business interests. The hearing concluded with CM Thomas-Henry flagging two imminent provider closures: the African American Parents Educational Center and Christ the King Community Daycare and Preschool, the latter expected to generate significant public testimony.

Numbers

- Over 10,000 unique licensed child care programs operating in New York City: more than 2,200 licensed daycare centers, more than 1,500 licensed school-aged child care centers, close to 5,600 group family day cares, and around 740 family day cares.
- More than half of child care programs in New York City employ fewer than five people; only 3% employ more than 50.
- Close to half of all child care workers have their own children at home; over 5,000 child care workers have a child under five at home.
- Child care prices grew by 230% nationally from 1992 to 2022 according to a 2024 US Small Business Administration report.
- For-profit child care facilities operate on average profit margins of less than 1%, well below the 15% healthy margin seen across most industries.
- The Mamdani Administration and Governor committed over $1.2 billion to advance universal child care in New York City, announced on day eight of the administration.
- $43 million invested to transform New York City Public Schools Division of Early Childhood Education to address contracting and payment delays.
- $40 million invested in the executive budget for increased rates for contracted early childhood providers, including a 5% rate increase for family child care providers and a 2% increase for family child care networks and center-based providers.
- $3 million emergency micro-grant fund launched for licensed home-based child care providers facing urgent challenges.
- Approximately 30,000 families were on the child care voucher waitlist as of the hearing.
- 3,000 invitations sent to families on the voucher waitlist earlier in October 2026, with a target of moving 10,000 or more families off the waitlist by the end of the current fiscal year.
- Voucher waitlist priority criteria: families must be under 300% of the federal poverty line, unhoused, or have a child with a disability.
- Average child care contract registration delay grew from 83 days after contract start date in FY2021 to 247 days after contract start date in FY2024, per the Independent Budget Office.
- Contract financing loan fund available to city contractors at a capped 3% interest rate, administered in conjunction with the Economic Development Corporation.
- Interest-free bridge loans available to child care providers through a partnership between the Mayor's Office of Contract Services and the Fund for the City of New York.
- The Little Apple child care center at One Centre Street opened earlier in 2026 and is currently serving approximately 40 children of municipal employees, free of charge.
- Business Pathways program has enrolled 448 residents across 12 food business cohorts and 189 residents across 9 child care cohorts since 2014 and 2015 respectively.
- 57 child care businesses launched through Business Pathways since the program began; 20 currently operating as of 2026.
- Each Business Pathways cohort costs $100,000 as a baseline; child care cohorts run 15 weeks, food cohorts 10 weeks, each meeting twice weekly for approximately 3 hours per session.
- Current 2026 Business Pathways child care cohort enrolling 25 NYCHA residents; food cohorts typically enroll 30.
- Approximately 4,000 businesses in NYCHA ZIP codes served by the Department of Small Business Services in the last fiscal year.
- SBS Workforce1 centers have made approximately 50 placements in the child care sector.
- At least 35 child care providers have used the Commercial Lease Assistance Program since its launch around the pandemic.
- Standard contracted classroom ratios: 18 children and 2 teachers for pre-K; 15 children and 2 teachers for 3K; 12 children and 2 teachers for 2K.
- State market rate for child care voucher reimbursement is scheduled to be updated in 2027, based on a survey currently open to providers.
