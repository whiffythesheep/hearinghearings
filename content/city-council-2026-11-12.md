Summary

Meeting Overview

The November 12, 2026 Stated Meeting was dominated by two themes: a veterans legislative package timed to the day after Veterans Day, and responses to an acute building safety crisis following a fatal collapse in Flatbush the previous evening. Speaker Menin opened with a moment of silence for the eight people killed in the Flatbush collapse and noted twenty-three injuries including three firefighters, before moving through a full agenda that passed several significant pieces of legislation and introduced a substantial slate of new bills.

The marquee legislative action on veterans was Int 1051-A, sponsored by CM Frank Morano (R-Staten Island), which eliminates property taxes entirely on the primary residences of permanently and totally disabled veterans with a 100% service-related disability rating. The bill passed 48-1. Morano used his floor remarks to note pointedly that New Jersey and Nassau County already provide this benefit, an implicit rebuke to Albany and prior City Councils. Minority Leader David Carr's Int 0928-A establishing an LGBTQ+ liaison within the Department of Veterans Services passed as part of the same package, with Carr explaining that many veterans discharged under Don't Ask Don't Tell remain unaware they can now seek an upgrade to their discharge status. CM Inna Vernikov's Int 0488-A requiring HPD to report on veteran uptake of Mitchell-Lama housing preferences also passed, framed as a transparency measure to ensure an underutilized benefit actually reaches veterans. Resolution 560, calling on Congress to pass the Fair Access to Co-ops for Veterans Act, passed on a voice vote.

Speaker Menin's Int 0891, the surveillance pricing prohibition, passed 44-5, making New York City the first jurisdiction in the country to ban the practice of using individual consumer data including location, browsing history, and demographic information to set differentiated prices for the same goods or services. Menin cited a 23% price differential found on a major grocery delivery platform and a $100 item that became more expensive when a retailer detected a shopper's location near their store. The bill fines violators up to $1,000 per violation, with a corrective action safe harbor for small businesses. The five dissenting votes were notably from both ends of the ideological spectrum, suggesting different objections. Int 0178-A, requiring HPD and DOB to provide centralized technical assistance for commercial-to-residential conversions including a designated interagency liaison, passed alongside the finance resolutions setting the city's property tax rates and class proportions for fiscal year 2027. Int 0202-A, requiring DOT to install approved school safety treatments within 120 or 180 days depending on complexity, also passed.

The building collapse and a broader spike in fire deaths animated several new bill introductions. CM Pierina Sanchez introduced Int 1174 to create a public fire emergency data dashboard modeled on NYPD CompStat, and Int 1175 to require a study of electrical systems in aging residential buildings, citing a near-doubling of fire deaths year-over-year and noting over 80% of city buildings are more than 50 years old. CM Hankerson introduced Int 1182 to establish a standalone Department of Emergency Medical Services, an idea that resurfaces periodically given long-standing pay and staffing disparities between FDNY firefighters and EMTs. The Council also received CM Louis's introduction of Int 1165, requiring an analysis and action plan on more than 50,000 vacant rent-stabilized units, which Menin highlighted in her remarks as unconscionable waste during a housing crisis with a 1.4% vacancy rate. Land use approvals included the Studio 54 rehabilitation text amendment enabling flexible transfer of development rights to preserve the theater, and two Queens Boulevard and 108-05 68th Road rezonings in Community District 6 incorporating Mandatory Inclusionary Housing requirements.

General discussion also featured CM Cabán introducing a resolution endorsing the Starbucks Workers United boycott, framing it in explicitly class-political terms and referencing Trump and billionaires in the same breath as union-busting, which is unlikely to advance beyond the Council chamber but signals the progressive bloc's posture. CM Brewer introduced a resolution opposing any renaming of Penn Station, noting President Trump had conditioned release of $16 billion in Gateway Tunnel federal funding on renaming the station after himself, which Brewer dismissed tersely as unnecessary confusion and expense without improving service. CM Morano introduced Int 1166 to deny building permits to developers owing $25,000 or more in unpaid property taxes or water and sewer bills, closing a gap in existing law that only counts certain fines toward that threshold. CM Narcisse flagged the Flatbush collapse as personally affecting constituents she has long represented. The meeting closed with the adoption of a resolution designating the fourth week of November as Shangri-Las Week, which is exactly what it sounds like.
