Summary

Meeting Overview

The August 13, 2026 Stated Meeting of the New York City Council was a substantive legislative session passing several notable packages of bills, with Speaker Menin's remarks setting the tone on immigration, insurance affordability, public health and consumer protection. The session opened with the Speaker announcing that Council employee Rafael Rubio had been granted permanent asylum after wrongful ICE detention and two weeks in solitary confinement, and she used that occasion to draw a broader contrast with New Yorkers who are not getting the same outcomes, linking it to the Council's $82 million budget allocation for immigration legal services.

The most consequential legislation passed was a package of four bills (Int 0655-A, Int 0894-A, Int 0917-A, Int 0918-A) reforming the outdoor dining program. CM Restler made the central argument: the Adams-era 2023 reforms caused a 90% decline in restaurant participation because costs were prohibitive. The new legislation removes seasonal restrictions on roadway cafes, allows sidewalk cafe winterization enclosures from November 30 to March 31, requires cleanliness standards with license revocation after three violations in two years, and allows quarterly rather than lump-sum payment of consent fees. The latter bill, Int 0918-A, was CM Thomas-Henry's first to pass the body. CM Carr (Minority Leader) voted against the roadway cafe expansion, arguing that without a member call-up option the only real decision-maker remains DOT, whose community engagement he characterized as exhibiting "established elitism." The bills passed with solid but not unanimous majorities, with between 37 and 42 votes in favor depending on the specific bill.

The Scoop Act package (Int 0857-A, Int 0864-A, Int 0872-A, Int 0880-A) passed to require DOSP to install dog waste bag dispensers on at least 1,200 public litter baskets, establish a Parks composting pilot for dog run waste, mandate a multilingual public education campaign, and require signage with penalty information at park entrances. Int 0685-B established a new Office of Insurance Affordability within the Charter, responding to what the Speaker characterized as homeowners insurance rising 26% annually and small business liability insurance running 52% higher than other states. Three resolutions (Res 0387, Res 0389, Res 0395) called on Albany to cap homeowners insurance increases at 25% per year, improve auto insurance consumer information, and address property insurance costs for affordable housing providers. The Council also passed Int 0839-A making the Certification of No Harassment Pilot Program permanent, a significant tenant protection measure, and Int 0935-A requiring quarterly NYPD reporting on confirmed firearm discharges including incidents where nobody was struck.

In land use, the Council approved the Queens Community District 2 Walk to Park site selection covering dozens of privately owned properties for potential Parks acquisition, and approved the Flatiron NoMad Major Concessions expansion with CMs Wilson and Epstein speaking to its sustainability commitments including public restrooms and compost bins. Several rezonings in Brooklyn and Queens with Mandatory Inclusionary Housing components were approved with modifications and referred back to the City Planning Commission. A notable administrative item was the City Clerk's certification that a petition seeking to amend the Charter to require nonpartisan ranked-choice voting elections was ineligible for submission to the Board of Elections, which passed without debate.

General discussion surfaced several emerging issues. CM Maloney introduced a Legionnaires' disease legislative package following an outbreak that killed seven people in her and the Speaker's districts, with five residents still hospitalized at the time of the meeting; the Speaker announced a hearing for September 2. CM Feliz (Oswald) gave an impassioned plea about violence and open-air drug dealing in his district, citing a 15-year-old killed the previous night and an alleged arson on 183rd Street conducted in full view of news cameras and police. CM Epstein reintroduced Int 1008 to protect Medicare-eligible municipal retirees from being forced into privatized Medicare Advantage plans. CM Banks introduced Int 0991 to require a catalog of standard pre-approved designs for small capital projects, a straightforward efficiency reform. CM Brewer introduced a pedicab reform package (Int 0992, Int 0993, Int 0994) to tighten regulation, move oversight from the Department of Consumer and Worker Protection to the TLC, and require insurance, citing an estimated 1,500 pedicabs operating with inadequate credentials. The meeting closed with condolences to CM Farah Louis on the death of her father.
