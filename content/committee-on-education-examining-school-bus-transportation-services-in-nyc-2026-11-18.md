Summary

Meeting Overview

The City Council's Education Committee held an oversight hearing on November 18, 2026 examining New York City's school bus transportation system, with a particular focus on service failures affecting students with disabilities, students in temporary housing, and students in foster care. The committee also heard testimony on two pieces of legislation: Intro 835, sponsored by CM Lincoln Restler and co-prime sponsored by Chair Dinowitz, which would require the DOE to publish monthly school bus performance data on a public dashboard; and Resolution 735, sponsored by CM Julie Won and CM Restler, calling on Albany to pass legislation protecting school bus workers' wages and benefits during contract transitions and prioritizing experienced drivers.

Deputy Chancellor for School Operations Kevin Moran led a DOE panel that defended the department's record while acknowledging significant failures. The DOE manages roughly 152,000 students on yellow buses daily, with 72,000 of those being students with disabilities transported curb-to-school. Moran cited progress including growth in the NYC School Bus app from 1,800 to 10,200 daily users, the launch of vendor report cards, and ridership tracking pilots covering around 818 routes. He acknowledged that at the start of the current school year, roughly 2,000 students lacked assigned routes, and noted that 467 students still lacked routes at time of hearing. He stated that pupil transportation demand has grown 31 percent over four years, requiring 928 additional routes this year alone, even as overall student enrollment declined.

Council members were persistent and at times scathing about the DOE's failure to implement the Via Transportation contract, awarded in 2019 for $51.7 million, of which over $35 million has already been paid. Route optimization, one of the four contracted deliverables, has still not been implemented in 2026, and the DOE continues to rely on routing software from the 1990s, specifically AJ Log and FoxPro. Members challenged the DOE on its failure during the pilot period to issue liquidated damages for drivers not logging into the tracking system, with only $270,000 in fines issued across all vendors this year for a violation category that could have generated $44.3 million in penalties in 2024-25. The DOE confirmed there is no sliding scale for repeat offenders, and that only one bus company is currently on a corrective action plan, with the DOE initially refusing to name it. Members also questioned the 93 percent driver login rate, noting the opt-in model is fundamentally poor program design for a public accountability system.

Structural problems received sustained attention. Members raised incidents including six nonverbal students left on a bus for more than four hours without notification, and a constituent's child offered a pickup time an hour before school ends. The hearing exposed that there is no consistent, defined role for school transportation coordinators at individual schools; that parent complaint pathways are fragmented between schools, bus companies, and OPT's call center; that 350 students with mandated bus paraprofessionals are still awaiting that service; that 17 students with nurse recommendations on their IEPs are still unassigned; and that inter-borough routes cover over 10,000 students across 2,699 routes. The upcoming expiration of the bus contracts on June 30, 2028, with an estimated 18-month procurement timeline, dominated discussion of the structural future of the system, with members noting that the same contracts have been in place since 1979. The DOE was cautious about the timeline but said there was reason for optimism if Albany passes the employee protection legislation covered by Resolution 735.

Numbers

- Approximately 152,000 students receive yellow school bus transportation daily in New York City.
- 72,000 of those students are students with disabilities transported curb-to-school.
- 10,000 students are pre-K and early intervention students transported directly home.
- Over 500,000 students use OMNY transit cards to access public transportation independently.
- Pupil transportation is the third-largest DOE contract category at $2.28 billion in Fiscal Year 2026 spending, up $634 million from Fiscal Year 2021.
- The Via Transportation contract has a total value of $51.7 million, with over $35 million already paid as of the hearing.
- In the 2024-25 school year, the DOE could have assessed $44.3 million in penalties for driver tracking non-compliance but assessed only a fraction of that.
- $270,000 in liquidated damages has been issued to all vendors so far this current year for tracking violations, at $75 per violation.
- Only one bus company is currently under a corrective action plan.
- Curb-to-school routes cost an average of $239,000 per route in Fiscal Year 2027; stop-to-school routes cost an average of $176,253.
- In Fiscal Year 2024, the DOE spent $21,719 per student on curb-to-school service and approximately $4,900-$6,761 per student on stop-to-school service.
- The DOE had projected $27.9 million in route efficiency savings for Fiscal Year 2027 and $55.9 million for Fiscal Year 2028; Moran acknowledged these savings are unlikely to materialise due to 928 new routes added this year.
- Demand for curb-to-school transportation has grown 31 percent over four years, from 54,890 to 71,915 students; required routes have grown 17 percent, from 5,771 to 6,770.
- In 2024-25, there were nearly 90,000 delayed routes, approximately 500 per school day; 56,000 students were late by over an hour.
- On a typical school day cited in the hearing, 211 buses were delayed, with 434 students on board, over half delayed by more than 30 minutes.
- The NYC School Bus app has grown from 1,824 daily users to over 10,200 this academic year, out of a total of 150,000 students on yellow buses.
- Ridership boarding and exit tracking currently covers approximately 818 of 9,000 routes, with full rollout expected by spring 2027.
- 2,699 inter-borough routes serve over 10,000 students.
- 467 students lacked an assigned bus route at the time of the hearing, down from roughly 2,000 at the start of the school year.
- 350 students with mandated bus paraprofessionals are still awaiting that service; 17 students with nurse recommendations on their IEPs are unassigned.
- OPT currently has 150 staff with 10 vacancies, down from an estimated 150-200 pre-COVID.
- The OPT call center handles approximately 400-500 complaints per day.
- Approximately 200 students are on the waiting list for travel training; the department is adding five travel trainers.
- 114 electric school buses are currently on the road; 533 more are in the pipeline through Biden infrastructure grants.

Action Points

- DOE to provide the committee with the full routing data previously requested, including a response to the outstanding clarifying email sent by the committee.
- DOE to provide data on how many students are on bus routes exceeding 90 minutes.
- DOE to provide a breakdown of liquidated damages issued by vendor for Via tracking violations.
- DOE to investigate and report back on the specific constituent case raised by Chair Dinowitz involving a pickup scheduled an hour before school ends.
- DOE to follow up on how many students have received makeup instruction or related services for time lost to bus failures in 2026-27, and how students still owed services are being identified.
- DOE (Moran) to develop automated geofencing and time-bound alerts for bus routes so delays and deviations trigger notification without relying on driver or company reporting.
- DOE to investigate whether the NYC School Bus app can display a history of lateness accessible to parents, rather than only within a one-hour window.
- DOE to investigate and fix the app's failure to correctly handle half-day schedules and the resulting driver login errors.
- DOE to boost outreach efforts, including multilingual communications, to increase family adoption of the NYC School Bus app.
- DOE to report to the committee on how many calls to the OPT call center in the last school year were made in languages other than English.
- DOE to implement a unified call center so parents have a single point of contact for transportation complaints.
- DOE to work with school leadership to publish the name and contact information of transportation coordinators on each school's website, resolving union and role-definition issues first, and to report back on a timeline.
- DOE (Moran) to come back with a concrete proposal for what a dedicated transportation coordinator role should look like at schools with large bus operations, including whether a threshold number of buses should trigger a funded dedicated position.
- DOE to provide the committee with an update on the new procurement process for bus contracts before June 30, 2028 expiration, including whether employee protection legislation from Albany changes the timeline.
- DOE to provide data on the number and on-time performance of inter-borough routes.
- DOE to report back on how many paraprofessionals have been recruited through the new contracted agency process and provide a timeline for placing all 350-plus unassigned students.
- DOE to report back on the number of bus company corporal punishment allegations substantiated from the 340 investigations in 2024-25, with a tracker shared with the committee.
- DOE to disclose, after legal review, which bus company is currently on a corrective action plan.
- DOE to come back with a proposal for maintaining dual bus and OMNY eligibility for students during travel training, including a timeline for implementation.
- DOE to update the student transportation preference form to include a fuller range of options including travel training, in coordination with the Office for Students with Disabilities.
- DOE to report on collaboration with Future Ready and District 79 on recruiting students and young adults into school bus driver and paraprofessional roles, and confirm advocacy for Future Ready funding in the upcoming preliminary budget.
- DOE to confirm in the preliminary budget whether travel training positions will be proposed for expansion rather than cut again.
