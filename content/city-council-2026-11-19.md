Summary

Meeting Overview

The November 19, 2026 stated meeting of the New York City Council was a relatively busy end-of-session session, with a cluster of bills passed, several new pieces of legislation introduced, and a fair amount of members using general discussion time to flag upcoming priorities. The meeting covered ground ranging from housing safety in the wake of a fatal Flatbush building collapse, to reproductive health, to e-bike battery fires, to — in what may be a first for a major American legislature — the boiling of live lobsters.

The most substantively significant votes were a package of reproductive health measures. The Council passed Intro 200-A requiring the Department of Health to develop public-facing materials about doula access and establish a doula advisory council, and Intro 211-A requiring DOHMH to conduct a multilingual public education campaign on fertility treatment options and insurance coverage. Companion resolutions called on Albany and the Governor to create an IVF tax credit (Res 108), mandate insurance coverage of preimplantation genetic testing (Res 447), require SUNY and CUNY campuses to stock emergency contraception vending machines (Res 89-A), and require insurers to cover lactation services without restrictions (Res 637). Taken together, these represent a deliberate Council positioning of New York City as a reproductive health sanctuary in the post-Roe environment, though the practical reach of most of these resolutions depends entirely on Albany acting, which it may or may not do.

On housing and buildings, the Council passed two notable measures in the wake of the Flatbush collapse, which killed eight people and injured nineteen. Intro 1198, introduced by CM Thomas-Henry and supported vocally by CM Louis whose district was affected, would require the Department of Buildings to notify local council members and borough presidents within 24 hours of issuing a Class 1 or Class 2 stop work order and to provide weekly reports on outstanding violations. Separately, Intro 1023-A, the electronic dwelling registration bill championed by CM Banks, passed with broad support. As Banks pointed out with evident frustration, the existing system nominally allows online registration but requires a printed, physically mailed form and a separate system to pay the fee, which is precisely the kind of legacy-system absurdity that makes city government look foolish. The bill fixes that. The Council also approved a package of Finance items including Article 11 property tax exemptions for affordable housing projects in the Bronx and Manhattan, and a transparency resolution on expense budget designations.

Three bills on governmental operations (Intros 1127-C, 1128-A, 1129-A), sponsored by Deputy Speaker Nantasha Williams, cleaned up the Charter and Administrative Code by repealing obsolete reporting requirements, establishing a default due-date system for agency reports to the Council, and setting a standard procedure for how agencies transmit reports and operational attestations. This is unglamorous but genuinely useful administrative housekeeping of the kind that tends to get ignored until someone actually tries to hold an agency accountable and finds the statutory framework is a mess.

In general discussion, several significant legislative signals were given. CM Cabán called out Amazon by name for what she described as corporate extortion over the pending Delivery Protection Act, following a pop-up notification to New York City users threatening a $5 per-delivery surcharge if the bill passed. Speaker Menin's office had already released a flavored e-cigarette enforcement report, and a hearing on stronger closure powers for repeat violators was flagged for the following day. CM Ossé used the disbanding of the NYPD Strategic Response Group by Mayor Mamdani to push for passage of the CURB Act, which would codify the disbandment in law and add protest-response reporting requirements. CM Paladino introduced the lobster boiling bill (Intro 1188), which she framed earnestly as an animal welfare matter; whether it goes anywhere is another question. The meeting closed with CM Mealy offering a brief and moving tribute to her late mother, which was a reminder that even a busy legislative agenda involves human beings.

Numbers

- Flatbush building collapse: 8 people killed, approximately 19 others critically injured.
- DOE pupil transportation budget for Fiscal Year 2027: $2.28 billion, described as DOE's third largest contract category.
- DOHMH flavored e-cigarette investigation covered 193 businesses located within 500 feet of a public middle or high school across the five boroughs.
- 40% of shops surveyed carrying e-cigarettes offered products with a prohibited flavor, which has been illegal to sell since 2019.
- Approximately one-third of schools surveyed had shops with prohibited advertising within 500 feet.
- MN-2 budget action: appropriation of new City revenues in Fiscal Year 2027 totaling $1.153 billion.
- Fordham Bedford Cluster Phase 3 Article 11 tax exemption: 161 affordable housing units across four properties in the Bronx.
- Fordham Bedford units restricted to residents earning 60% of area median income or less.
- Battery-related fires citywide: 406 fires in the most recent year counted, up from 241 the prior year, an increase of approximately 68%.
- CM Yang's proposed bill would lower the threshold for sealing an e-bike retailer from three violations in three years to two violations within two years of a first violation.
- Res 89-A would require at least one emergency contraception vending machine at each SUNY and CUNY campus location.
- Res 108 would create a refundable tax credit covering 75% of eligible IVF expenses for up to three cycles not covered by insurance.
- 110 East 138th Street/63 Exterior Street Bronx development: two mixed-use buildings proposed in the Special Harlem River Waterfront District.
- 68 Road Rezoning (Queens, CM Schulman's district): approximately 33 residential units, 8 of which permanently affordable, plus community facility space.
- 300-12 Queens Boulevard rezoning (Queens, CM Schulman's district): approximately 389 residential units, 97 permanently affordable, plus ground-floor commercial space in a 21-story mixed-use building.
- 35% of patients accessing abortion services in New York City came from outside the state, according to the Abortion Access Hub, cited in support of Intro 1185.
- New York City is home to approximately 200,000 small businesses employing nearly 1 million New Yorkers, per Speaker Menin.
- Over 40,000 Haitians reside in New York City, cited in support of Res 626 on Temporary Protected Status.
- Estimated 21 billion gallons of polluted water and raw sewage enter New York City waterways annually, cited by CM Restler in support of Intro 1196.
- Approximately 60% of New York City sewer infrastructure is combined wastewater and stormwater infrastructure.
- Puerto Rico's public university system cut by 50% under the Financial Oversight and Management Board, cited by CM Gutiérrez.
- Amazon described as a $3 trillion company by CM Cabán in context of the Delivery Protection Act dispute.
- Delivery Protection Act licensing fee: $500 every other year per delivery company.
- General Orders vote totals: most items passed 48-0; Intro 200-A passed 44-0 with 6 abstentions; Planning Commission appointments (M94, M96, M97 and accompanying resolutions) passed 42-5 with 1 abstention; Board of Correction appointment (M100 and Res 750) passed 41-6 with 1 abstention.

Action Points

- DOHMH to develop multilingual public-facing materials on doula rights and hospital access under Intro 200-A, including a feedback form for doulas and a new doula advisory council.
- DOHMH to launch a multilingual citywide public education campaign on fertility treatment options, insurance coverage, Medicaid benefits, and financial assistance under Intro 211-A.
- Department of Buildings to notify local council members and borough presidents within 24 hours of issuing Class 1 or Class 2 stop work orders under Intro 1198, and to provide weekly reports on outstanding violations and the reasons for lifting orders.
- Department of Education to distribute materials to students and parents on the importance of dental care and fluoride under Intro 259-A.
- HPD to implement electronic dwelling registration and electronic fee payment in a single online system under Intro 1023-A.
- Department of Buildings to notify property owners within 7 days of permit issuance of sign-off requirements, and to send follow-up notifications for permits expiring without sign-off, under Intro 717-A.
- City agencies to comply with updated report transmission procedures and default due-date system established under Intros 1127-C, 1128-A, and 1129-A.
- Council Committee on Fire and Emergency Management to hold an emergency hearing on battery storage and fire safety the following day, as flagged by CM Yang.
- Council Committee on Consumer and Worker Protection to hear CM Narcisse's bill giving DCWP authority to close businesses with three violations for selling e-cigarettes, as flagged by Speaker Menin.
- Council to hear the CURB Act, which would codify the disbandment of the NYPD Strategic Response Group and add protest-response reporting requirements, as requested by CM Ossé.
- City Chief Procurement Officer to approve any changes to nonprofit contractor filing requirements or requests for additional records under Intro 1191.
- City to reimburse nonprofit contractors for reasonable interest and lender fees caused by city-controlled registration delays under Intro 1192.
- Large new construction buildings to be required to install on-site non-potable water reuse systems under Intro 1196, referred to Environmental Protection and Waterfronts committee.
- Speaker and Mayor's Office to designate appointees to a new collective bargaining assessment committee to recommend wage increases for the lowest-paid city workers under Intro 1195.
- Bike share operators such as Citi Bike to verify user age via government- or school-issued ID under Intro 1200, referred to Transportation and Infrastructure committee.
- Council to request Albany pass and Governor sign legislation on IVF tax credits (Res 108), preimplantation genetic testing insurance coverage (Res 447), emergency contraception vending machines at SUNY and CUNY (Res 89-A), and lactation service insurance coverage (Res 637).
- Council to call on US Senate and President to designate Haiti for Temporary Protected Status under Res 626.
- Council to call on US Congress to set a date certain for ending the Puerto Rico Financial Oversight and Management Board under Res 28.
- Three new Planning Commission members (Anthony Crowell, Kenneth Knuckles, Margaret Anadu, Meenakshi Srinivasan) confirmed; Martha King confirmed to Board of Correction.
- TLC commissioner appointments for Ligia Guallpa, Felicia Singh, and Lorelei Salas referred to Rules, Privileges, Elections, Standards and Ethics committee for advice and consent review.
