Summary

Meeting Overview

The October 8, 2026 Stated Meeting of the New York City Council was dominated by two substantive themes: a sweeping package of AI regulation bills introduced in the wake of a Committee of the Whole hearing earlier that week, and a busy slate of land use approvals advancing the City's housing agenda across multiple boroughs. The meeting also passed legislation on towed vehicle tracking, small business construction support, gifted and talented program access, and several notable introductions on consumer protection, domestic violence reporting, food safety, and women's health.

Speaker Menin used her floor remarks to frame the AI legislation as a necessary response to federal inaction, drawing the analogy to pharmaceutical, aviation, and financial regulation: just as those industries are not permitted to self-regulate, neither should frontier AI companies be. She flagged testimony from whistleblower Jacob Coxon at Monday's hearing, who stated it was "more likely than not" that humanity could lose control of AI leading to extinction — and noted that the companies themselves could not identify who would be responsible for responding to an AI catastrophe, whether they had insurance for such an event, or whether internal safety concerns were being properly heard. The package introduced today includes Int 1100 (third-party validation and shutdown capability), Int 1101 (civilian enforcement of AI violations), Int 1109 (reporting of AI safety incidents on city contracts), Int 1116 (private cause of action for third-party AI misuse), Int 1118 (chatbot data privacy and transparency), Int 1119 (AI emergency response plans), Int 1123 (whistleblower protections for AI-related reporting), and Int 1130 (prohibiting deceptive AI advertising). CM Santosuosso spoke to Int 1130 specifically, noting that AI companies at the hearing could not clearly assess the risk of a cataclysmic event caused by their own technology.

On land use, the Council approved or advanced modifications to a substantial number of rezonings adding thousands of housing units across Brooklyn, Queens, and the Bronx, nearly all with Mandatory Inclusionary Housing requirements attached. Highlights include the Sojourner Truth-Mapes Rezoning in the Bronx delivering 457 units of 100 percent affordable housing; the Park Avenue Brooklyn Rezoning covering 10 properties with 391 units and 2,500 new yeshiva school seats; the 289 Kent Avenue Rezoning converting the former ICE headquarters in Williamsburg into 278 units; and Fort Hamilton Mews in Bay Ridge with 192 units including up to 88 permanently affordable. The Council also pulled the Studio 54 rehabilitation application for Council review, which would facilitate a floor area bonus for the Roundabout Theatre Company's renovation of the historic Manhattan venue. Two tax exemptions for affordable housing projects in the Bronx and Brooklyn were also approved.

Among the non-AI legislation passed or introduced, Int 0489-2026-A creating a centralized online towed vehicle database passed 46 to 0 with one abstention (CM Ossé abstaining without explanation). Int 0799-2026-A establishes a support fund — loans, grants, or services — for small retailers harmed by City roadway construction. CM Louis introduced Int 1113 and Int 1114 on uterine fibroid provider training and cervical and ovarian cancer reporting respectively, framing them explicitly in terms of racial health disparities: over 80 percent of Black women develop fibroids by age 50, at nearly three times the incidence rate of other groups. CM Epstein introduced the POWER Act (Int 1105), which would create a private right of action for unfair or deceptive business practices, a protection that exists in 43 states and the District of Columbia but not yet New York. CM Morano introduced Int 1117 requiring community impact statements before transient hotel permits are issued, prompted by a proposed 160-bed homeless shelter filed as a transient hotel in his district. CM Joseph introduced Int 1111 to require quarterly DOE reporting on Medicaid reimbursement for special education services, arguing uncollected federal and state dollars were being left on the table at a moment when hundreds of thousands of New Yorkers face Medicaid eligibility risk.

Action Points

- Department of Finance to create and operate a centralized online database and notification system for towed vehicles, replacing the existing precinct-notification system, per Int 0489-2026-A now passed.
- Department of Small Business Services to establish a support fund providing loans, grants, services or materials to small retail businesses affected by City roadway construction, per Int 0799-2026-A now passed.
- Mayor's Office of Child Care and Early Childhood Education to study how language access needs and geographic location affect access to early elementary gifted and talented programs, per Int 0684-2026-A now passed.
- City Planning Commission to review modifications to the Fort Hamilton Mews Rezoning, Park Avenue Brooklyn Rezoning, 69-67 108th Street Rezoning, and 289 Kent Avenue Rezoning applications as referred back by the Council today.
- Council's Committee on Zoning and Franchises to take up preconsidered land use applications including the 108-05 68th Road Rezoning, 100-12 Queens Boulevard Rezoning, South Richmond Designated Open Space Amendment, and both Studio 54 applications.
- Committee on Finance to take up preconsidered tax exemption applications for Fordham Bedford Cluster Phase 1 in the Bronx and Gowanus Green A in Brooklyn.
- Council to hold additional committee hearings on AI legislation beyond the Committee of the Whole, with Speaker Menin indicating multiple committees will take up different aspects of the AI package.
- Department of Health and Mental Hygiene to develop mandatory training for health care providers on uterine fibroid diagnosis and treatment, per Int 1113-2026 introduced for referral.
- Department of Health and Mental Hygiene to report annually on cervical and ovarian cancer screenings and diagnoses citywide, per Int 1114-2026 introduced for referral.
- Department of Education to report quarterly by borough and district on special education Medicaid-reimbursable services delivered, claimed, reimbursed, and where gaps exist, per Int 1111-2026 introduced for referral.
- NYPD to expand annual domestic violence reporting to include total arrests and detailed demographic and geographic breakdowns of domestic incident reports, per Int 1106-2026 introduced for referral.
- Department of Probation to provide written notice before intake interviews for youth accused of crimes, explaining the process and identifying legal representation organizations, per Int 1104-2026 introduced for referral.
- DCAS to assess existing employee resource groups at City agencies, identify best practices and issue guidance, per Int 1122-2026 introduced for referral.
- NYC Department of Transportation or relevant agency to install sound meters near seaplane bases and publicly report noise levels, per Int 1115-2026 introduced for referral.
- Developers seeking permits for new or converted transient hotels to prepare and publicly file community impact statements before permits are issued, per Int 1117-2026 introduced for referral.
- Food service managers to renew food protection course certification every five years under Int 1124-2026 introduced for referral, addressing a gap where New York does not currently require renewal.
- New York State Legislature and Governor to be called upon to pass legislation extending commencement and completion deadlines for the Affordable Housing from Commercial Conversions tax incentive program, per Res 0663-2026 introduced for referral.
- Governor to be called upon to sign S.6487C/A.7033D empowering the New York State Council on the Arts to designate Little Yemen in the Bronx as a cultural district, per Res 0458-2026-A adopted today.
