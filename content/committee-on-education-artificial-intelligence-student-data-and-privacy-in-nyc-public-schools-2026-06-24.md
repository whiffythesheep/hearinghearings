Summary

Meeting Overview

The Education and Technology Committees held a joint hearing on June 24, 2026 covering artificial intelligence, student data and privacy in NYC Public Schools, along with three additional bills on school admissions transparency and equity of access to gifted and talented and special education programs. The AI oversight session dominated proceedings and ran for several hours, drawing testimony from DOE officials, students, parents, educators, academics and advocacy organizations. The hearing exposed substantial gaps between the DOE's stated policies and what is actually happening in schools, and produced a sustained and at times pointed exchange between Council members and the DOE's First Deputy Chancellor Danielle Gente, along with her team.

The DOE's central position was that it published preliminary AI guidance in March 2026, ran a 45-day public feedback period that generated 6,491 responses, and is now working on a more detailed policy framework for the coming school year. Officials maintained that the IRMA review process provides a privacy and security floor for approved tools, that student-facing generative AI is largely restricted, and that tools like Google Gemini are only available in around 35 high schools where principals have specifically requested it. Council members pushed back hard on all of this. Chair Dinowitz noted that approved vendors include tools with embedded AI capabilities that students can and apparently do access, that principals have described feeling like they are "flying the plane as they are building it," and that the DOE's own website incorrectly claimed that its legal team reviews tools for algorithmic bias when it does not. The DOE acknowledged the website error and confirmed that the IRMA process does not yet evaluate for algorithmic bias, equity impact or instructional effectiveness, and that the capacity to do so is still being developed. The DOE could not commit to adding bias review as a procurement requirement. Several Council members, including both chairs, have signed a letter calling for a two-year moratorium on student-facing AI, though the hearing did not produce a vote or formal decision on that question.

The May 2026 State Comptroller audit featured heavily. It found that NYC Public Schools lacks a formal written data classification policy, does not maintain a comprehensive list of all applications used by each school, and that of 141 data breach incidents reviewed, 67 were reported late. The DOE's Chief Privacy Officer acknowledged the findings, said the department disagrees with some of them but has accepted the recommendations, and described incremental improvements including updating Chancellor's Regulation A-820 in fall 2025. He confirmed that annual privacy training is required of all 160,000 employees but that only around 138,000 completed it in the most recent year, and that the DOE cannot verify which incomplete cases involved employees with actual access to personally identifiable information. Council members found this deeply unsatisfying, particularly given that the Illuminate Education breach from around 2022 resulted in 380,000 families not being notified until 2024. The DOE confirmed it no longer uses Illuminate, and said it is now exploring adding financial penalties to data processing agreements with vendors who fail to report breaches promptly.

On the bills before the committee: the DOE expressed general support for the intent of the two preconsidered bills sponsored by Chair Dinowitz requiring annual reporting on approved algorithmic tools (T2026-2129) and quarterly reporting on data breaches involving those tools (T2026-2130), while noting overlap with existing Local Law 35 obligations and requesting coordination on implementation. On Int 0412-2026 requiring disclosure of school admissions policies, the DOE argued most of the information is already publicly available via the MySchools directory and InfoHub. On CM Zhuang's two equity bills, Int 0684-2026 and Int 0853-2026 requiring studies on how language access, income and geography affect access to gifted and talented and special education programs respectively, the DOE did not appear with officials prepared to address the substantive data questions CM Wong raised, drawing a direct rebuke from the chair and CM Wong, who noted that families from Black, Brown and immigrant communities are being systematically underserved and that nobody seemed to know whose responsibility it was to show up and answer for it.

Public testimony was overwhelmingly skeptical of the DOE's handling of AI in schools. Student witnesses from the NYCLU Teen Activist Project spoke compellingly about cognitive offloading, the hollowness of AI-generated work and the chilling effect of AI grading on their confidence. A researcher from the Collaborative Research Center for Resilience flagged that most ed tech vendors are legally designated as school officials under FERPA, allowing data sharing without parental consent, a point the DOE did not foreground in its own testimony. The Parent Coalition for Student Privacy alleged that Google Gemini is installed on thousands of elementary school Chromebooks contrary to the DOE's characterization. TechNYC supported the intent of the two transparency bills while suggesting the definition of algorithmic tool be narrowed and the October 31 quarterly reporting deadline be reviewed. A professor from Fordham University and representatives from MOUSE and Urban Assembly argued that thoughtful, teacher-guided AI literacy education has genuine value and that a blanket moratorium would leave students less prepared. The overall weight of public testimony, however, supported a moratorium, with multiple speakers arguing that the DOE has allowed vendors to run ahead of policy, that parents are not meaningfully informed about what tools their children use, and that data privacy protections are inadequate.

Numbers

- 6,491 survey responses were received during the 45-day public feedback period on the DOE's preliminary AI guidance, representing less than 1% of parents in the system.
- Approximately 35 high schools have requested access to Google Gemini for students as of spring 2026, according to DOE testimony.
- NYC Public Schools experienced 141 data breach incidents between January 2023 and February 2025, according to the State Comptroller.
- Of 141 breaches reviewed by the Comptroller, 67 (nearly half) were reported late.
- 380,000 families were not notified of the Illuminate Education breach until 2024, more than two years after the incident and seven months after the DOE was informed of additional students affected.
- 238,000 staff members completed privacy and security training in 2025-26, against a total workforce of approximately 160,000 (the discrepancy likely reflects counting methodology; the DOE stated approximately 138,000 completed training in the most recent year).
- 138,000 employees completed the annual data privacy training in the most recent school year, out of approximately 160,000 total employees, leaving an estimated 22,000 non-compliant.
- Training completion was 73% in 2024, the figure cited in the Comptroller's audit.
- 117,000 staff members completed privacy training in 2023-24.
- 8,300 staff members have completed the DOE's centrally developed AI training course to date.
- 50 teachers are currently participating in the DOE's beta testing of AI tools for instructional use, spanning grades 6-12 across core content areas.
- Students using Amira reading tool up to three times per week for 10 minutes per day showed approximately four to five months of acceleration in reading comprehension improvement compared to non-users, according to vendor-reported data.
- Early childhood screen time policy limits use to 30 minutes per week and 15 minutes per day; no equivalent policy exists for kindergarten through grade 5.
- 10,000 families apply annually for approximately 2,500 gifted and talented seats across New York City.
- White students represent approximately 12.5% of students with disabilities in NYC Public Schools.
- 71% of private school tuition reimbursement for students with disabilities went to white families; 24% went to Black and Latino families, despite Black and Latino students representing approximately 75% of students with disabilities.
- Approximately $702 million in special education private school tuition was reimbursed in the most recent year referenced.
- A $17.25 million court settlement involving Naviance (a PowerSchool product) for alleged illegal data disclosure is available to New York City families, who have not been notified by the DOE.
- 79 sensory gym sites have been opened by the DOE across New York City, up from an initial 10.
- 900 intervention teachers focused on literacy have been hired and trained, working primarily with historically marginalized students.
- A 15-lesson high school instructional module on AI concepts, ethics and societal impact was delivered by 38 teachers through the Future Ready program in the current school year.
- MOUSE delivered generative AI fluency training to 83 NYC teachers in one Council district and 25 high school teachers in another district in the week preceding the hearing.
- Only 28% of English language learners with IEPs receive special education services in another language, per NYLPI testimony.
- 95% of English-speaking students are fully receiving recommended special education services, compared to 47% of Spanish-speaking students.

- DOE's Division of Inclusive and Accessible Learning to provide CM Zhuang with District 75 enrollment data broken down by geographic location including travel distance.
- DOE to investigate and report back on why the Annual Professional Performance Review algorithmic tool listed under Local Law 35 does not disclose its vendor.

- DOE to provide information on whether any vendor non-disclosure or data processing agreements have been FOIL-requested and the status of any redactions, per NYCLU testimony flagging trade-secret exemptions.
- DOE to explore and report back on including financial penalties in data processing agreements for vendors that fail to report data breaches in a timely manner.
- DOE to identify which schools are using the Prodigy app and report this to the committee, per Chair Dinowitz's request.
- DOE to publish a formal written data classification policy in the coming months, per the Chief Privacy Officer's commitment.
- DOE to update Chancellor's Regulation and data privacy and security policies page to improve clarity and accessibility, per Chief Privacy Officer's testimony.
- DOE to provide Council with a status update on each recommendation made in the confidential portion of the State Comptroller's cybersecurity audit, to the extent disclosable.
- DOE to finalize and publish updated AI guidance, inclusive of developmental age-appropriateness distinctions and stronger student-facing guardrails, before the start of the 2026-27 school year, per First Deputy Chancellor's commitment.
- DOE to report back to the committee on what steps have been taken since the 2024 Comptroller audit, beyond updating the Chancellor's Regulation and issuing additional reminder communications, to improve employee training completion rates.
- DOE to notify New York City families of their eligibility for the $17.25 million Naviance data breach settlement, per the Parent Coalition for Student Privacy's allegation that no notification has been issued.
- DOE officials who testified to return before leaving the hearing to provide CM Wong with data on gifted and talented program demographics and special education access, per Chair Dinowitz's instruction.
- Council committees to submit additional follow-up questions to the DOE in writing following the hearing.
