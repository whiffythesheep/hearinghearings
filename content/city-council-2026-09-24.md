Summary

Meeting Overview

The September 24, 2026 Stated Meeting of the New York City Council passed five substantive pieces of legislation, approved several land use rezonings, and introduced a substantial slate of new bills. The headline votes were on two bills sponsored by CM Crystal Hudson: Int 0246-2026-A, requiring the Department of Correction to return commissary funds in cash to incarcerated individuals upon release, and Int 0248-2026-A, requiring the Commissioner of Social Services to create a streamlined Fair Fares enrollment process by auto-enrolling, with consent, individuals already applying for SNAP or cash assistance. Speaker Menin noted pointedly that a 40% enrollment rate for Fair Fares is a failing grade, particularly given that the Council's budget expansion extended eligibility to nearly one million New Yorkers earning up to 200% of the federal poverty line. Hudson's framing was crisp and worth noting: both bills rest on the principle that government should return to people what is already theirs without an onerous process, a philosophy that is obvious when stated but apparently requires legislation to implement.

The Council also passed Int 1015-2026-A, sponsored by CM Pierina Sanchez, extending the J-51 tax abatement program for ten years through June 2036, raising the cost recovery ceiling from 70% to 100% of rehabilitation costs and lifting the assessed valuation cap for co-ops and condos from $45,000 to $60,000 per unit. The program supports building preservation and Local Law 97 compliance and is expected to reach up to 300,000 units citywide. On land use, the Council approved rezonings at 815 Hutchinson River Parkway in the Bronx (industrial to commercial) and 862-868 Kent Avenue in Brooklyn (industrial to mixed-use residential with mandatory inclusionary housing, including a synagogue), while disapproving a sidewalk cafe application from Cali Tajadas Bakery and Restaurant in Corona, Queens. Several other rezonings in Queens and Brooklyn were approved with modifications and referred back to the City Planning Commission, including projects in Flushing, Bushwick, and Long Island City, all paired with Mandatory Inclusionary Housing designations.

The general discussion session produced a notable volume of new bill introductions covering a wide range of issues. CM Brewer introduced a supplemental tree-planting bill after the Parks Department stopped accepting Council funding and requests for new trees outside its neighborhood rotation program, a bureaucratic own-goal that the bill attempts to correct. CM Louis introduced two bills to expand and strengthen the Alternative Enforcement Program for distressed buildings, doubling the minimum number of buildings the program must identify annually and significantly increasing fees for owners who fail to remediate. CM Banks introduced legislation to expand outreach for the SCRIE and DRIE rent freeze programs and mandate annual agency training. CM Morano introduced a bill to create an office of assistance for 9/11 first responders and survivors, making the reasonable observation that people who survived September 11 should not have to survive the paperwork. CM Wilson introduced the PET Act to require price transparency at veterinary facilities, citing a 60% price increase over the past decade and private equity consolidation of the sector. CM Schulman introduced a bill to lower the Legionella reporting threshold dramatically, from 1,000 colony-forming units per milliliter to 10, following two outbreak clusters this summer. CM Sanchez introduced a bill for commercial tenant legal fee recovery to level the playing field against landlords in eviction proceedings.

The meeting closed with a sharp public exchange on Gaza. CMs Hanif and Cabán both spoke about joining civil disobedience actions opposing Netanyahu's UN appearance and calling for an end to US arms shipments to Israel, describing the situation in Gaza as a genocide. CM Dinowitz pushed back directly in the final remarks, arguing that the term genocide was being applied before any military action took place and after a ceasefire, that it obscures rather than clarifies the policy issues, and that Israel is subject to a double standard not applied to any other country. The exchange was substantive and unresolved, and is unlikely to be the last time it surfaces in this chamber.
