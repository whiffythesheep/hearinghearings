Summary

Meeting Overview

The Veterans Committee held an oversight hearing on programs and services for LGBTQ+ veterans in New York City, along with hearings on two pieces of legislation. The session was chaired by CM Frank Morano and included testimony from Minority Leader Joe Borelli, who sponsored Int 0928-2026, and from the NYC Department of Veterans Services, represented by Commissioner Johnny Mata and several senior staff. The hearing was timed to coincide with Pride Month and drew on the legacy of James Zappalorti, a gay Vietnam veteran murdered on Staten Island whose case helped spark passage of the New York State Hate Crimes Act of 2000.

The two bills under consideration were Int 0914-2026, introduced by Morano, which would require a feasibility study on a pilot program for reconsolidation of traumatic memories therapy for veterans, and Int 0928-2026, introduced by Minority Leader Borelli, which would codify an LGBTQ+ liaison position within the Department of Veterans Services and require annual reporting on LGBTQ+ veteran assistance. DVS indicated it already has two designated liaisons in place, making the Borelli bill largely a codification of existing practice, but noted the reporting requirements would be genuinely new and valuable. On the Morano bill, DVS was diplomatically skeptical, noting the department is not a health care provider and suggesting Health and Hospitals would be better suited to run such a pilot. Morano signalled openness to amending the bill accordingly.

The dominant theme throughout the hearing was the severe lack of data on LGBTQ+ veterans. DVS does not currently collect gender identity information during intake, meaning the agency cannot say how many LGBTQ+ veterans it serves. National estimates suggest around one million LGBTQ+ veterans exist, with perhaps 70,000 in New York State, most concentrated in New York City, but these figures are rough. Nearly 89% of VA records lack gender identity data entirely. DVS outlined plans to redesign its intake process to capture demographic information more holistically, including sexual orientation and gender identity, and said it wants to involve LGBTQ+ advocates in designing the relevant questions. The agency also disclosed it is developing a certificate of eligibility for LGBTQ+ veterans discharged under discriminatory policies, which would allow them to access four categories of City benefits without having to present a discharge record documenting the basis for their separation. This program is expected to launch by mid-2027 at the latest and is grounded in existing Charter authority.

The discharge upgrade program, known as DUELS, was discussed at length. In fiscal year 2026, 38 LGBTQ+ veterans sought assistance through the program, with around 20 cases still pending and eight successful upgrades recorded across the last two fiscal years. The agency flagged that the trans ban introduced in January 2025 is already generating new cases and is being actively tracked. The hearing also touched on military sexual trauma, mental health disparities, outreach to surviving spouses, borough-level partnership networks, and coordination with agencies including the Mayor's Office to End Gender-Based Violence, the Department of Social Services, and Health and Hospitals. DVS identified sustained legal services funding as the most urgent near-term need, given that discharge upgrade cases can only realistically be handled by lawyers and caseloads are expected to grow.

Numbers

- Between 1980 and 1993, over 19,000 LGBTQ+ service members were discharged from the US military, many with other than honorable status.
- Under Don't Ask, Don't Tell, more than 13,000 service members were denied benefits, recognition and dignity.
- Between World War II and the repeal of Don't Ask, Don't Tell in 2011, an estimated 114,000 service members were discharged based on actual or perceived sexual orientation.
- Between 2000 and 2014, approximately 1,300 transgender individuals were discharged from the military because of their gender identity.
- Approximately 1,000 transgender service members have been discharged following the 2025 ban on transgender military service.
- National estimates put the total LGBTQ+ veteran population at approximately one million.
- An estimated 37,000 transgender veterans exist nationally.
- An estimated 70,000 LGBTQ+ veterans reside in New York State, most believed to be concentrated in New York City.
- Nearly 89% of VA records lack gender identity information.
- 36% of LGBTQ+ veterans nationally report avoiding services due to perceived bias.
- In fiscal year 2026, 38 LGBTQ+ veterans sought legal assistance through the DUELS discharge upgrade program.
- Approximately 20 of those 38 discharge upgrade cases remain pending.
- Eight successful discharge upgrades have been recorded across the last two fiscal years.
- Discharge upgrade cases typically take one to three years to resolve.
- DVS currently has two designated LGBTQ+ liaisons, one in place since 2024 and a second added more recently.
- Three outreach events specifically targeted at LGBTQ+ veterans were held in the last fiscal year, supplemented by monthly veterans mental health coalition meetings that include LGBTQ+ topics.
- The proposed certificate of eligibility would unlock access to four categories of City benefits: civil service exam residency preference points via DCAS, an ICERS pension buyback, and two Department of Finance tax benefits.
- Pride Health Centers exist in four of the five boroughs, with no such center currently on Staten Island.
- Local Law 4 of 2022 was passed unanimously by the City Council and extended City veteran benefits to those discharged because of sexual orientation.
- DVS reported it is unaware of any surviving spouses of LGBTQ+ veterans who have been denied benefits.

Action Points

- DVS to redesign its veteran intake process to collect demographic data including gender identity and sexual orientation, in consultation with LGBTQ+ advocates who will help shape the relevant questions.
- DVS to finalise and implement the certificate of eligibility for LGBTQ+ veterans, targeting a launch by mid-2027 or earlier, following active conversations with the Mayor's Office and relevant City agencies.
- DVS to execute MOUs with larger City agencies to facilitate timely data sharing under Local Law 37.
- DVS to send recommended language revisions for the Local Law 37 questionnaire to the relevant office for further discussion.
- DVS to upgrade its referral tracking system to record outcomes after veterans are referred to partner organisations, moving toward a longer-term case management model.
- DVS to update its partner intake process, including a refreshed assessment of what services each partner organisation provides and whether they meet current standards.
- DVS to develop a targeted outreach and awareness campaign for LGBTQ+ veterans, including specific messaging for those affected by the 2025 transgender ban.
- DVS to actively track new discharge upgrade cases arising from the 2025 transgender ban and develop targeted messaging for that cohort.
- DVS to provide the Committee with a more detailed breakdown of the 38 DUELS cases from fiscal year 2026, including how many remain pending, how many resulted in successful upgrades, and how many veterans regained VA benefits as a result.
- DVS to follow up with the Committee on the number and nature of outreach events conducted by the LGBTQ+ liaisons in the last fiscal year, and develop metrics for measuring liaison effectiveness.
- DVS to explore holding a forum specifically for LGBTQ+ veterans to support data collection and community engagement.
- CM Morano to consider amending Int 0914-2026 to assign the reconsolidation of traumatic memories therapy feasibility study to Health and Hospitals rather than DVS, given DVS is not a health care provider.
- DVS to monitor developments related to proposed federal MISSION Act changes that could cut veteran benefits over ten years and report back to the Committee on implications for New York City veterans.
- CM Wong's office to connect DVS with relevant nonprofits working on LGBTQ+ outreach, as DVS explicitly requested this assistance.
- DVS to report back to the Committee in approximately one year with concrete data on how many LGBTQ+ veterans were served, to assess progress against commitments made at this hearing.
