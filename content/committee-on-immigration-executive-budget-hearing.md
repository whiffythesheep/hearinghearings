Summary

Meeting Overview

The Committee on Immigration, jointly with the Committee on Finance, held its FY2027 Executive Budget hearing on Thursday, May 28, 2026, focused on the Mayor's Office of Immigrant Affairs. Commissioner Faisa Ali testified on behalf of MOIA, which has a nominal budget of $781,962 but an effective staffing budget of around $5.3 million when staff costs spread across other agencies are included. Chair Encarnación used the hearing to press for MOIA to be elevated to an independent agency, arguing the current structure obscures accountability and limits resources. The Commissioner acknowledged the constraints but defended the model on the grounds that proximity to the Mayor's Office gives the agency influence it might not otherwise have. This is a reasonable argument, but it also conveniently avoids a more uncomfortable truth: that an agency with a nominal budget under $800,000 is being asked to coordinate immigrant services across a city of eight-plus million people in a period of aggressive federal immigration enforcement.

The legal services discussion dominated the substantive portion of the hearing. MOIA's FY27 budget including contracts comes to roughly $34.2 million, with the preliminary budget having baselined around $33 million for legal support centers and expanded services. Chair Encarnación called for restoration of last year's $50-plus million in immigration legal services funding and an additional $30 million on top of that, citing the escalating complexity of cases and the sevenfold increase in ICE arrests of Asian immigrants alone. The legal support hotline operated by Catholic Charities received nearly 20,000 calls in FY26, with average call duration nearly doubling from four to seven minutes, a telling indicator of how much harder cases have become. The Rapid Response Legal Collaborative and Haitian Response Initiative were presented as operational successes, though the underlying numbers suggest demand is substantially exceeding capacity.

The hearing also covered the May 22 EO 13 audit, which found serious gaps in how city agencies track and report interactions with federal immigration authorities. Agencies were not consistently logging encounters, incidents were surfacing after reports were published, and existing guidance did not require reporting of subpoenas or warrants. MOIA acknowledged the gaps and said updated reporting requirements have been issued telling agencies to err on the side of over-inclusion. Whether agencies will actually comply is, as always, the harder question. On language access, MOIA reported a 2023 secret shopper evaluation found more than half of assessed agencies in violation of Local Law 30, a finding that sits awkwardly alongside the administration's stated commitment to language justice as opposed to mere compliance.

The tail end of the hearing covered immigrant worker protections, housing and immigration intersections, services for LGBTQ-plus immigrants under Local Law 73, coordination with the new Mayor's Office of Civic Engagement, and the launch of the NYC Neighborhood Passport program on June 11, which encourages New Yorkers to explore immigrant neighborhoods across the boroughs. The Passport program is the kind of soft-power civic engagement initiative that is genuinely useful but also tends to get more airtime than it deserves relative to the harder structural questions about legal services funding and agency independence. MOIA committed to responding in writing to eleven pages of questions from the Chair that could not be addressed during the hearing, which gives a reasonable sense of how much ground a two-hour budget hearing cannot actually cover.

Action Points

- MOIA to respond in writing to Chair Encarnación's eleven pages of submitted questions that could not be addressed during the hearing.
- MOIA to follow up with Chair Encarnación on the NYPD recommendation to send a patrol executive in response to 911 calls about ICE activity, and clarify what that designation means operationally.
- MOIA to continue implementing Local Law 73 in coordination with the Mayor's Office of LGBT-plus Affairs and the Mayor's Office of Equity, using assessment findings to produce a phased service plan for transgender, gender non-conforming, non-binary, and intersex immigrants.
- MOIA to launch a targeted multilingual media campaign in Amharic, Tibetan, Korean, Bengali, and other Asian languages by mid-summer in response to the sevenfold increase in ICE arrests of Asian immigrants.
- MOIA to continue requiring agencies to apply over-inclusive reporting standards for interactions with federal immigration authorities, following gaps identified in the EO 13 audit.
- MOIA to continue publishing agency immigration interaction policies online as agencies complete that process.
- MOIA to coordinate with the Mayor's Office of Civic Engagement on immigrant outreach and information-sharing.
- MOIA to coordinate with the Envision Freedom Fund on preliminary research into post-detention mental health impacts.
- MOIA to continue building Census 2030 infrastructure through language access, ethnic media, and community partnerships, and to coordinate with the new Office of Census once established.
- Council members invited to sign up for the public hearing scheduled for Wednesday, June 10.
