Summary

Meeting Overview

The City Council's Committee on Transportation and Infrastructure held an oversight hearing on Vision Zero, the city's street safety program now in its twelfth year, alongside six pieces of legislation and one resolution. The hearing drew testimony from DOT Commissioner Mike Flynn, NYPD officials including Chief Obey and Deputy Chief Thomas Alpes, the Business Integrity Commission, and Public Advocate Jumaane Williams. The session was substantive and at times pointed, covering enforcement trends, crash investigation procedures, infrastructure rollouts, new technology, and the legislative proposals on the table.

DOT Commissioner Flynn testified under Mayor Mamdani's direction, presenting Vision Zero Reimagined, a ten-year action plan released in September 2026. He cited record-low traffic fatalities in 2025 and strong year-to-date figures for 2026, including all-time-low pedestrian deaths. The plan commits to safety improvements at all of the city's most dangerous corridors within three years, daylighting or equivalent treatments at 1,000 intersections per year, neighborhood-wide 20 mph slow zones, and the creation of a public Vision Zero dashboard. Flynn said DOT is hiring roughly 200 new staff, with about half already in the pipeline, and described a new Office of Curb Management being stood up. The administration generally supported the Council's legislative agenda but consistently pushed back on specific mandates, arguing that flexibility is critical to effective implementation. That tension between Council wanting binding commitments and DOT wanting discretion ran through most of the hearing.

On enforcement, Chair Abreu pressed NYPD hard on the decline in moving summonses from roughly 700,000 annually before the pandemic to just over 413,000 in fiscal year 2026, a 41% drop. NYPD representatives declined to commit to returning to pre-pandemic levels, arguing that enforcement quality and data-driven targeting matter more than raw numbers, and that the landscape has changed significantly with the explosion in micro-mobility. Chief Obey pointed to dramatic increases in motorcycle and moped seizures and summonses as partly explaining the reallocation of enforcement resources. Chair Abreu was visibly skeptical, noting that camera enforcement and micro-mobility cannot fully explain a drop that predates the camera rollout. The committee also dug into the NYPD's Collision Investigation Squad, which handles roughly 200 cases per year and focuses on fatalities and critical injuries. A specific case was raised involving a cyclist struck in Midtown in June 2026 who was unable to walk for six weeks but whose case was apparently closed at precinct level and not escalated to CIS, prompting sharp questions about what qualifies as a critical injury and whether the thresholds are appropriate. NYPD acknowledged it would follow up. On drag racing, CM Santosuosso described ongoing street racing on the Belt Parkway the night after three teenagers were killed, and Deputy Chief Alpes reported a 161% increase in arrests for street takeovers year to date but acknowledged the problem persists.

Several bills received substantive discussion. Int 1153, the Stop Super Speeders Act, targeting drivers with 16 or more speed camera violations in 12 months and requiring installation of intelligent speed assistance devices, received the clearest administration support. DOT confirmed the state budget already authorizes a pilot program, and the city bill would provide required local authorization. Implementation complexity, particularly around out-of-state registered vehicles and back-end software, was flagged as a challenge. Int 1070, Chair Abreu's low-traffic neighborhood pilot bill, was welcomed by DOT with some requests to refine definitions; Flynn described learning from London's model and confirmed direct engagement with TfL. Int 613, requiring 100 miles of protected bike lanes annually for six years, was supported in principle but opposed as a rigid mandate. Int 113, CM Brooks-Powers' dangerous driving study bill, was supported with requests for minor amendments around data access issues, notably the difficulty of obtaining insurance data. Int 46, the autism warning plaques bill, faced DOT resistance on grounds of sign clutter and lack of evidence for behavior change, drawing a sharp response from the bill's sponsor. Resolution 687, calling on Albany to pass the Drag Racing and Speeding Enforcement Act, was presented without significant controversy.

The hearing surfaced several operational frustrations. CM Dinowitz described DOT failing to acknowledge receipt of constituent requests for stop signs and speed humps, suggesting a cultural problem within the agency around responsiveness to Council members outside of high-profile projects. CM Restler raised the fact that NYPD crash data on the city's open data portal had not been updated in five months, receiving a non-committal answer about timing. CM Pierina Sanchez raised streetlights out for nine to ten months at a time in the Bronx under a contractor called Highland, and the average streetlight repair time having ballooned from under three days to 8.5 or 28.5 days depending on which figure from the transcript is correct. On truck safety, the Business Integrity Commission was questioned closely about its side guard waiver program following the death of a teenager struck by a hydro-excavation truck in the East Village in September 2026. BIC defended the waiver granted in that case on the grounds that the vehicle had equipment filling the undercarriage gap, but acknowledged it did not have data on how often waivered trucks have been involved in pedestrian and cyclist collisions, which the Chair found disappointing.

Numbers

- In 2025, New York City recorded 205 traffic fatalities, a 31% reduction from 2014 levels.
- In fiscal years 2018 and 2019, NYPD issued approximately 700,000 Vision Zero-related moving summonses annually.
- In fiscal year 2021, NYPD moving summonses fell to approximately 300,000, the post-pandemic low.
- In fiscal year 2026, NYPD issued just over 413,000 Vision Zero-related moving summonses, approximately 41% below pre-pandemic levels.
- NYPD motorcycle seizures in 2025 totalled 5,567, compared to 2,169 in 2019.
- NYPD moped seizures in 2025 totalled 14,421, compared to 1,610 in 2019.
- As of September 2026, NYPD recorded 10,001 e-bike and 468 standard scooter moving violations in 2026 year to date.
- 81.2% of NYPD moving enforcement in 2026 year to date was accounted for by cars and SUVs.
- The city has more than 2,200 speed cameras across 750 school speed zones, operating 24/7 since August 2022, and red light cameras at 600 intersections.
- A 2024 City Comptroller report found that over 1 million speed camera violations between an unspecified period ending June 2023 were voided due to obscured or ghost plates, representing 22% of all camera-based violations over that period.
- In 2026, through October 18, NYPD conducted 31 joint ghost plate operations resulting in 440 arrests, 18,805 summonses, and 1,937 vehicle seizures.
- In 2025, NYPD issued 430,058 obstructed plate parking summonses.
- CIS handled 212 cases in 2026 year to date: 167 fatalities and 45 critical injuries. Of these, 77 (36%) were deemed criminal. 40 of 63 fatal criminal cases are closed. 75% of hit-and-run cases where a vehicle was identified led to an arrest; roughly two-thirds of those led to arrest overall.
- NYPD responded to 271 documented street takeovers or car meetups citywide from January 1 to October 18, 2026, an 11% increase year on year, with 170 arrests (up 161%), 157 summonses (up 246%), and 291 vehicle seizures (up 104%).
- DOT plans to deliver daylighting or equivalent safety treatments at 1,000 intersections per year under Vision Zero Reimagined.
- Over 6,000 leading pedestrian intervals have been installed citywide to date; the Council's data team found LPIs reduce pedestrian casualties by 12.7%.
- DOT has completed over 200 school 15 mph slow zones in 2026 and expects to reach approximately 800 by year end, with all 2,300 eligible school locations to be completed by the end of the current mayoral term.
- DOT is hiring approximately 200 new staff, with about half already in the pipeline, funded by historic new capital commitments beginning in fiscal year 2027.
- The average number of days to repair a streetlight has increased from under three days historically to between 8.5 and 28.5 days per the MMR (figures quoted inconsistently in testimony).
- BIC has jurisdiction over approximately 7,000 licensed trade waste trucks. Fewer than 80 side guard violations have been issued since the side guard rule took effect in 2023, representing a claimed 99% compliance rate.
- Transportation Alternatives identified 50 Boulevards of Death comprising 4% of the city's street miles but accounting for over 20% of traffic fatalities.
- 63 elected officials and 17 community boards have formally requested Sammy's Law neighborhood slow zones.
- Reckless driving arrests (VTL 1192) are up to 1,218 in 2026 year to date from 784 in full-year 2025. VTL 511 arrests for driving with a suspended or revoked license are up 9% year to date to 22,063 versus 20,709 at full-year 2025.
