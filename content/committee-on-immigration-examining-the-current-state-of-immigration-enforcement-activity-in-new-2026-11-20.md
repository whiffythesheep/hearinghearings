Summary

Meeting Overview

The City Council's Committee on Immigration held an oversight hearing on November 20, 2026, covering the current state of federal immigration enforcement in New York City, along with four pieces of legislation: Intro 1077-A (limiting NYPD detention on civil immigration detainers), Intro 1078-A (limiting Department of Correction communication with federal immigration authorities), Intro 739 (requiring agency training and publication of implementation plans on immigration enforcement cooperation), and a resolution calling on Albany to pass the BUILD Act, which would fund workforce development grants for immigration legal defense.

Community testimony from Assembly Member Catalina Cruz, Queens Neighborhoods United member John Atwolpe, and Arab American Family Support Center advocacy manager Mona Hermez painted a vivid picture of daily ICE operations in neighborhoods like Corona, Jackson Heights, and Elmhurst: warrantless street raids, enforcement near schools, families losing primary breadwinners overnight, and community volunteers filling gaps that neither the city nor nonprofits have capacity to fill. Public Advocate Jumaane Williams testified in support of the BUILD Act resolution, citing a 71 percent increase in ICE enforcement actions between January 2025 and March 2026 and warning that fear of immigration enforcement was causing immigrant residents to avoid city services, refuse disaster assistance, and forgo medical care. Cruz called specifically for stronger NYPD internal policies with real consequences for violations, mandatory DOE staff training on immigrants' rights, and significantly expanded legal services funding, noting that the state had recently secured $82 million for legal services this year.

The administration panel, led by MOIA Commissioner Faisal Leanne and NYPD Deputy Commissioner for Legal Matters Michael Gerber, defended the city's record under Executive Order 13 and existing sanctuary law. Gerber confirmed the NYPD has not honored a civil immigration detainer in a decade, described a suite of new Patrol Guide policies codifying non-cooperation with ICE, and explained a legal bureau review process that vets all external information requests. He acknowledged that the October 8 Marble Hill incident, in which NYPD officers moved protesters to allow ICE vehicles to exit near Lenox Hill Hospital, created a serious perception problem even if officers were acting to prevent harm. Council Member De La Rosa, who witnessed the incident, pressed hard on why NYPD appeared to be facilitating ICE's exit rather than detaining agents who had shot a constituent. Gerber maintained officers lacked authority to detain federal agents but acknowledged the communications failure was real and that the incident involving the congresswoman-elect was under active investigation.

Council Members probed several systemic vulnerabilities: ICE staging operations from city-owned and quasi-public property (parks, school parking lots, the Brooklyn Navy Yard, Floyd Bennett Field); informal information-sharing through the roughly 22-25 federal task forces NYPD participates in; crossing guards asking about immigration status in violation of department policy; and the absence of full member-of-service training nearly two years into the Trump enforcement surge. Gerber committed to providing the Council with a full list of task forces and information-sharing details. MOIA confirmed that 37 of 42 required agencies have posted signage under Local Law 114, with a full compliance report due November 30. The administration expressed support for the spirit of Intros 1077-A and 1078-A but said the Law Department was still reviewing both bills, a hedge that drew visible frustration from committee members who noted the bills had substantial prior history.

Numbers

- 71 percent increase in ICE enforcement actions across the New York City area between January 20, 2025, and March 10, 2026, documented in the city's May 2026 Executive Order 13 report.
- At least 32 people have died in ICE custody across the five boroughs in 2026 alone, according to MOIA Commissioner Leanne.
- 213,000 people in the New York metro area are projected to lose legal immigration status by end of 2026 due to federal policy changes, according to the U.S. federal government.
- 500,000 Haitians have lost legal status in New York City, referenced by CM Rita Joseph.
- $82 million secured by Albany this year for immigration legal services, of which $48 million was specifically referenced (full allocation unclear from transcript).
- $4.5 million allocated by the city to expand the Mayor's Office of Immigrant Affairs immigration legal support hotline to seven-day operation.
- MOIA hotline hours expanded to Monday through Friday, 9am to 8pm, and Saturday and Sunday, 9am to 5pm.
- Only 26.9 percent of represented immigration respondents were ordered removed in fiscal years 2019 through 2024, compared with 61.8 percent of unrepresented respondents, according to the American Immigration Council.
- Approximately 1,400 unaccompanied minors are currently in the immigration legal system, with roughly half believed to live in New York City.
- 37 out of 42 covered agencies have posted required sanctuary law signage as of the hearing date, with a full compliance report due to the Council by November 30, 2026.
- NYPD has not honored a single civil immigration detainer in approximately a decade.
- NYPD conducted an audit in 2025-2026 reviewing tens of thousands of emails and communications from task force officers; a number of cases were substantiated and are subject to pending charges.
- NYPD participates in approximately 22 to 25 federal task forces.
- NYPD's Operations Order 4 on immigration enforcement was described as the most-read operations order in department history.
- Five arrests were made by NYPD on the night of the October 8 Marble Hill incident outside Lenox Hill Hospital.
- MOIA community engagement team consists of 7 staff members collectively speaking 13 languages; constituent services team has 5 additional staff speaking further languages including Spanish and Korean.
- MOIA works with over 100 community-based organizations across the five boroughs.
- Oscar Bogle, shot by ICE agents in Marble Hill last month, was 28 years old and had his five-year-old son in the vehicle; a bullet reportedly remains lodged in his body.

Action Points

- NYPD to provide the committee with data on substantiated misconduct cases, charges brought, and disciplinary outcomes related to sanctuary law violations.
- NYPD to provide the committee with a full list of all federal task forces NYPD participates in, their stated purpose, the federal agencies involved, number of agents allocated, and any information-sharing agreements in place for each.
- NYPD to conduct a new internal audit of task force communications in early 2027, as committed by Deputy Commissioner Gerber.
- NYPD to expand sanctuary law training beyond executives to all members of service, with a plan to be developed in the new year, including a component for uniform officers.
- MOIA to provide the Council with a full compliance report on Local Law 114 signage requirements by November 30, 2026.
- MOIA to complete the email audit of NYPD-ICE communications by end of 2026 and publish findings.
- MOIA to share publicly available quarterly reports on city agency interactions with non-local law enforcement with committee members after the hearing.
- MOIA and council members to collaborate on proactive measures to prevent ICE from staging operations in city-owned and quasi-public properties, including school parking lots and parks.
- MOIA to engage with the administration regarding legal action against ICE for unauthorized use of city-owned lots, following the Mayor's November 2 letter to ICE.
- NYPD to work with the Law Department to complete review of Intros 1077-A and 1078-A and engage the Council through the legislative process.
- DOE to continue expanding mandatory sanctuary training beyond principals to other school-based staff, in coordination with unions.
- Committee on Immigration to hold a dedicated hearing on unaccompanied minors in early 2027; MOIA and relevant agencies asked to have specific numbers on unaccompanied minors ready.
- MOIA to investigate and document instances of ICE use of city parks property, including Flushing Meadows Corona Park, flagged by CM Thomas-Henry, and coordinate with Parks Department on enforcement.
- NYPD to look into allegations of NYPD involvement in the Kevin Sarita Rocha home detention incident referenced by CM Aviles and report back to the committee.
- MOIA to follow up with CM Kushner on the specific incident involving a school parking lot used by ICE in his district, and develop joint proactive security protocols for city-adjacent properties in high-enforcement neighborhoods.
- NYPD to develop a real-time community communications protocol for use during ICE enforcement incidents, so that officers can clearly explain NYPD's role to community members on scene.
