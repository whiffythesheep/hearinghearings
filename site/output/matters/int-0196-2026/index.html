Int 0196-2026

Law to require high sodium warnings at chain restaurants

Awaiting the Mayor · Sponsors: Oswald J. Feliz, Lynn C. Schulman, Tiffany L. Cabán, Christopher Marte, Rita C. Joseph, Farah N. Louis, Selvena N. Brooks-Powers, Linda Lee, Chi A. Ossé, Harvey D. Epstein, Shekar Krishnan, Eric Dinowitz, Lincoln Restler, Alexa Avilés, Chris Banks, Kevin C. Riley, Ty Hankerson, Shaun Abreu, Susan Zhuang, Jennifer Gutiérrez, Shahana K. Hanif, Shirley Aldebol, Nantasha M. Williams, Carl Wilson, Mercedes Narcisse, Vickie Paladino, Frank Morano · View on Legistar ↗

In brief

This bill would require chain restaurants with 15 or more locations to post a red-and-white triangle warning icon on menus and food tags for items containing 1,800 milligrams or more of sodium per serving, along with a factual warning statement about high sodium intake. It also requires the Department of Health to issue implementing rules within 6 months and conduct public outreach to affected restaurants. Violations would carry a civil penalty of $200 to $500.

Official title

A Local Law to amend the administrative code of the city of New York, in relation to modifying the sodium warning at designated food service establishments
