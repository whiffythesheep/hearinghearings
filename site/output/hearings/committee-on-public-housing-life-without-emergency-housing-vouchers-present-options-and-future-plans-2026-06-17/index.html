Life Without Emergency Housing Vouchers: Present Options and Future Plans

Committee on Public Housing

Chair: Chris Banks
June 17, 2026 · 3hrs 3m · Watch on councilnyc.viebit.com ↗ · View on council.nyc.gov ↗

Summary

Meeting Overview

The Committee on Public Housing held an oversight hearing on the premature federal termination of the Emergency Housing Vouchers program and the city's plans to keep affected households stably housed. The hearing also addressed T2026-2091, a resolution sponsored by Chair Banks calling on Albany to pass A.10992/S.9430-A, which would strengthen New York State's housing access voucher pilot program by prioritizing EHV households at risk of losing assistance and improving inspection standards. The federal government, via HUD, announced in March 2025 that EHV funding would run out by end of 2026, roughly four years ahead of the program's original 2030 statutory end date. In New York City, roughly 7,200 EHV households are split between NYCHA (approximately 5,100 to 5,700) and HPD (approximately 2,000), making New York the largest EHV administrator in the country.

Before agency testimony, the committee heard from a pre-panel of directly impacted residents and advocates. Stephanie M., a domestic violence survivor and single mother of three neurodivergent children, described how it took ten years to be rehoused and how the voucher provided her first period of genuine safety. Gina Capucci of New Destiny Housing, which navigated housing for over 700 EHV survivors, called on the City to extend City FHEPS eligibility to NYCHA EHV participants and fund it in the FY2027 budget, warning that without it survivors face eviction proceedings, shelter, and the risk of returning to abusers. A family member of a 62-year-old domestic violence survivor testified to the complete lack of guidance from NYCHA when funding termination was announced, describing hours on hold with no actionable information. Kenny Burgos of the New York Apartment Association offered the unexpected position that landlord groups support voucher expansion, arguing that vouchers mean stable tenancies and reliable payments, and endorsing the state resolution on both its prioritization and inspection streamlining provisions. He used his testimony to name the mayor, not landlords, as the obstacle to voucher expansion.

NYCHA's Lakisha Milanes and HPD's Adam Phillips laid out their respective transition strategies in agency testimony. NYCHA's original plan to convert EHV holders directly to Section 8 was blocked when HUD denied a waiver request in January 2026, after having first granted permission in principle, citing a Section 8 funding shortfall. NYCHA is now matching households to vacant project-based Section 8 units within its PACT portfolio, targeting 1,700 households for transition to a HOME-TBRA program to be administered by HPD, and using a small number of mainstream vouchers for in-place conversions. As of the hearing date, 3,400 of 5,100 NYCHA EHV households (roughly 67%) had returned public housing applications, 568 had been matched to units, and just 14 had physically moved. HPD, by contrast, had a stronger operational footing: all roughly 2,000 of its EHV households had received pre-filled HOME-TBRA applications and nearly 80% had returned them. HPD projects its EHV funding runs out by September 2026; NYCHA's by mid-November 2026.

The hearing exposed a significant gap. NYCHA acknowledged it does not have 5,200 units available and that roughly 3,500 additional project-based units would be needed to house everyone not routed through HOME-TBRA. The HOME-TBRA bridge is explicitly temporary, estimated to last about two years before that funding too runs dry. NYCHA was repeatedly pressed on whether any household would face eviction or homelessness and would not commit to a zero-displacement guarantee, noting that NYCHA is not the landlord for voucher holders and cannot control private-market landlord behavior. The administration declined to adopt City FHEPS as an additional tool, arguing it preferred to exhaust existing federal HOME resources first. Council members, particularly Restler and the Public Advocate, pressed on NYCHA's high vacancy rates, slow unit turnover, and the mismatch between the urgency of the housing cliff and the pace of placements: 14 households moved in a program affecting more than 5,000 families with a deadline less than five months away.

Numbers

  • EHV program originally funded through September 2030 under the American Rescue Plan Act; HUD announced in March 2025 that funding would run out by end of calendar year 2026, four years early.
  • Approximately 70,000 EHV vouchers were issued nationwide across 626 public housing authorities.
  • New York City received approximately 7,888 to 8,000 EHV vouchers in total, the largest allocation of any city in the nation.
  • NYCHA currently administers approximately 5,100 to 5,700 EHV households; HPD administers approximately 2,000.
  • NYCHA administers the largest Section 8 program in the country, currently serving over 110,000 households and approximately 222,000 residents across more than 26,000 participating landlords.
  • Average household income for NYCHA's EHV participants is approximately $22,500; for HPD's EHV participants, approximately $19,500.
  • Average monthly tenant rent contribution under the Section 8 program is $477, with a household income average of approximately $24,000 across all Section 8 participants.
  • Approximately 55% of NYCHA EHV participants are children under 18; approximately 30% of HPD EHV participants are children.
  • Approximately 15 to 16% of HPD EHV households are headed by domestic violence survivors; approximately 1,290 EHV vouchers citywide were allocated to domestic violence survivors.
  • Approximately 15% of HPD EHV households are headed by seniors and approximately 20% include a person with a disability.
  • Approximately 84% of EHV households were experiencing homelessness or at risk of homelessness at the time of referral.
  • NYCHA EHV borough distribution: approximately 47% Bronx, 23% Brooklyn, 16.5% Queens, 9% Manhattan, 3 to 4% Staten Island, plus a small number outside New York City.
  • As of June 9, NYCHA had received approximately 3,350 to 3,400 completed public housing applications from EHV households, representing roughly 67% of total participants.
  • 568 NYCHA EHV households had been matched to move-ready units within the PACT portfolio; 14 had physically moved as of the hearing date.
  • Approximately 1,000 of the 3,400 NYCHA respondents are out of compliance with program requirements such as annual recertification or inspection.
  • NYCHA has approximately 700 move-ready units within the PACT portfolio currently available for placement.
  • NYCHA transitioned approximately 75 households to mainstream vouchers allowing them to remain in place.
  • Approximately 1,700 NYCHA EHV households are expected to transition to HOME-TBRA, to be administered by HPD; HOME-TBRA funding is estimated to last approximately two years.
  • Approximately 3,500 NYCHA EHV households are expected to receive longer-term solutions through project-based Section 8 or other non-HOME resources.
  • The annual cost of NYCHA's EHV program is approximately $125 million.
  • The City has identified $80 million in HOME-ARP funding to reallocate to HOME-TBRA to assist a portion of NYCHA's EHV participants, subject to HUD approval.
  • HPD's FY2026 substantial amendment relocates approximately $35 million in HOME funds from NYCHA to a new HOME program for HPD voucher holders.
  • HPD EHV funding is projected to run out by end of September 2026; NYCHA EHV funding by mid-November 2026.
  • Approximately 20 HPD EHV households moved outside New York City and are ineligible for the HOME-TBRA transition program.
  • Placements from shelter into NYCHA housing have fallen by over 80% since fiscal year 2015 and now account for less than 5% of total exits from shelter.
  • Coalition for the Homeless data cited by the Public Advocate: longer-term shelter users in the DHS system grew 12% between 2023 and 2024, an increase of over 7,500 people.

Action Points

  • NYCHA to report monthly to the Committee on the number of EHV households matched to units, moved into units, and transitioned to alternative programs, through the funding expiration deadline.
  • NYCHA to provide the Committee with a breakdown of matched units by bedroom size and by development following the hearing.
  • NYCHA to provide the Committee with a breakdown of how many vacant move-ready units are located in PACT developments that have completed the transition versus those still in construction.
  • NYCHA to brief the Public Advocate's office on NYCHA vacancy rates, placement rates, and unit turnover processes.
  • HPD to provide the Committee with the date on which NYCHA notified HPD that NYCHA could not use the $35 million in HOME funds, and to respond to outstanding finance questions in writing.
  • HPD to confirm in writing whether landlord outreach communications were sent on the same timeline as NYCHA's and whether receipt can be confirmed.
  • NYCHA to confirm in writing the precise processing timeline for rental packages once received, currently estimated at approximately one week.
  • NYCHA and HPD to continue coordinating with City Hall on identifying additional resources beyond HOME-TBRA for the estimated 3,500 or more households not yet accounted for.
  • NYCHA to continue outreach to the approximately 33% of EHV households that have not yet returned public housing applications, including through the Mayor's Office of Public Engagement and peer-to-peer texting.
  • NYCHA to work with the City Council to reinforce messaging to EHV households that completing the public housing application is a required first step for transition assistance.
  • NYCHA to continue outreach to approximately 1,000 households currently out of compliance with recertification or inspection requirements to bring them back into good standing.
  • HPD and NYCHA to continue advocacy to federal partners and congressional leaders for additional Section 8 funding as the only long-term solution to the EHV funding cliff.
  • City Council Progressive Caucus and Chair Banks to continue budget negotiations with NYCHA over funding for vacant unit readiness and maintenance staffing under the Rentals Within Reach campaign.
  • New Destiny Housing to deliver its letter signed by approximately 350 organizations and impacted residents to City Hall calling on the Mayor to extend City FHEPS eligibility to EHV participants.
  • Albany to be pressed via T2026-2091 to pass A.10992/S.9430-A, which would prioritize EHV households in the state housing access voucher pilot program and standardize local inspection requirements.
