The Department's construction safety-related regulatory framework, training requirements, education and outreach, enforcement and technology and innovation. The Department's regulatory framework relies on the requirements in our codes, particularly Chapter 33 of the New York City Building Code and related Department rules. Chapter 33 sets the City's core safety requirements for construction and demolition work, focusing on protecting the public, workers and neighboring properties. It mandates safe job site conditions, site safety plans for large projects, stringent requirements for the use of cranes and heavy equipment, procedures for controlled demolition work and the protecting and monitoring of adjacent buildings. Chapter 33 not only sets requirements for how construction and demolition work is carried out — it also establishes requirements for site safety professionals at larger jobs.

For example, it requires a licensed construction superintendent to oversee certain construction and demolition projects, including the construction of new buildings, the full demolition of existing buildings and large alterations to existing buildings. Through rule, DOB has specified the tasks a construction superintendent is expected to perform in fulfilling their core obligations of maintaining a safe site, including a daily inspection of the job site and ensuring compliance with Chapter 33 and with approved construction documents. Additionally, pursuant to Local Law 149 of 2021, DOB has put restrictions on the number of job sites a construction superintendent can be responsible for and will begin to enforce a strict one-job limit and mandatory on-site presence starting early next year.

When DOB makes changes to the codes or our processes, such as changes to the superintendent requirements, it is important to make sure that the industry, building owners and the public are provided notice. To that end, DOB uses service notices, construction advisories, building bulletins and our My Building newsletter. If you have not signed up for the My Building newsletter, please go online and enter your email. We use these tools to keep the construction industry and public informed, to provide more detailed guidance, to clarify regulatory requirements and to alert the industry and the public to emerging safety issues and regulatory changes. The Department also uses its social media platforms and holds quarterly meetings with various segments of the construction industry and even monthly meetings in our borough offices where such issues are discussed. Ultimately, these proactive outreach efforts ensure that our industry partners, building owners and the public are well informed and can prepare for changes that will impact them.

The Department is always looking for ways to modernize and facilitate a safer City. I am proud to say that in partnership with the Mayor's Office, we are convening the Mayor's Committee on Construction Safety — one of the key recommendations and actions in the housing plan — as part of a broader effort to address injuries and fatalities across New York City's construction industry. Invitations to the first committee meeting were sent out this week and the committee is expected to convene shortly. This effort will bring together labor representatives, workers, industry experts and senior City officials to examine systematic safety challenges and recommend reforms, which include legislative reforms that we would work on with the Council. The committee's mandate includes identifying the root causes of persistent construction injuries and fatalities, evaluating current enforcement tools and identifying gaps in accountability, particularly where contractors repeatedly violate safety standards. The committee will also assess how emerging technologies can help reduce hazardous tasks and protect workers in extreme weather conditions, which was the focus of the Mayor's Executive Order 17 issued earlier this year. Through this work, the committee will strengthen safety while supporting the City's housing and broader development goals. Chair Sanchez, we look forward to your participation on the committee.

Pointing beyond itself to further support housing and development, the Department recently launched a related effort: the Affordable and Efficient Construction Reform Task Force. The task force is an initiative aimed at streamlining the development process in New York City and is a major component of the administration's Block-by-Block Housing Plan. The Department is working with industry and agency partners to evaluate multiple facets of the agency's regulatory framework to identify areas for improvement, to speed up construction and to make it less expensive to build in the City without compromising safety. The goal is to develop recommendations for operational changes and modifications to the City's construction codes, a process that is working in parallel to the important work of our code committee process.

As we shared during the recent hearing on the Mayor's Housing Plan, construction sites are safer than they have been in previous years. Proactive and complaint-based inspections, industry outreach and engagement and mandatory site safety training for workers have contributed to this progress. We believe that training is a key component that directly correlates to the downward trend in construction-related injuries and fatalities. Since calendar year 2023, the number of construction-related injuries on job sites have steadily decreased. In calendar year 2023, there were 692 reported injuries compared to 482 in calendar year 2024 — a 30% decrease. In calendar year 2025, there were 320 reported injuries, marking a 34% decrease from the year before. As of now, there have been 266 reported injuries thus far for calendar year 2026. Meanwhile, there were seven construction-related fatalities in calendar year 2023, ten in calendar year 2024, seven in calendar year 2025 and four so far in 2026.

I want to underscore this: there is no such thing as an acceptable injury or a fatality on a construction site. We believe that with training, education and outreach, those incidents are preventable and we have to work harder to make it so. What our data shows is that worker falls are the leading cause of construction-related injuries and fatalities, which makes fall prevention a focus for the Department. That is reflected in the curriculum — both the mandated SST training and refresher courses cover fall prevention and a number of other topics. Further, the City Council and the industry worked on Local Law 102 of 2026, passed at the end of last year and effective earlier this year, which updated the SST curriculum to include a new mental health module that addresses suicide prevention, substance misuse risks and the everyday stresses of construction work. This reflects a shift in how New York City thinks about construction safety, moving beyond hard hat hazards to the well-being of workers themselves. This marks a deliberate evolution of the SST program by acknowledging the human realities of the workforce that builds our City.

Today, approved course providers have issued over 396,000 active site safety training cards, nearly 74,000 active supervisor cards and approximately 114,000 active worker cards. DOB continues to conduct outreach regarding the site safety requirements, including through its annual Construction Safety Week event, publishing an annual construction safety report and conducting targeted outreach when we identify an issue. We take site safety training seriously. We have taken strong enforcement action against course providers who put the public at risk. Just last month, we coordinated with the Department of Investigations and the Brooklyn District Attorney's Office...

As the City Council is aware, the establishment of regulatory requirements and modifications to applicable codes is only one part of DOB's work. Ensuring compliance with those requirements is another, and includes enforcement of technical and safety standards. We receive complaints of potentially violating conditions from a number of sources, including 311, referrals from other City agencies such as HPD or FDNY, or a notification from the industry in connection with an incident. After notification of an incident or potentially violating condition, inspectors will be dispatched to the building or construction site to inspect. Where a violating condition is found, appropriate action is taken, which may include issuance of an OATH summons, a stop work order or a vacate order, depending upon the nature of the violating condition.

In the case of immediately hazardous violations — Class one — when the owner has failed to certify the correction of violating conditions, the Department will reinspect until all violating conditions are corrected. When appropriate, the Department utilizes enhanced enforcement tools such as issuing criminal court summonses or initiating affirmative litigation, which requires coordination with the New York City Law Department. Enforcement actions could also include disciplinary actions against licensed design professionals.

A response to an incident at 57 East Burnside Avenue in the Bronx is an example of where the Department took swift enforcement action in partnership with the Chair's office. On January 12, 2026, construction crews employed by a general contractor were performing demolition operations on a large single-story building. While work was ongoing, a building wall along Wolf Avenue experienced an uncontrolled collapse, spilling out onto the adjacent sidewalk shed and sending debris approximately 15 feet onto the roadway. Fortunately, no one was hurt because of the collapse, which would have resulted in a serious injury. The DOB investigation into this incident determined that the contractor ignored a previously issued full stop work order for the site, which legally prevented demolition from proceeding. Additionally, the contractor was performing the demolition out of sequence and contrary to the approved engineering plans, and was illegally using an excavator to carry out the demolition work. We took immediate action to suspend the registration of the contractor and issued violations. We are using every tool in our toolbox to hold bad actors accountable and look forward to working with the City Council to expand our enforcement tools.

Since the City Council passed Local Law 79 of 2024, requiring DOB to create a proactive inspection program, the Department has shifted from a primarily complaint-based approach to a more proactive one, which strengthens public safety and enables us to intervene before major issues arise. The program uses predictive analytics to focus resources on the City's most at-risk buildings and on bad actors. In calendar year 2024, the Department conducted 127,535 reactive inspections and 49,431 proactive inspections, with proactive inspections accounting for 28% of the total. In calendar year 2025, we conducted 129,058 reactive inspections and 57,673 proactive inspections, increasing the proactive enforcement share to 31%. For calendar year 2026 to date, the Department has conducted 109,793 reactive inspections and 54,226 proactive inspections, further increasing the proactive share to 33%.

The proactive inspections program is comprised of a team of 59, including inspectors and administrative staff. However, the work of the proactive inspections team and our inspector staff in general has a downstream impact on other areas of the agency. When violations are issued, that results in increased workload for other units, such as attorneys who represent the Department at OATH and the administrative and clerical staff who review and process certificates of correction.

Lastly, I would like to touch upon technology and innovation and what DOB is doing in that space to improve operational efficiency and safety. Expanding the City's available housing supply remains a priority for this administration. Further, the Department's work as it relates to commercial development directly impacts the creation of jobs and the industries that provide essential services to New Yorkers. Therefore, it is paramount that we have the technological capacity to support and keep pace with development in the City. That includes implementing enhancements to available modules and functionality in DOB NOW, as well as exploring ways technology could facilitate faster building inspections, such as with the use of drones.

We are continuing our work with the Buildings Tech Lab, a public-private initiative with the Partnership Fund for New York City. The Buildings Tech Lab connects technology companies with subject matter experts with the goal of identifying, developing and testing technologies to streamline our processes and improve operations. Through the Buildings Tech Lab, DOB is utilizing software to more efficiently route inspectors. That means we are able to respond to a larger number of sites in a shorter time frame. Overall, more efficient routing coupled with our high inspector staffing level of 609 inspectors, 14 in the pipeline and a vacancy rate of 8%, results in increased productivity.

This fall, DOB plans to engage formally with two firms — Teleme and Gripes — to develop and test software to help our enforcement team promote safety on and in existing buildings. Teleme will focus on using predictive analytics to identify violation-prone actors in the construction and real estate industries and support our enforcement investigations, with a particular focus on cases of tenant harassment. Gripes will help bring all agency data into one dashboard to streamline our enforcement operations. Next year, we plan to engage several more firms through the Tech Lab to support other aspects of our enforcement mission. Thank you for the opportunity to provide this testimony before you today.
