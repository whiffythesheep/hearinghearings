The Administration's Approach to Construction Safety

Committee on Housing and Buildings

Chair: Pierina Ana Sanchez
October 21, 2026 · 2hrs 32m · Watch on councilnyc.viebit.com ↗ · View on council.nyc.gov ↗

Meeting overview

The City Council Committee on Housing and Buildings held an oversight hearing on the administration's approach to construction safety, with DOB Commissioner Ahmed and several deputy commissioners providing testimony. The hearing covered construction injury and fatality trends, the proactive inspection program created by Local Law 79 of 2024, the partial collapse at the former Pfizer headquarters in Midtown, fraudulent site safety training cards, sidewalk shed reform, and the status of two new task forces on construction safety and affordable code reform.

The headline numbers tell a genuinely positive story, at least on injuries: DOB reported a steady decline from 692 reported injuries in 2023 to 266 so far in calendar year 2026, and from 759 worker injuries in 2018 — before mandatory site safety training was implemented — to the current level. Fatalities are stickier, hovering between six and ten per year since 2022, with eight in fiscal year 2026 and one so far in the current fiscal year. Five of the eight FY2026 fatalities were related to excavation and underpinning work, one was burn inhalation, and one was a struck-by incident. Worker falls remain the leading cause of both injuries and fatalities, though fall-related injuries have also declined sharply, from 251 in 2024 to 110 so far in 2026. Chair Sanchez pressed the department on whether the fatality numbers are actually improving or just bouncing around in the same narrow range year after year, and was not fully reassured. She flagged the persistent "other" category of incidents — 102 of 320 injuries in 2025 — as too vague to be analytically useful; DOB said a system migration to Inspections Plus would allow that category to be broken down more precisely.

The proactive inspection program under Local Law 79 of 2024 was a point of genuine mutual satisfaction between the committee and DOB. The proactive share of enforcement inspections has grown from 28 percent in 2024 to 33 percent year-to-date in 2026, and the program has produced over 14,000 violations at more than 13,000 unique locations since January 2025. DOB described using predictive analytics to identify high-risk buildings adjacent to construction sites and to map networks of bad actors across licenses and properties, feeding information to DA offices and the tenant harassment task force. The committee's frustration, voiced particularly by Council Members Joseph and Zhuang, was that even when violations are issued and fines paid, repeat offenders continue to get permits and keep building. DOB acknowledged the limits of civil penalty structures and noted it has been increasing use of criminal court summonses — 71 so far this calendar year, up from 36 in all of 2025 — and nuisance abatement actions, though it conceded these are difficult cases to bring. The problem of after-hours construction without variances, and the difficulty of enforcement without an inspector physically witnessing the violation, generated extended and pointed exchanges with CM Zhuang, who represents a district with documented, community-recorded illegal construction activity.

On the Pfizer headquarters partial collapse, DOB confirmed it inspected 180 related construction sites, issued 71 OATH violations and 19 stop work orders, but said the violations did not reveal structural risks comparable to the collapse site. DOB declined to discuss the cause of the collapse itself, citing an ongoing investigation with law enforcement partners. The committee also asked about 122 Broadway, where cracked beams on the 32nd floor were repaired without notifying DOB; the department confirmed it issued failure-to-notify violations to the site safety manager, construction superintendent, general contractor and special inspection agency, and required weekly engineering reports and a peer review before lifting the stop work order. On the Red Coat fraudulent training card indictment, DOB described a multi-layered audit process for course providers including undercover attendance at training sessions, and confirmed it revoked Red Coat's approval and invalidated its approximately 2,000 active cards. Small Business Services is providing free replacement training, with particular outreach in Bengali to the affected immigrant worker community.

Sidewalk sheds got substantial attention, with DOB reporting a reduction from over 8,000 sheds citywide to approximately 6,861 as of the morning of the hearing — a 15 percent drop in 10 months — partly attributable to the prior Council's shed reform legislation, which shortened permit duration from one year to 90 days and required owners to explain what work they are doing to address the underlying problem. Civil penalties for sheds up more than 180 days are still coming into effect. The committee pressed DOB on the tension between access agreement disputes under state law Section 881 and prolonged shed duration, and on whether data on the 90-day permit regime is yet sufficient to draw conclusions. Two new task forces were announced: the Mayor's Committee on Construction Safety, which will bring together labor, industry and agency officials to examine enforcement gaps, PPE accountability, repeat violators, emerging technology and extreme heat; and the Affordable and Efficient Construction Reform Task Force, focused on streamlining development without compromising safety standards, running parallel to the regular code committee process.
