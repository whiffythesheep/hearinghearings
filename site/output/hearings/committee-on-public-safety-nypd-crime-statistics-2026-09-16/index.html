NYPD Crime Statistics

Committee on Public Safety

Chair: Oswald J. Feliz
September 16, 2026 · 3hrs 17m · Watch on councilnyc.viebit.com ↗ · View on council.nyc.gov ↗

Meeting overview

The Public Safety Committee held an oversight hearing on NYPD crime statistics, featuring testimony from Inspector William Holt, Commanding Officer of the Office of Crime Control Strategies, and Deputy Commissioner of Legal Matters Michael Garber. Public Advocate Jumaane Williams also provided opening testimony. The hearing covered both the genuinely impressive reductions in shootings and murders and the more troubling picture on other crime categories that remain well above pre-pandemic levels.

The headline numbers are real and worth acknowledging: murders, shooting incidents and shooting victims are all at historic lows in the CompStat era. Year-to-date murders are down 24.1% from last year, with 173 total. The summer of 2025 — which also included the NBA Finals, FIFA World Cup and the largest Fourth of July celebration in the country — produced the fewest shooting incidents, victims and murders of any summer on record. Major crime citywide is down 6.2%. The NYPD credited its data-driven precision policing strategy, violence reduction zones, crew takedowns and breaking down internal silos between patrol, detectives and intelligence. The Bronx, historically the city's most challenging borough, is leading citywide in crime reduction at 11.9%, partly attributed to the split of Bronx commands into two separate patrol boroughs with additional resources.

The harder conversation concerned the crime categories that have not recovered to pre-pandemic levels. Felony assaults are slightly up year-to-date and have been climbing for years — approximately 40% are domestic violence incidents, and assaults on public sector employees are up more than 50% compared to last year. The NYPD has an 80% arrest rate on felony assault, an all-time high, but 263 individuals have been arrested three or more times for felony assault this year, up 205% compared to 2018. The department acknowledged recidivism as a serious problem but directed blame toward the broader criminal justice system — prosecutors, judges and bail policy — rather than its own practices. Grand larceny auto, while down 12.8% this year, remains roughly 140% above 2019 levels. The NYPD attributed part of the spike to key fob technology vulnerabilities that made certain car models easier to steal, and noted that coordination with manufacturers has produced some improvement.

Several members pressed on data integrity and constitutional policing. Council Member Ariola raised a widely circulated video of an officer apparently discouraging a crime report at Washington Square Park, which is under Internal Affairs investigation. Council Member Wong raised the case of a Transit District 20 captain transferred in March 2025 for allegedly encouraging the falsification of crime reports, with a disciplinary matter still pending. CM Wong also noted that the NYPD has been out of compliance with the 2020 state P-STAT law, which requires reporting on people killed by police and deaths in police custody. The department acknowledged providing some data under that law but did not concede full non-compliance. The NYPD described its auditing systems in detail — Data Integrity Unit, Crime Audit Team, ICAD audits of 911 calls against complaint reports, and quarterly command reviews — but members from both parties expressed skepticism that constituents being turned away from filing reports at precincts would ever show up in the data these systems are designed to catch.

Public Advocate Williams and CM Cabon pressed on the absence of collaborative public safety strategy beyond policing. Williams argued that crew takedowns create a unique window for other city agencies to reclaim spaces and address underlying conditions, but that this has not been systematically happening despite her raising it for sixteen years. CM Cabon pushed the NYPD witnesses on whether they advocate internally for investment in housing, mental health and other services that would reduce demand on policing. The department acknowledged in the abstract that root causes exist and matter, but did not describe any concrete advocacy role. The committee did not advance any legislation at this hearing; it was oversight only. Chair Feliz closed by committing to hold a similar hearing annually.

▸ Numbers
  • Year-to-date murders in NYC: 173, the lowest in the CompStat era, down 24.1% from the prior year.
  • 18 fewer murders year-to-date than the previous record low.
  • Year-to-date shooting incidents: 442, the lowest in CompStat history, down 9.6% from last year.
  • Year-to-date shooting victims: 538, the lowest in CompStat history, down 11.9% from last year.
  • Summer 2025 shooting incidents June 1 to August 31: 195, down 32 from the previous record low set in 2025.
  • Summer 2025 shooting victims: 249, down 44 from the previous record low set in 2018.
  • Summer 2025 murders: 66, down 12 from the previous record low set in 2017.
  • Major crime citywide down 6.2% year-to-date, representing approximately 5,000 fewer index crimes.
  • Auto theft down 12.8%, burglary down 13.9%, robbery down 11.9%, grand larceny down 5%.
  • The Bronx leading citywide in crime reduction at 11.9%, representing nearly 2,500 fewer crimes; Bronx South down 16.3%, Bronx North down 7.3%.
  • Index crimes in summer violence reduction zones down 23%, with Bronx South and Bronx North down 38% and 31% respectively within zones.
  • Felony assaults year-to-date approximately 15,021, compared to approximately 15,000 in the same period in 2019, with the category up persistently for several years.
  • Approximately 40% of felony assaults are domestic violence incidents; NYPD has a 92% arrest rate on domestic violence felony assaults, supported by over 450 dedicated investigators.
  • Assaults on public sector employees up more than 50% compared to the same period last year, representing approximately 2,165 incidents or 10.6% of total felony assaults.
  • 263 individuals arrested three or more times for felony assault year-to-date, up 205% compared to 86 such individuals in 2018.
  • Repeat offenders — those arrested two or more times — account for approximately 20% of felony assaults.
  • NYPD arrest rate on all felony assault complaints: approximately 80%, described as an all-time high, representing roughly 17,000 arrests.
  • Grand larceny auto year-to-date: approximately 8,524, versus approximately 3,623 in the same period in 2019, an increase of roughly 140%; down 12.8% compared to last year.
  • Operation Pit Crew in 2026: 16 individuals charged with 971 counts, more than one million dollars in stolen auto parts, 252 victims.
  • Larceny by false promise (jewelry swap scams and similar): up approximately 40%, with 486 victims year-to-date.
  • Criminal summonses issued in first half of 2026: approximately 60,000, an eight-year high, driven substantially by transit rule enforcement.
  • Civil summonses issued last fiscal year: approximately 100,000, with nearly half dismissed, largely due to paperwork deficiencies on handwritten tickets.
  • Transit crime: approximately 4 million subway rides per day on weekdays, with approximately 6 index crimes per day; transit felony assaults up 27% year-to-date (435 versus 397), transit robberies up 18%, transit murders up from 3 to 5, transit shootings up from 2 to 4.
  • Anti-Jewish hate crimes represent over 50% of all confirmed hate crimes in NYC, against a Jewish population of roughly 10% of city residents.
  • Approximately 54.5% of the 1,230-member mobile field force deployed Monday to Friday is assigned to the Bronx; that figure rises to approximately 50% on weekends.
  • Court cases on top charge of taking up more than one seat on the subway increased 3,000% in one year under the previous administration; this enforcement has continued under the Mamdani administration.
  • 580 police officer positions were removed from the final budget by Mayor Mamdani; the department noted that compared to two years ago, it has 2,000 more officers than it did then.
  • Over 50% of hate crimes confirmed as anti-Jewish, a trend sustained since October 7, 2023, fluctuating roughly between 50% and 60% of total confirmed hate crimes.
▸ Action Points
  • NYPD to provide committee with a breakdown of grand larceny auto figures by type of vehicle, including cars versus mopeds.
  • NYPD to provide data on approximate average total police officer deployment in the Bronx, including mobile field forces and specialty teams, to give a more accurate picture than static borough assignments alone.
  • NYPD to provide data on the number of officers disciplined for refusing to take crime reports or downgrading crime classifications, covering at least the last two years.
  • NYPD to provide data on how many additional New Yorkers were assaulted by defendants who had a prior violent offense case pending at the time of the assault.
  • NYPD to provide response time data, and specifically any data available on instances where officers responded to a 911 call but the complainant was no longer present.
  • NYPD to follow up on whether crime statistics are updated and corrections publicly issued when preliminary figures are revised, in response to CM Morano's question about the 2025 revision from 19 to 24 murders and 140 to 158 rapes.
  • NYPD to follow up with CM Wong's office on data requested in Wong's pre-hearing letter covering multiple crime data categories.
  • NYPD to provide CM Wong with audit data related to Transit District 20, specifically complaints reviewed and error rates, subject to constraints around the pending disciplinary matter involving the captain transferred in March 2025.
  • NYPD to follow up with CM Wong on the department's compliance status with the 2020 New York State P-STAT law requiring reporting on people killed by police and deaths in police custody, and to provide a timeline for achieving full compliance.
  • NYPD to provide data on how many officers have been substantiated for misconduct in cases where they killed civilians since 2014.
  • NYPD to provide recidivism data for crime categories beyond felony assault, to determine whether repeat offenders are similarly driving increases in other major crime categories.
  • NYPD to provide a breakdown of felony assaults by degree — first degree versus second degree.
  • NYPD to provide data on the number of instances where crime reports were discouraged or refused, to the extent it can be pulled from existing systems.
  • Public Advocate Williams's office to receive, from NYPD, data on instances where officers responded to calls but complainants were absent, if such data exists.
  • Chair Feliz committed to holding an annual hearing on NYPD crime statistics before the end of each year to review progress and outstanding challenges.
  • Any Council member office that receives constituent complaints of officers refusing to take crime reports is asked by NYPD to refer those complaints immediately to the department for Internal Affairs investigation.
