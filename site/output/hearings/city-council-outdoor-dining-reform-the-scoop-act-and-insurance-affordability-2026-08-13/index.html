Outdoor Dining Reform, the Scoop Act and Insurance Affordability

City Council

Chair: Julie Menin
August 13, 2026 · 1hr 47m · Watch on councilnyc.viebit.com ↗ · View on council.nyc.gov ↗

Meeting overview

The August 13, 2026 Stated Meeting of the New York City Council was a substantive legislative session passing several notable packages of bills, with Speaker Menin's remarks setting the tone on immigration, insurance affordability, public health and consumer protection. The session opened with the Speaker announcing that Council employee Rafael Rubio had been granted permanent asylum after wrongful ICE detention and two weeks in solitary confinement, and she used that occasion to draw a broader contrast with New Yorkers who are not getting the same outcomes, linking it to the Council's $82 million budget allocation for immigration legal services.

The most consequential legislation passed was a package of four bills (Int 0655-A, Int 0894-A, Int 0917-A, Int 0918-A) reforming the outdoor dining program. CM Restler made the central argument: the Adams-era 2023 reforms caused a 90% decline in restaurant participation because costs were prohibitive. The new legislation removes seasonal restrictions on roadway cafes, allows sidewalk cafe winterization enclosures from November 30 to March 31, requires cleanliness standards with license revocation after three violations in two years, and allows quarterly rather than lump-sum payment of consent fees. The latter bill, Int 0918-A, was CM Thomas-Henry's first to pass the body. CM Carr (Minority Leader) voted against the roadway cafe expansion, arguing that without a member call-up option the only real decision-maker remains DOT, whose community engagement he characterized as exhibiting "established elitism." The bills passed with solid but not unanimous majorities, with between 37 and 42 votes in favor depending on the specific bill.

The Scoop Act package (Int 0857-A, Int 0864-A, Int 0872-A, Int 0880-A) passed to require DOSP to install dog waste bag dispensers on at least 1,200 public litter baskets, establish a Parks composting pilot for dog run waste, mandate a multilingual public education campaign, and require signage with penalty information at park entrances. Int 0685-B established a new Office of Insurance Affordability within the Charter, responding to what the Speaker characterized as homeowners insurance rising 26% annually and small business liability insurance running 52% higher than other states. Three resolutions (Res 0387, Res 0389, Res 0395) called on Albany to cap homeowners insurance increases at 25% per year, improve auto insurance consumer information, and address property insurance costs for affordable housing providers. The Council also passed Int 0839-A making the Certification of No Harassment Pilot Program permanent, a significant tenant protection measure, and Int 0935-A requiring quarterly NYPD reporting on confirmed firearm discharges including incidents where nobody was struck.

In land use, the Council approved the Queens Community District 2 Walk to Park site selection covering dozens of privately owned properties for potential Parks acquisition, and approved the Flatiron NoMad Major Concessions expansion with CMs Wilson and Epstein speaking to its sustainability commitments including public restrooms and compost bins. Several rezonings in Brooklyn and Queens with Mandatory Inclusionary Housing components were approved with modifications and referred back to the City Planning Commission. A notable administrative item was the City Clerk's certification that a petition seeking to amend the Charter to require nonpartisan ranked-choice voting elections was ineligible for submission to the Board of Elections, which passed without debate.

General discussion surfaced several emerging issues. CM Maloney introduced a Legionnaires' disease legislative package following an outbreak that killed seven people in her and the Speaker's districts, with five residents still hospitalized at the time of the meeting; the Speaker announced a hearing for September 2. CM Feliz (Oswald) gave an impassioned plea about violence and open-air drug dealing in his district, citing a 15-year-old killed the previous night and an alleged arson on 183rd Street conducted in full view of news cameras and police. CM Epstein reintroduced Int 1008 to protect Medicare-eligible municipal retirees from being forced into privatized Medicare Advantage plans. CM Banks introduced Int 0991 to require a catalog of standard pre-approved designs for small capital projects, a straightforward efficiency reform. CM Brewer introduced a pedicab reform package (Int 0992, Int 0993, Int 0994) to tighten regulation, move oversight from the Department of Consumer and Worker Protection to the TLC, and require insurance, citing an estimated 1,500 pedicabs operating with inadequate credentials. The meeting closed with condolences to CM Farah Louis on the death of her father.

▸ Numbers
  • Rafael Rubio was held in ICE detention and spent two weeks in solitary confinement before being granted permanent asylum.
  • The Council allocated close to $82 million in the budget for immigration legal services.
  • New York State Governor Hochul announced more than $7 million to legal aid services for unaccompanied minors.
  • The Speaker is investigating four prediction market platforms: Kalshi, Polymarket, Coinbase and Gemini, with 60 questions sent to each company.
  • Homeowners insurance in New York has been increasing by approximately 26% annually.
  • Liability insurance across all industries in New York has been increasing by approximately 10% annually.
  • Small businesses in New York pay four to five times the national average on liability insurance, around 52% higher than other states.
  • New York City lost approximately 8,000 businesses in the second quarter of 2025 alone.
  • The outdoor dining reforms followed a 90% decline in restaurant participation under the Adams-era 2023 program.
  • On average, adding outdoor dining creates one new job per restaurant.
  • There were approximately 1,300 complaints about outdoor dining relating to garbage and rats in the prior year.
  • Outdoor dining operators will be required to close by 11 PM and face license revocation after three violations within a two-year period.
  • Sidewalk cafe winterization enclosures will be permitted annually from November 30 to March 31.
  • Dog waste bag dispensers are to be installed on or next to at least 1,200 public litter baskets on City streets.
  • Seven New Yorkers died from Legionnaires' disease in the outbreak affecting the Speaker's and CM Maloney's districts.
  • Five residents remained in critical condition in hospital at the time of the meeting.
  • Auto insurance premiums rose nearly 35% between 2022 and 2025 nationwide.
  • New York City residents paid an average annual automobile insurance premium of $4,223 in 2025, nearly twice the national average.
  • Since 2020, New York homeowners have seen average insurance premiums rise by more than $1,000.
  • Nearly half a million New York homeowners are estimated to be completely uninsured.
  • Res 0395 calls for capping homeowners insurance rate increases at 25% per year.
  • Approximately 250,000 migratory birds die annually in New York City, partly due to nighttime building illumination, under the argument made by CM Schulman for the Lights Out legislation.
  • 34% of law enforcement officers suffer noise-induced hearing loss or tinnitus, more than twice the rate of the general public, cited in support of the police hearing protection pilot (Int 0997).
  • The 47-03 108th Street Rezoning will produce 190 dwelling units, 51 of which will be permanently affordable.
  • The 63-02 Fresh Pond Road Rezoning will produce 54 dwelling units, 13 of which will be permanently affordable.
  • An estimated 1,500 pedicabs were operating in New York City in 2024, many with inadequate credentials, with 172 complaints recently recorded.
  • Most items on the General Orders Calendar were adopted 42 to 0; Int 0864-A passed 37 to 4 with one abstention; Int 0655-A passed 37 to 5; Int 0839-A passed 36 to 5 with one abstention; Int 0894-A passed 38 to 4; Int 1002-2026 passed 41 to 1; Res 0592-2026 passed 38 to 4; Res 0595-2026 passed 39 to 3.
  • A 7.4 magnitude earthquake struck Colombia, with deaths, collapses and thousands missing, mentioned during general discussion.
▸ Action Points
  • Department of Sanitation to install and regularly refill dog waste bag dispensers on or next to at least 1,200 public litter baskets on City streets, per Int 0857-A.
  • Department of Parks to establish a pilot program to collect and compost dog waste from dog runs under its jurisdiction, per Int 0864-A.
  • Department of Parks and relevant agencies to post multilingual signs at park entrances and playground facilities stating the prohibition on leaving dog waste and the associated penalties, per Int 0880-A.
  • Department of Health, in consultation with DOSP, to develop and conduct a multilingual public education and outreach campaign on dog waste removal, per Int 0872-A.
  • NYPD to publish quarterly reports on confirmed firearm discharges including date, time, location, borough, precinct and circumstances, including incidents where no one was struck, per Int 0935-A.
  • New Office of Insurance Affordability to engage with stakeholders and regulators and investigate the drivers of high insurance costs in New York City, per Int 0685-B.
  • Department of Housing Preservation and Development to report every three years beginning April 15, 2027 under the now-permanent Certification of No Harassment Program, per Int 0839-A.
  • Department of Finance Commissioner to establish a temporary program to resolve outstanding Environmental Control Board judgment penalties, per Int 1002-2026.
  • Council members and staff with Legionnaires' disease bill ideas to submit them to the legislative division ahead of the September 2 hearing.
  • All Council members invited to attend the September 2 Legionnaires' disease oversight hearing.
  • Council to refer 200 Kent Avenue rezoning (LU 0115 and LU 0116) back to City Planning Commission with modifications.
  • Council to refer 47-03 108th Street rezoning (LU 0118 and LU 0119) back to City Planning Commission with modifications, including striking Option 2 and reducing the rezoning area to preserve small homes.
  • Department of Citywide Administrative Services and Department of Parks and Recreation to proceed with potential acquisition of dozens of privately owned properties in Queens CD 2 for parkland, per Res 0611.
  • City to implement a catalog of standard pre-approved designs for small recurring capital projects in consultation with DCAS and relevant agencies, per Int 0991, once enacted.
  • NYPD to develop a pilot program providing in-ear electronic hearing protection equipment to officers and to publish a report on the pilot's effectiveness, per Int 0997, once enacted.
  • NYPD to train all officers on laws and policies governing physical restraints on pregnant and postpartum individuals in custody and to report annually on training progress, per Int 0995, once enacted.
  • NYPD to report quarterly on arrests where a child was present, including number and ages of children and whether each was left in the care of another adult, per Int 0996, once enacted.
  • Department of Health to establish a Legionnaires' disease hotline staffed by health care providers during summer months and outbreaks, per Int 1016, once enacted.
  • Roadway and sidewalk cafe operators to ensure outdoor setups are clear of trash, debris, graffiti, vermin, food scraps and unsanitary conditions or face civil penalties, and to lose their license after three violations within two years, per Int 0917-A.
  • Speaker's office to circulate details of CM Farah Louis's father's funeral arrangements to Council members that same afternoon.
