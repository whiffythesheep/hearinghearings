Mayor's Office of Ethnic & Community Media and Government Spending on Advertisements

Committee on Governmental Operations, State & Federal Legislation

Chair: Gale A. Brewer
October 28, 2026 · 2hrs 34m · Watch on councilnyc.viebit.com ↗ · View on council.nyc.gov ↗

Meeting overview

The Committee on Governmental Operations, State and Federal Legislation held an oversight hearing on the Mayor's Office of Ethnic and Community Media and government spending on advertisements, alongside consideration of three good-government bills and a preconsidered intro. The hearing was notable for the candor with which Council members, led by Chair Brewer and Speaker Menin, confronted the administration's effective dismantling of a functioning program through neglect, reorganization and inaction.

Speaker Menin set out the trajectory with numbers that tell the story plainly: City advertising spending with ethnic and community media peaked at $23.1 million in 2022 and had collapsed to $7 million by FY2024, even as the percentage figures look superficially respectable because overall ad spending has fallen off a cliff simultaneously. She recounted the Council's own forums with Black, Asian and Latino media outlets, where publishers reported losing contact with City Hall entirely and having no point of access to the administration. Chair Brewer added that outlets are reporting up to a 200% drop in City advertising revenue since Mayor Mamdani took office.

The administration's witness was Massoud Ahmed, newly appointed Executive Director of the Mayor's Office of Ethnic and Community Media, who started the role three weeks before the hearing. He is, by his own account, the only person in the office. He offered a thoughtful vision of Local Law 83 as a nationally pioneering instrument with dual benefits: getting City information to underserved communities and sustaining the civic infrastructure of local journalism. The problem was that he could not answer basic operational questions, including the total advertising budget across City agencies, why the office was unstaffed for eight months, why it was twice reorganized, or what any outlet in any specific borough is currently receiving. His answers to nearly every factual question were a variant of "I cannot speak to what happened before I arrived" or "I will follow up." Council members were increasingly pointed about this, with CM Stevens and CM Dinowitz noting that outlets like the Norwood News have already collapsed, and that waiting until the end of the fiscal year for a report is not a neutral bureaucratic timeline but a death sentence for struggling publishers.

CM Morano raised a genuine structural concern that Ahmed acknowledged but could not resolve: the office is now housed under the Mayor's Director of Communications, creating an inherent tension between sustaining independent journalism and being overseen by the political communications operation of the administration those journalists are meant to scrutinize. He also identified what amounts to a loophole in Local Law 83: the 50% spending requirement applies only to broadcast, print and digital advertising, not to social media or influencer campaigns, meaning an administration could shift spending toward those channels, reduce traditional advertising and still claim technical compliance. Ahmed was unable to confirm or deny this dynamic. CM Restler highlighted a related distortion in reported compliance figures: of $31 million nominally directed toward ethnic and community media in FY2025, $18 million was classified as out of scope, meaning actual qualifying spend was closer to 24% of the total, not the 57% headline figure in the annual report.

The three bills sponsored by CM Williams, Int 1127-2026, Int 1128-2026 and Int 1129-2026, address good-government housekeeping: repealing or amending 25 outdated or duplicative reporting requirements in the Charter and Administrative Code, establishing a uniform default due date for agency reports, and creating a procedure for agencies to attest compliance with operational mandates. Chair Brewer's preconsidered intro would create a working group under DORIS to study agency use of instant messaging technologies, following revelations about a Signal group used by the Mayor with auto-delete enabled. All four bills were heard but not voted on at this session.

  • Ahmed to review the ballot question changes to public notification requirements and assess their likely impact on advertising revenue for ethnic and community media outlets, following CM Restler's question.
