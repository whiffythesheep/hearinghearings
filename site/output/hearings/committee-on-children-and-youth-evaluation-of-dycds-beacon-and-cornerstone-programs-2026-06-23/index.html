Evaluation of DYCD's Beacon and Cornerstone Programs

Committee on Children and Youth

Chair: Althea V. Stevens
June 23, 2026 · 2hrs 6m · Watch on councilnyc.viebit.com ↗ · View on council.nyc.gov ↗

Summary

Meeting Overview

The Committee on Children and Youth, chaired by CM Althea V. Stevens, held an oversight hearing on June 23, 2026 focused primarily on DYCD's Beacon and Cornerstone community center programs, alongside a batch of resolutions calling on state and federal governments to act on youth-related financial security and fatherhood initiatives. The resolutions — covering baby bonds for foster youth, guaranteed income for low-income young adults, a federally funded universal child savings account, a state-managed $1,000 child account, sports betting revenue for youth sports, fatherhood resource centers, and restrictions on predatory food marketing — were largely introduced without extended debate, with CM Williams submitting a written statement emphasizing the racial wealth gap as context for the baby bond and guaranteed income measures.

The substantive work of the hearing concerned the chronic underfunding and procurement stagnation of Beacon and Cornerstone programs. The last Beacon RFP was issued in 2017 and the last Cornerstone concept paper in 2015; both programs have been running on extensions ever since. DYCD Associate Commissioner Wanda Ash-Ral confirmed that the agency is conducting an internal assessment of funding needs, staffing structures and program design, with an eventual goal of releasing a combined community center RFP covering both Beacons and Cornerstones, though no timeline was offered. The committee was not satisfied with this answer. CM Felder pressed hard on the specific problem of oversubscribed Beacons — citing a persistent wait list at the Goddard Riverside Beacon on the Upper West Side, with parents reportedly bringing children to work because no slots are available — and asked directly whether the fix was a money problem or a space problem. DYCD acknowledged the issue but declined to quantify funding needs without completing their assessment first, which the Chair pointedly noted could take years while children go unserved. Stevens called for an interim measure to address sites with wait lists now, rather than waiting for a full procurement cycle.

CM Joseph probed the gaps in DYCD's outcome measurement, establishing that the agency does not formally track academic improvement, workforce placement numbers, voter registration figures or adult education graduation rates at Beacon or Cornerstone sites. Providers measure some of these things themselves, but there is no centralized data collection. Joseph argued — correctly, and without much pushback — that without outcome data, the programs cannot make a strong case for expanded funding or additional seats. Other issues raised included: the absurdity of a three-day attendance requirement before Beacon participants are counted as enrolled (unique to Beacons, with no equivalent in Cornerstone or Compass); the fact that state stipends are not allowable budget line items for workforce development activities; the late-night hours extending to 11 PM that are underutilized and contribute to staff burnout; and the long-standing structural problem that most Cornerstone sites have only one full-time employee, making any expanded expectation functionally unworkable.

Provider testimony from the Center for Family Life, Children's Aid and United Neighborhood Houses reinforced the committee's concerns, calling for new RFPs with base rates reflecting true operational costs, cost-of-living adjustments in all contracts, stipends for interns and counselors-in-training as allowable budget expenses, and early structured engagement of families and providers in the procurement design process. United Neighborhood Houses noted that settlement houses operate one-third of all Beacons and half of all Cornerstones. Assembly Member Dais testified in support of Res 0142-2026 calling for a Center for Fatherhood Initiatives under the state Office of Children and Family Services, noting the Senate has already passed the bill but the Assembly has not. Two members of the Father Speaks Up movement testified about their personal experiences navigating family court and custody obstacles. A student from Queens High School for the Sciences testified in support of Res 0032-2026, describing inequitable access to youth sports programs and overcrowded Saturday Night Lights facilities.

Numbers

  • There are 92 Beacon programs located in New York City public schools.
  • There are 100 Cornerstone programs operating in NYCHA developments.
  • In fiscal year 2025, Beacon programs served more than 62,000 youth and 17,000 adults.
  • Cornerstone programs served over 21,000 young people and 6,700 adults in fiscal year 2025.
  • More than 126,000 community members attended or accessed Beacon community events in fiscal year 2025.
  • Beacon youth enrollment in FY2025 by grade: approximately 18,000 elementary, 17,000 middle school, 11,000 high school.
  • Saturday Night Lights operates 141 programs citywide, of which 46 are in Beacon sites and 50 are in Cornerstone sites.
  • Saturday Night Lights served 4,424 participants in fiscal year 2025.
  • The last comprehensive Beacon RFP was issued in 2017; the last Cornerstone concept paper was issued in 2015.
  • The 2017 Beacon RFP covered 76 weekend standalone programs and 15 Beacons with preventive family services components.
  • The 2017 contracts were set to expire in 2020 with an option for three years renewal, making the current contracts six years past their initial end date.
  • NYCHA has more than 600,000 open work orders, with an average repair complaint completion time of approximately 444 days.
  • The DYCD centralized maintenance ticketing system for Cornerstone facilities has been operational for approximately one year.
  • DYCD has procured approximately three vendors dedicated to triaging facility repair issues at Cornerstone sites.
  • DYCD assesses provider performance a minimum of three times per year through scored evaluations.
  • Res 0278-2026 proposes a $1,000 state-managed deposit into accounts for eligible children, accessible at age 18.
  • The median household net worth for Black families in New York is 14 times lower than for white families, per CM Williams' statement.
  • Res 0277-2026 references the American Opportunity Accounts Act (S.441/HR 1041), which would create a federally funded savings account for every American child.
  • DYCD fatherhood programming expanded to 17 additional sites in the current year, including sites at Claremont Center and Metropolitan College in the Bronx.
  • Workforce development stipends available through some out-of-school providers were characterized as approximately $100 per participant, described by the Chair as insufficient.

Action Points

  • DYCD to provide the committee with a list of all Beacon and Cornerstone sites currently operating with wait lists or oversubscription, to inform potential interim measures.
  • DYCD to explore and report back to the committee on interim options to expand capacity or add slots at oversubscribed Beacon sites without waiting for a new RFP.
  • DYCD to investigate and report back to the committee on why state stipends are not an allowable cost in Beacon and Cornerstone contracts for workforce development activities.
  • DYCD to brief the committee on its internal assessment timeline and when a concept paper or RFP for a combined Beacon and Cornerstone procurement can realistically be expected.
  • DYCD to consider integrating Saturday Night Lights programming into the core Beacon and Cornerstone program model rather than maintaining it as a standalone program in any future RFP.
  • DYCD to review and address the three-day attendance prerequisite for Beacon participant enrollment, which has no equivalent in Cornerstone or Compass programs.
  • DYCD to conduct and share analysis of attendance data by time slot to determine whether late-night hours extending to 11 PM are justified by utilization levels.
  • DYCD to evaluate staffing structures at Beacon and Cornerstone sites, particularly the single full-time director model at Cornerstone sites, as part of the procurement redesign process.
  • DYCD to engage stakeholders including families, providers, principals and community members early in the procurement design process, incorporating lessons from the Compass after-school procurement.
  • DYCD to assess trends in the centralized maintenance ticketing system for Cornerstone facilities and report findings to the committee.
  • DYCD to work with City Hall to explore stabilization funding for Beacon and Cornerstone contracts ahead of any new procurement.
  • Committee to request a dedicated joint hearing with NYCHA focused on Cornerstone facility conditions and the division of maintenance responsibilities.
  • CM Stevens to connect Assembly Member Dais with DYCD fatherhood programming staff, including contacts at Claremont Center and Metropolitan College, and with the City Council's fatherhood-related initiatives.
  • Assembly Member Dais to engage with CUNY on incorporating fatherhood and parenting resources into college programming, following the suggestion raised in testimony.
  • DYCD to confirm with the committee whether the preventive family services component previously attached to the 15 specialized Beacons has been fully transitioned to ACS supervision and what the current funding level for those services is.
