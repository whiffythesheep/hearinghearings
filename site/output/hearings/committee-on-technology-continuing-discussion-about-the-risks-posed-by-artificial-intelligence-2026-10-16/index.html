Continuing Discussion about the Risks Posed by Artificial Intelligence

Committee on Technology

Chair: Carmen N. De La Rosa
October 16, 2026 · 1hr 57m · Watch on councilnyc.viebit.com ↗ · View on council.nyc.gov ↗

Meeting overview

The Technology Committee held a follow-up hearing to last week's landmark Committee of the Whole session, at which representatives from OpenAI, Meta, Anthropic and Google testified. This session focused on xAI (now merged with SpaceX) and featured two panels: first, Gabriella Madrid, SpaceX's Senior Manager of Global Government Affairs, testifying under oath; and second, Devin Kim, a former early xAI employee and current whistleblower, alongside his attorney Ella Salazar. Speaker Julie Menin set the framing: cities must lead on AI safety because the federal government is failing to act, self-regulation is unacceptable, and New York City has a suite of legislative proposals including third-party independent validation, whistleblower incentives, emergency preparedness requirements and a plan for AI hacking city systems.

The Madrid testimony was, to put it diplomatically, unrevealing. She repeatedly defaulted to variations of "safety is core to everything we do" while being unable or unwilling to answer basic operational questions: how many employees work on safety, whether the company has catastrophic liability insurance, what incidents have been reported to authorities, and whether independent third-party validators have been engaged. Council Speaker Menin demonstrated live during the hearing that regular Grok, without any special settings, produced a sexualized image of her within thirty seconds of being asked — directly contradicting Madrid's repeated assurances that "strict controls" prevent exactly this. Madrid's response was to reiterate the controls policy. The committee was visibly and justifiably frustrated. Chair De La Rosa noted that xAI had to be subpoenaed to appear at all, having skipped last week's hearing at which every other major AI company testified, and that Madrid's claimed ignorance of the firing of Devin Kim — a founding employee who built the systems used to train Grok and whose case had been widely reported — strained credibility well past breaking point.

Kim's testimony was the substantive heart of the hearing. He confirmed that when he raised concerns about Grok's ability to assist with bioweapon creation, his supervisor responded that safeguards were "useless" and "it would kill us all anyway." Models were launched without those safeguards. He was fired the same week he was scheduled to present safety findings to leadership; most of the safety team resigned afterward. He stated that at the relevant period, approximately three employees were dedicated to safety out of roughly two hundred technical staff. He identified three critical gaps in xAI's approach absent from other frontier developers: no committed thresholds above which dangerous-capability models will not be released, no documentation and justification for deployment decisions, and no testing for loss-of-control and manipulation capabilities. He confirmed that, to his knowledge, xAI is not using independent third-party validators free from conflict of interest. His attorney Salazar argued for broad whistleblower protections covering safety risks that are dangerous but not yet illegal, algorithmic bias, non-consensual synthetic imagery and data privacy breaches.

The Council's legislative agenda, though no bills were formally voted on, was consistently referenced throughout. Key proposals include: mandatory independent third-party validation before deployment of high-stakes AI systems; a first-in-nation whistleblower incentive bill offering significant financial rewards from per-instance penalties; a bill requiring a city contingency plan for AI hacking city systems; and emergency preparedness requirements. Chair De La Rosa pressed Madrid on whether xAI would comply with, challenge, or exit New York if the validation bill passed — Madrid declined to answer, though the committee noted xAI has already challenged similar laws in California, Colorado and Minnesota. The hearing closed with the committee demanding written follow-up answers to the numerous questions Madrid could not or would not address.

▸ Numbers
  • xAI/SpaceX has set aside approximately $530 million for potential litigation losses, some of which relate to lawsuits over sexualized imagery generated by Grok chatbots.
  • xAI reported making over 100,000 reports to the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children in the current year.
  • Those 100,000-plus reports have resulted in approximately 300 arrests.
  • Three federal lawsuits were filed by xAI against users already facing criminal charges for creating illegal content using Grok.
  • In December 2025, within days of Grok's image editing features being integrated into the platform, approximately 23,000 images generated appeared to depict young children in sexualized contexts.
  • At the time of Devin Kim's employment, approximately three employees were principally dedicated to safety at xAI.
  • Kim estimated that approximately two hundred technical staff were involved in direct training and development of Grok at the relevant period, making the safety-to-technical-staff ratio roughly 1.5 percent.
  • xAI was founded approximately one year before Kim's departure, meaning the three-person safety team figure applies to a period roughly one year into the company's existence.
  • Kim lived at the office for four months during Grok's development, working around the clock including weekends.
  • SpaceX was founded approximately 25 years ago and now launches more rockets and orbits more satellites per year than any other nation or company.
  • Starlink provides broadband connectivity across more than 160 countries.
  • New York City has more than 400,000 tech jobs, exceeding San Francisco's total.
  • xAI performs a full risk assessment of its practices and controls at least once per year by its own account.
  • Private chat data is deleted from xAI systems within 30 days.
  • The Council's whistleblower bill structures financial rewards as a significant portion of financial penalties calculated on a per-agent, per-instance basis, meaning penalties could reach into the multi-millions for widespread deployments.
  • California's SB 53, establishing transparency requirements and employee whistleblower protections for frontier AI developers, was signed into law in the prior year.
  • The White House Task Force on Superintelligence held its first meeting the day before this hearing.
  • A September 2025 article cited a claim that within six to twelve months a swarm of AI agents could take over the entire internet via persistent botnet, causing hundreds of billions of dollars in damage — a claim Elon Musk reportedly endorsed on X.
▸ Action Points

  • xAI/SpaceX Madrid to provide the committee with details on whether the company carries catastrophic liability insurance and the scope of coverage.
  • xAI/SpaceX Madrid to confirm whether independent third-party validators free from financial conflict of interest are engaged, and to disclose their identities.
  • xAI/SpaceX Madrid to report what safety or cybersecurity incidents have been reported to authorities, under what legal requirements, and when.
  • xAI/SpaceX Madrid to clarify whether the company sells or deploys AI systems to New York City agencies, law enforcement agencies, hospitals or insurance companies operating in New York City, and to provide an exhaustive list of government partnerships.
  • xAI/SpaceX Madrid to confirm whether user class action lawsuits are barred under the company's bylaws or terms of service.
  • xAI/SpaceX Madrid to provide the committee with data on the volume and categories of reported misuse of Grok, including sexualized deepfakes, hate speech and privacy breaches.
  • xAI/SpaceX Madrid to provide details on the law enforcement reporting portal and process for communicating threats of violence to local authorities beyond NCMEC reporting.
  • xAI/SpaceX Madrid to have the safety team investigate and report back on the specific instance in which Speaker Menin's image was generated by Grok in a sexualized context during the hearing.
  • xAI/SpaceX Madrid to confirm whether all cloud infrastructure is located in the United States.
  • xAI/SpaceX Madrid to confirm whether xAI will formally commit to third-party validation aligned with the Anthropic commitment announced the prior evening, as Elon Musk suggested on X.
  • xAI/SpaceX Madrid to provide the committee with a written response to all unanswered questions from the hearing before a deadline to be set by the committee.
  • Council committee staff to follow up with xAI counsel on the status of bills under review by the company and to seek formal written feedback on legislative proposals.
  • Council to continue pushing the whistleblower bill, incorporating Kim's recommendation that loss-of-control events and unauthorized access incidents be counted as reportable incidents before anyone is killed or injured.
  • Council to consider strengthening the RAYS Act trigger conditions in light of Kim's testimony that neither the Hugging Face incident nor the Australian Medicare breach would qualify as reportable under the current threshold requiring death or injury.
  • Committee to circulate the full list of unanswered questions to xAI counsel with a written deadline for responses as a condition of ongoing engagement short of further subpoena action.
