Turning to the legislation being formally heard today. Introduction 262 would extend the operational season for beaches and outdoor pools and would increase the daily operating hours for beaches, outdoor pools, and indoor pools.

We share the Council's desire to expand New Yorkers' access to swimming, but the legislation as drafted presents significant budgetary, operational, and safety concerns. Beaches are currently open for swimming from the Saturday of Memorial Day weekend and close the Sunday after Labor Day. Outdoor pool season is generally patterned on the school calendar, opening after schools end in late June and closing again this Sunday after Labor Day.

As currently drafted, the legislation would require that both beaches and outdoor pools open in mid-April and remain open until mid-October, times of the year when air and water temperatures often range in the 52 to 60 degree range, leading us to expect that visitation would be exceedingly minimal.

Also, summer beach and pool operation entails hiring thousands of seasonal staff to serve as lifeguards, maintenance, and security personnel. The vast majority of our seasonal workforce consists of teachers, college students, and high school students, all of whom would be unavailable during the proposed extensions, as they are occupied with their primary academic or professional pursuits. We are proud of our extensive staff recruitment efforts, but the stark reality is that employees required to maintain and safeguard our beaches and pools would simply not be available during the proposed extension.

Additionally, the bill would require beaches and pools to remain open each day until eight p.m. Setting aside staffing challenges, lifeguards need to be able to see patrons in the water at either a beach or pool in order to safeguard them.
