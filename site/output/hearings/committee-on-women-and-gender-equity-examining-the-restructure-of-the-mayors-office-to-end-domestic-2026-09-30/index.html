Examining the Restructure of the Mayor's Office to End Domestic and Gender-Based Violence under the Office of Community Safety

Committee on Women and Gender Equity

Chair: Amanda C. Farías
September 30, 2026 · 2hrs 43m · Watch on councilnyc.viebit.com ↗ · View on council.nyc.gov ↗

Meeting overview

The Committee on Women and Gender Equity held an oversight hearing on September 30, 2026, examining the restructuring of the Mayor's Office to End Domestic and Gender-Based Violence (MODGBV) under the newly created Office of Community Safety (OCS), established by Executive Order 15 in March 2026. The committee also considered several pieces of legislation: a proposed local law requiring the MODGBV to report aggregate data on calls to its survivor hotline; a resolution declaring November 25 as the International Day for the Elimination of Violence Against Women in honor of the Mirabal sisters; Proposed Int. No. 295-A requiring the Department of Health and Mental Hygiene to establish a menstrual health screening program for endometriosis and polycystic ovarian syndrome (PCOS); and a resolution calling on New York State to require insurers to cover lactation consultation and counseling services without restrictions. No votes were taken on any legislation.

The administration was represented by OCS Commissioner Dr. Aisha Delaney-Brumsey and a second commissioner identified as Sethi. They argued that the merger elevates rather than diminishes the MODGBV's work, pointing to a $15.7 million budget increase in FY2027, the largest since 2018, bringing the total MODGBV budget to $89.1 million. They described coordination benefits across gun violence, mental health, hate crime prevention and domestic violence, and noted that the MODGBV retains its distinct identity, staff and programs within OCS. They acknowledged missteps in failing to consult the advisory council before the announcement but said they have since responded to the council's letter and met with stakeholders. Council Speaker Julie Menin pressed the administration on whether downgrading the commissioner title to executive director sent the wrong signal to survivors, expressed her view that the MODGBV should remain a freestanding office with a dedicated commissioner, and raised concerns about a 25 percent vacancy rate across the office's 79 staff lines, with 59 positions currently filled. Chair Farías questioned the administration on data reporting continuity, contracting infrastructure, the Crime Victims Assistance Program (CVAP) RFP, budget transparency and the coordination of gender-based violence work with housing, homelessness and gun violence programs.

A large and substantive panel of providers and survivors testified in opposition to the restructuring, almost uniformly calling for the MODGBV to be restored as a standalone office with a commissioner-level leader reporting directly to the mayor. Testifiers included representatives from Sanctuary for Families, Safe Horizon, Urban Resource Institute, Womankind, New Destiny Housing, SAKHI for South Asian Survivors, KAFSC, the Violence and Abuse Prevention Program, Girls for Gender Equity, Sisters in Purple, the Legal Aid Society, the Coalition Against Trafficking in Women and individual survivors. Common themes included: the restructuring announcement was made without survivor or provider consultation, which was particularly damaging for a field built on trauma-informed, transparent practice; the MODGBV's 25-year track record as a public health-focused, survivor-centered coordinator is structurally distinct from general community safety work and cannot simply be absorbed into it; the mayor has not publicly condemned domestic violence despite at least six high-profile domestic violence homicides in his first eight months; adding bureaucratic layers between the office and City Hall will impair the office's ability to coordinate across agencies; small culturally specific organizations face particular risks from contracting disruptions during the transition; and prevention, especially work with young people, perpetrators and communities, remains chronically underfunded. One notable exception was KAFSC, which offered a cautiously supportive view of the broader community safety framework, arguing it could improve coordination for immigrant survivors.

Safe Horizon's CEO raised a specific concern about the CVAP RFP, arguing that it reduces precinct-based funding while adding new requirements, with the program already having been cut from serving 53,000 survivors annually to 40,000 under the Adams administration, and warning it will fall further under the new RFP. New Destiny Housing flagged an urgent housing crisis: approximately 2,000 Emergency Housing Voucher households, many of them domestic violence survivors, face loss of their vouchers with no alternative matched, and called on the City to designate these households as qualifying referrals under CityFHEPS to prevent shelter entry. Survivors testifying in person described concrete failures in case management, court systems, housing and emergency response, illustrating the human cost of fragmented systems. The hearing, while producing no votes or formal decisions, generated a clear legislative and oversight record that the Council chair intends to use in budget negotiations and ongoing engagement with the administration.

▸ Numbers
  • There were over 249,000 domestic violence incident reports in New York City in 2024.
  • The MODGBV received a $15.7 million budget increase in FY2027, the largest increase since the office's mission was expanded in 2018.
  • The total MODGBV budget for FY2027 is $89.1 million.
  • Approximately $7 to $8 million of the $89.1 million budget is allocated to staff; the remainder goes to contracted programming and discretionary Council contracts.
  • The MODGBV has 79 staff lines, of which 59 are currently filled, representing approximately a 25 percent vacancy rate.
  • There are approximately 100 staff total across all OCS offices.
  • The MODGBV currently manages 90 contracts.
  • The Home Plus program was expanded to $4.3 million in FY2027 to help survivors remain safely housed.
  • Funding for immigration and family law services at the Family Justice Centers increased by 20 percent.
  • Mental health and case management services at the Family Justice Centers increased by 20 percent.
  • Youth-focused prevention through the Relationship Abuse Prevention Program increased by 20 percent.
  • $3 million annually in new community-based, culturally specific services for survivors of gender-based violence was invested in FY2027.
  • The Family Justice Centers receive approximately 63,000 visits annually.
  • The CVAP previously reached 53,000 survivors annually when fully funded; this fell to 40,000 following Adams-era budget cuts and is expected to fall further under the new RFP.
  • The MODGBV hotline receives nearly 100,000 calls annually.
  • More than one in five families in the New York City shelter system identify domestic violence as the reason they entered shelter.
  • Approximately 60 New Yorkers per year are killed in domestic violence incidents, with roughly half killed by a partner.
  • On any given day across New York State, over 1,000 survivors are turned away from services due to lack of capacity.
  • The City administers close to 4,800 Emergency Housing Vouchers, including more than 1,000 survivor-led households; approximately 2,000 households have not yet been matched to any alternatives as funding runs out.
  • Federal data shows 6.2 percent of women aged 22 to 49 have been diagnosed with endometriosis and 8.7 percent with PCOS in 2022 and 2023.
  • Among South Asian American New Yorkers, 85 percent of women aged 18 to 34 report experiencing sexual assault at least once in their lifetime; 48 percent report experiencing domestic violence.
  • SAKHI reported a 14 percent increase in survivors served, a 50 percent increase in its food justice program and a 1,000 percent increase in its legal justice program year over year.
  • The Domestic Violence Project at the Urban Justice Center opened 2,069 new cases in the first eight months of the Mamdani administration and answered 12,267 warm line calls, more than all calls received in 2025.
  • Safe Horizon's CVAP clients reported a 95 percent satisfaction rate with services in the program's current form.
  • At least six high-profile domestic violence homicides occurred in New York City in the first eight months of the Mamdani administration.
  • A Staten Island Family Justice Center director of community programs position had been vacant for three years before being recently filled.
▸ Action Points
  • Administration to provide the committee with a detailed budget breakdown distinguishing MODGBV-specific funding from broader OCS funding within the $89.1 million total.
  • Administration to provide the committee with a breakdown of what portion of the $15.7 million increase went to MODGBV programs and contracted services versus other OCS offices.
  • OCS to follow up with the committee on whether 311 call center staff have been updated on the MODGBV restructuring so that survivor access is not delayed.
  • OCS to report back to CM Gutiérrez on whether survivors and callers are experiencing any difficulties reaching the MODGBV following restructuring.
  • OCS to continue filling remaining 20 vacancies across the MODGBV's 79 staff lines and to report on progress.
  • Administration to share details of planned community-based service programs funded by the new $3 million culturally specific services investment before year end, and to invite stakeholder feedback.
  • Administration to ensure no gaps in contract payments during the transition of MODGBV contracts to OCS, with particular attention to smaller organizations that cannot float delayed payments.
  • OCS Commissioner Sethi to continue leading the interagency working group on building out internal contracting infrastructure and to communicate transition steps to providers proactively.
  • Administration to assess and clarify title and salary structures across the combined OCS offices in an equitable manner and to keep the committee informed as decisions are made.
  • Administration to respond to concerns about the CVAP RFP reducing precinct-based funding while adding requirements, and to consider whether the RFP should be revised.
  • Administration to engage with New Destiny Housing and the committee on using CityFHEPS to prevent shelter entry for approximately 2,000 Emergency Housing Voucher households that have not been matched to alternatives.
  • Administration to designate HRA domestic violence program households as qualifying referrals under CityFHEPS to address the shelter history eligibility gap identified by New Destiny Housing.
  • Chair Farías to elevate survivor concerns about statute of limitations on rape cases and family court custodial issues to relevant state officials and district attorneys.
  • Chair Farías to share information with the Speaker's finance team about expanded DOVE fund flexibility to support advocacy for sustained funding.
  • Providers and community-based organizations to submit details of successful programs and funding requests to the chair's office ahead of budget season.
  • Administration to ensure the MODGBV advisory council's quarterly meetings continue with Deputy Mayor Francois in attendance and that recommendations from the advisory council are responded to in a timely and transparent manner.
  • Administration to include survivor and provider voices in future structural or policy decisions affecting the MODGBV from the outset, not after announcement.
  • Chair Farías to pursue work with schools and providers on consent awareness legislation and programming in the new year.
  • Legal Aid Society testimony to be considered by committee in assessing whether the City Charter's classification of domestic violence as a public health issue is being respected under the OCS restructuring.
