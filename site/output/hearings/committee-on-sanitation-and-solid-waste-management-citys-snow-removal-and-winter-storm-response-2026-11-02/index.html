City's Snow Removal and Winter Storm Response

Committee on Sanitation and Solid Waste Management

Chair: Justin E. Sanchez
November 2, 2026 · 1hr 31m · Watch on councilnyc.viebit.com ↗ · View on council.nyc.gov ↗

Meeting overview

The Committee on Sanitation and Solid Waste Management held an oversight hearing on the city's snow removal and winter storm response, alongside consideration of Int 1042-2026, a local law that would require the Department of Sanitation (DSNY) to publish and visualize snow plowing operations data publicly. The hearing was substantive, covering pedestrian accessibility at intersections, bike lane clearing, alley service notifications, equipment readiness, interagency coordination, and the city's obligations to disabled and elderly New Yorkers. The Commissioner — who appears to be relatively new to the role — was the primary witness, with testimony also touching on contributions from Parks Department employees and New York City Service.

The most consequential policy discussion concerned the clearing of intersections and curb ramps. DSNY outlined a new prioritization framework targeting approximately 20,000 corners this winter, selected based on proximity to transit stops, DOT pedestrian volume rankings, and the presence of accessible pedestrian signals. This is a meaningful improvement in approach, though the Commissioner was candid that 20,000 corners is a small fraction of all intersections citywide, and declined to commit to specific clearance timelines given storm variability. The department also acknowledged that its emergency snow shoveler workforce is still being built and requires active recruitment support from elected officials — a notable admission of institutional dependency on volunteer labor for a core public safety function.

Several structural frustrations emerged. CM Restler pressed hard on bridge and overpass clearing, arguing that DOT chronically fails to clear pedestrian walkways on overpasses — citing a five-year record of inaction in his district — and called for DSNY to simply absorb that responsibility. The Commissioner was sympathetic but non-committal, noting that priority resources are already allocated to the 20,000 corners and unsheltered bus stops. CM Thomas-Henry raised the alley service notification failure from last winter, where residents went weeks without trash collection because they did not know to move bins to the street. The Commissioner acknowledged the department relied too heavily on people assuming the right thing, and committed to a more proactive communications approach including text, calls, and possibly mailers. CM Hankerson focused on the gap between making data available and making it accessible, pushing on why residents cannot access historical plow tracking data directly through a public-facing website; the Commissioner confirmed this is technically feasible but requires development resources.

Int 1042-2026 received limited direct discussion but was implicitly validated by the Commissioner's admission that the current snow operations platform does not maintain historical records and does not allow easy review of prior-day activity — the exact problem the bill addresses. The department signalled openness to the bill's requirements while flagging resource constraints. A related thread on disability access raised the uncomfortable reality that the city's legal obligation under the NYC Human Rights Law to maintain accessible pathways is largely being discharged by delegating responsibility to private property owners, with fines capped at $100 to $250 and no meaningful city-provided support for disabled or elderly homeowners who cannot shovel. The department confirmed there is currently no scheduled roundtable with MOPD and disability advocates, though the Commissioner expressed willingness to set one up.

▸ Numbers
  • DSNY has identified approximately 20,000 priority corners for accelerated clearing this winter, based on proximity to transit, pedestrian volume rankings, and accessible pedestrian signals.
  • Priority corners are those within 200 feet of a bus stop or subway stop, in the top three of DOT's five-tier pedestrian volume rankings, or equipped with accessible pedestrian signals.
  • DSNY issued over 19,000 violations across two storms last winter for failure to clear sidewalks.
  • The Commissioner estimated that with full enforcement resources, another 100,000 violations could have been issued last winter.
  • The maximum fine for failure to clear a sidewalk is $250; the minimum first violation is $100.
  • DSNY deployed 47 dedicated bike lane plow units for the current winter season.
  • DSNY has 120 new snow plows available this winter.
  • DSNY has over 100 skid steers available for snow operations.
  • The average age of DSNY collection trucks is 5.1 years.
  • The average age of dual-bend vehicles is 5.7 years.
  • The average age of salt spreaders is 7.5 years, with a recommended service life of 12 years.
  • Only 12% of salt spreaders are beyond their 12-year recommended service life.
  • DSNY added 48 salt spreaders last year and another 20 this year.
  • DSNY has 26 snow melters, of which 13 were operated during last winter's storms; some are still in winter upgrade cycles.
  • Emergency snow shovelers are paid $30 per hour, increased from prior seasons in recognition of the emergency nature of the work.
  • Requirements contracts for supplemental snow equipment require 24 hours notice for contractor deployment.
  • DSNY estimates that under favorable post-storm conditions (around 40 degrees), all 20,000 priority corners could be cleared within one to two days; prolonged extreme cold would extend that timeline significantly.
  • City of Montreal clears sidewalks only in a portion of the city; DSNY described the level of service as comparable to what NYC business improvement districts provide in commercial corridors.
  • Properties in New York City number more than 800,000 for purposes of private sidewalk clearing responsibility.
▸ Action Points
  • DSNY to update outdated 311 guidance that describes bike lane clearing as occurring after streets and as not guaranteed, to reflect current policy of simultaneous treatment with roadways.
  • DSNY to develop a more proactive communications campaign ahead of and during snow events to notify alley service residents that service is suspended and trash must be moved to the front of the building; channels to include text messaging, phone calls, email, and potentially physical mailers.
  • DSNY to work with NYC Service to provide the committee with a list of local nonprofits involved in connecting volunteers to property owners who need sidewalk clearing assistance.
  • DSNY Commissioner to schedule a roundtable with MOPD and disability advocates to discuss storm response priorities for disabled New Yorkers; no date currently set but Commissioner committed to setting one up.
  • DSNY to work with DOT and NYC Public Schools on coordinating clearance of routes to schools, not just sidewalks immediately adjacent to school buildings.
  • DSNY to develop situational awareness protocols to identify protected bike lanes at risk of becoming impassable ice blocks during storms, and respond proactively through vehicle removal or towing.
  • DSNY to pursue proactive equipment procurement ahead of DOT capital infrastructure installations, rather than playing catch-up after new bike and pedestrian infrastructure is built.
  • DSNY and DOT to coordinate to ensure adequate clearance for collection trucks with plows around permanent street-based dining structures.
  • Council members across the city asked to assist DSNY in recruiting pre-registered emergency snow shovelers ahead of the winter season.
  • DSNY to continue conversations with DFTA and MOPD to ensure priority corner clearing reflects areas with high concentrations of seniors and people with mobility challenges.
  • DSNY to investigate and develop a billing procedure for recovering costs when department staff clear a private sidewalk after repeated violations by the same property owner.
  • CM Restler to continue pressing DOT on responsibility for clearing pedestrian walkways on overpasses and bridges; DSNY indicated willingness to assist where resources allow but did not accept formal responsibility.
  • DSNY to investigate the feasibility of making historical snow plow tracking data accessible directly to the public through its website, in connection with Int 1042-2026.
