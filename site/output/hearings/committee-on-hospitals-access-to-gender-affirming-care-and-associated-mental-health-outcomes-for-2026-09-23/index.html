Access to Gender-Affirming Care and Associated Mental Health Outcomes for Transgender and Nonbinary Youth

Committee on Hospitals

Chair: Mercedes Narcisse
September 23, 2026 · 3hrs 4m · Watch on councilnyc.viebit.com ↗ · View on council.nyc.gov ↗

Meeting overview

Three Council committees convened jointly on September 23, 2026 to examine access to gender-affirming care for transgender and non-binary youth in New York City and the associated mental health outcomes. The hearing, chaired primarily by CM Tiffany Cabán (Mental Health and Substance Use), CM Mercedes Narcisse (Hospitals) and CM Amanda Farías (Women and Gender Equity), was driven by the immediate political context: NYU Langone and Mount Sinai had recently settled with the Trump Department of Justice, agreeing to end puberty blocker and hormone therapy provision for trans youth permanently, following earlier withdrawals by New York-Presbyterian. The hearing considered three pieces of legislation: Int 0949-2026, which would require DOHMH to publish LGBTQI+ health resources and a searchable provider map; Res 0208-2026, calling on the State Legislature to mandate anti-bias training for all medical students; and Res 0547-2026, calling on Albany to establish a statewide gender-affirming care access program.

Administration testimony was led by Taylor Brown, Director of the Mayor's Office of LGBTQI+ Affairs, who announced a $15 million, three-year investment spread across a direct care fund for pediatric gender-affirming care providers, a new DOHMH call and chat line linking patients to services, and funded research to identify care gaps. NYC Health and Hospitals confirmed it has not changed its scope of practice for gender-affirming care, operates Pride Health clinics at eight facilities across the boroughs, and has capacity in both medical and behavioral health services. DOHMH presented Youth Risk Behavior Survey data showing that transgender youth report suicidal ideation at 42% and suicide attempts at 49%, compared to 16% and 9% respectively among cisgender youth, underscoring the public health case for access. Administration witnesses supported Int 0949-2026 in principle while flagging technical language concerns, and Legal Aid Society and NYCLU both raised concerns that a publicly searchable provider map could, in the current environment, expose providers to federal targeting, suggesting the map's design requires careful thought.

Public testimony came from a wide range of witnesses including youth advocates from the New York Transgender Advocacy Group, Planned Parenthood of Greater New York, the NYCLU, the Legal Aid Society, the Ali Forney Center, Girls for Gender Equity, Make the Road New York and the Trans and Queer Coalition, as well as parents of trans children and Manhattan Borough President Brad Hoylman-Sigal. The testimony was consistently supportive of all three bills. Recurring themes included the difficulty of finding affirming providers, the role of word-of-mouth in navigating care, the specific unmet need for puberty blocker access for younger children (under 13), the geographic concentration of services in Manhattan, insurance coding problems for puberty blocker implants, and the mental health toll of the federal political environment on top of pre-existing disparities. Several witnesses called for the City to use its contracting leverage over hospital systems to enforce compliance with the NYC Human Rights Law, and to withhold municipal funds from institutions that have capitulated to federal pressure. Parents described driving hours out of the city, paying out of pocket to private practitioners, and in some cases moving abroad. The Borough President highlighted that the $15 million investment, while welcome, is unlikely to offset the impending loss of Medicaid reimbursement if a federal rule takes effect on October 13.

The hearing produced no votes or formal decisions, which is typical for oversight hearings, but the overall direction is clear: the Council is moving toward passing Int 0949-2026 in some form, with the provider map design to be worked through with advocates and legal organizations. The administration's $15 million commitment exists but its operational details remain to be established. The more fundamental problem, that two of the city's largest hospital systems have permanently withdrawn from providing this care under federal pressure, is not something the Council or the Mamdani administration has yet solved, and the hearing made clear that no one has a fully credible answer to it.

▸ Numbers
  • 27 states plus Puerto Rico have enacted bans on gender-affirming care for transgender and non-binary youth, affecting 48% of transgender youth nationwide.
  • 7% of transgender and non-binary young people using hormones to support their gender transition were concerned about losing access to that care, according to a recent survey.
  • Access to gender-affirming interventions is associated with approximately 60% lower odds of moderate to severe depression and over 70% lower odds of suicidality in youth.
  • Anti-trans legislation in restrictive states has been associated with a 72% increase in suicide attempts among transgender and gender non-binary people compared to states without such legislation, per a study published in Nature.
  • Transgender youth who were unable to access hormones were nearly twice as likely to attempt suicide as those who could, per the 2025 Trevor Project survey.
  • Four out of ten trans and non-binary young people seriously considered attempting suicide in the past year, per the Trevor Project.
  • In 2023, New York City public high school students identifying as transgender reported suicidal ideation at a rate of 42%, versus 16% for cisgender students.
  • Non-binary students reported suicidal ideation at 38%; students identifying as some other gender at 45%; questioning students at 28%.
  • Transgender students reported suicide attempts at 49%; cisgender students at 9%.
  • A 2022 prospective study of 100 transgender and gender non-binary youth aged 13 to 20 found that gender-affirming interventions were associated with lower odds of moderate to severe depression and significantly lower odds of self-harm and suicidal thoughts.
  • A 2023 study tracking 300 transgender and non-binary youth over two years receiving gender-affirming hormone therapy showed significant decreases in depression and anxiety.
  • 17.3% of transgender New Yorkers have reported cost barriers to needed medical care, compared to 7.3% of non-LGBT individuals.
  • 36.2% of transgender New Yorkers reported unreliable transportation and housing insecurity as a barrier to health care, versus 17.1% of non-LGBT individuals.
  • More than one third of LGBT New Yorkers report too few health care providers trained in LGBT issues; among trans and gender non-conforming New Yorkers, more than half identified the lack of LGBT and trans providers as a significant barrier.
  • NYC Health and Hospitals operates Pride Health clinics at eight facilities: Bellevue, Elmhurst, Gotham Health Gouverneur, Gotham Health Judson, Jacobi, Lincoln, Metropolitan and Woodhull.
  • Health and Hospitals provides mental health services in approximately 50 schools in New York City.
  • Planned Parenthood of Greater New York conducted over 3,100 therapy visits in 2025, providing gender-affirming care to over 1,500 individuals.
  • The Mamdani administration announced a $15 million, three-year investment to protect and expand access to gender-affirming care for transgender and non-binary youth, split across a direct care provider fund, a patient navigation line and research.
  • NYU Langone reached a settlement with the DOJ reported to involve a payment of over $8 million and agreement to end gender-affirming care for youth.
  • A federal rule is expected to take effect on October 13 that would end Medicaid and CHIP reimbursement for gender-affirming care for young people.
  • Three in five LGBT adults are reported to be without food security, per testimony.
  • The DOHMH LGBT health services map was first launched in 2018 and has since received only ad hoc updates; a comprehensive review and survey of listed providers is planned for fall 2026.
  • Nearly one in five youth report attempting suicide and nearly 50% seriously consider attempting suicide within any given one-year period, per Girls for Gender Equity testimony.
▸ Action Points
  • DOHMH to conduct a comprehensive survey of all providers currently listed on the LGBT health services map to verify accuracy, confirm continued provision of culturally competent and affirming services, and reach out to additional providers for inclusion, with the survey to launch in fall 2026.
  • DOHMH to update the existing health map listing for Mount Sinai and any other providers known to have withdrawn gender-affirming care services, and to establish a clearer timeline for future updates to the map.
  • Mayor's Office of LGBTQI+ Affairs to publish a comprehensive rights and resource guide for transgender and non-binary New Yorkers as soon as possible.
  • DOHMH to implement the new direct care access fund for qualified providers of pediatric gender-affirming care, prioritizing continuity of puberty blocker and hormone therapy access.
  • DOHMH to launch a confidential call and chat line linking patients to gender-affirming care services in real time.
  • DOHMH to fund and conduct research identifying gaps in health care access and outcomes for transgender and non-binary New Yorkers.
  • Mayor's Office of LGBTQI+ Affairs to continue cross-agency coordination with DOHMH, Health and Hospitals and other agencies to ensure LGBTQI+ New Yorkers are informed about available services.
  • Mayor's Office of LGBTQI+ Affairs (Director Brown) to monitor federal legal and regulatory developments, including executive orders and litigation, and track their potential impact on transgender New Yorkers, in coordination with the Law Department and Office of Chief Counsel.
  • Administration witnesses to follow up with the Council on the question of what immediate support is available to patients facing care disruption before a formal complaint process begins, particularly with respect to the LGBTQI+ Health Care Bill of Rights.
  • CM Schulman (sponsor of Int 0949-2026) to work with Legal Aid Society, NYCLU and advocacy groups to refine the provider map provisions in the bill to address concerns about provider safety and potential federal targeting.
  • Council to engage with the administration on whether the $15 million investment can be directed specifically toward providers serving younger trans children, including those needing puberty blockers, given that this population has been identified as the most underserved.
  • Council to examine whether City contracting leverage over hospital systems such as Mount Sinai and NYU Langone can be used to enforce compliance with the NYC Human Rights Law regarding provision of gender-affirming care.
  • NYC Health and Hospitals to notify the Council of any significant policy changes affecting equitable access to gender-affirming care, within the bounds of patient privacy law.
  • Girls for Gender Equity to submit written bill edits and additional commentary on Int 0949-2026 for the committee record.
  • Council committees to follow up with NYCLU on their recommendation to amend Res 0547-2026 to reflect the State's broader definition of gender-affirming care, including mental health care and exclusion of non-consensual surgeries on intersex youth.
  • DOHMH to clarify whether the current DOHMH health portal or a new resource will be used to fulfill the requirements of Int 0949-2026, and to specify technical language amendments needed in the bill.
  • CM Narcisse and committee chairs to follow up with youth advocates from NYCTAG and Trans and Queer Coalition on additional City actions beyond the three bills, including expanded mental health provider access and preservation of LGBTQ+ historical representation in public spaces.
  • Andy Bowen (Queer Budget Center) to submit written feedback on all three bills to the committee record.
  • Sue Mendez (NYLPI) to submit detailed written testimony for the record including recommendations specific to undocumented and immigrant transgender youth.
