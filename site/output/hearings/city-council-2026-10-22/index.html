Meeting of October 22, 2026

City Council

Chair: Julie Menin
October 22, 2026 · 2hrs 32m · Watch on councilnyc.viebit.com ↗ · View on council.nyc.gov ↗

Meeting overview

The New York City Council Stated Meeting of October 22, 2026 passed several significant pieces of legislation, most notably a ban on horse-drawn carriages, the Community Opportunity to Purchase Act (COPA), and an expansion of rent-freeze eligibility for seniors and disabled New Yorkers. The meeting also advanced voting rights legislation for incarcerated people, a special education equity study, and a gender-based violence data bill.

The dominant item was Int 0943-2026-A, known as Roman's Law, which bans the issuance of new horse-drawn carriage licenses and permanently terminates all existing licenses on May 31, 2028. The bill requires a humane disposition plan for horses, prohibits their sale for slaughter, mandates a workforce transition plan by February 2027, and directs the administration to consider compensation for medallion owners. Speaker Menin framed it as the culmination of 19 years of failed attempts, referencing the death of Raman Mahajan, an 18-year-old tourist who died in a Central Park carriage incident earlier in 2026. Before the main vote, CM Gennaro moved to amend the bill by extending the sunset to January 1, 2030, which would have delayed the final vote to the next Stated. The amendment was defeated 6-43. Opposition to the bill itself was limited to seven members, with two abstentions. CM Morano offered the most substantive critique of the ban, noting that in the industry's 168-year history no driver had ever had a license revoked or been convicted of animal cruelty, and questioning whether the incident that triggered the bill was actually caused by the carriage industry given the family's lawsuit named the City, Con Edison, and several agencies but not the industry. CM Gennaro warned that the 14-month operational window is insufficient for owners to arrange humane horse retirement, noting annual horse care costs of $25,000-$30,000 per animal. CM Farah Louis abstained, citing inadequate worker protection commitments. CM Schulman, who chaired the relevant committee, and bill sponsor CM Marte both argued that further delay was unjustifiable after two decades of inaction.

Int 0905-2026-A, the Community Opportunity to Purchase Act (COPA), passed 38-9 with two abstentions after a six-year legislative saga that included a prior mayoral veto. The bill gives qualified nonprofit entities a first right of interest and first right to purchase certain residential properties when offered for sale. The bill was significantly narrowed from its original scope of approximately 10,000 buildings to around 2,100, limited to properties between 4 and 800 units that either carry an existing affordability agreement, are managed by HPD, or show a baseline level of physical distress. CM Nurse, the bill's sponsor, framed it as an anti-displacement tool for communities facing speculative investment. Opposition came primarily from Republican members and CM Morano, who raised constitutional concerns about restrictions on private property rights and predicted the bill would face judicial challenge. CM Mealy offered the most substantive skeptical voice from the majority bloc, citing data on subsidized housing providers collecting below 80 percent of rent rolls and expressing concern about generational wealth for Black and Brown homeowners. CM Gennaro voted no on both COPA and the horse carriage ban. The bill passed with broad progressive support and explicit backing from the Speaker, who described the narrowing from 10,000 to 2,100 buildings as the product of genuine stakeholder engagement including the real estate industry.

Int 0978-2026-B raises the maximum income eligibility for both SCRIE and DRIE from $50,000 to $75,000, effective July 1, 2026, and passed unanimously. The income cap had not been adjusted since 2019. The voting bills for incarcerated people, Int 0786-2026-A and Int 0797-2026-A, passed 42-7 and 48-1 respectively. Public Advocate Jumaane Williams spoke in support of the reporting bill, noting that close to 7,000 people are detained on Rikers Island, most of whom are legally eligible to vote, but that ballot rejection rates are high and procedurally opaque. The Council also approved COPA companion land use items in Harlem and Bronx zoning amendments, appointed Marricka Scott-McFadden as Bronx County Democratic Commissioner of Elections, and passed Int 0296-2026-A requiring improved gender-based violence data collection and a public dashboard. CM Justin Sanchez abstained from tax exemption resolutions Res 0705 and Res 0706 covering a Bronx cluster including 917 Eagle Avenue, citing 123 recorded housing violations at that building alone and 524 violations across 14 district properties in the cluster, arguing tax exemptions should not flow to landlords failing to maintain habitable conditions.

▸ Action Points
  • Administration to establish a horse-drawn carriage industry transition plan by February 2027, covering workforce transition, horse disposition, and consideration of compensation for medallion owners, per Int 0943-2026-A.
  • Council to work with administration during the transition period to ensure carriage workers receive civil service training, career counseling, education assistance, and employment referrals, per Speaker Menin's commitment.
  • Mayor's office to designate agency or agencies to implement the horse-drawn carriage transition plan, per Int 0943-2026-A.
  • Administration to implement COPA (Int 0905-2026-A), covering the first-right-of-interest and first-right-to-purchase process for approximately 2,100 qualifying residential properties.
  • Department of Correction to create procedures for securing and checking early mail and absentee ballots for eligible voters in DOC custody before submission to the Board of Elections, per Int 0786-2026-A.
  • Department of Correction to report semi-annually on voter registration activities, forms submitted, ballots distributed, and demographic data for people in City jails, per Int 0797-2026-A.
  • Department of Education to conduct and report annually on how geography, household income, and language access affect access to special education programs and services, and to recommend remedies, per Int 0853-2026-A.
  • Mayor's Office to End Gender-Based Violence to develop a gender-based violence data report, outreach program for healthcare providers, and a public data dashboard, and to require agency reporting on screenings and referrals, per Int 0296-2026-A.
  • HPD and/or other relevant agencies to administer COPA's first-right-of-interest process for qualifying distressed and subsidized residential properties.
  • CM Justin Sanchez to monitor administration and landlord performance on the MHANY Cluster 2 tax exemption properties, and stated he will oppose future exemptions if habitability standards are not met.
  • Council to monitor administration's implementation of the horse-drawn carriage transition plan and hold the administration accountable on worker compensation and horse welfare commitments, per commitments from multiple members including CM Ossé and CM Brooks-Powers.
  • CM Santosuosso to circulate the Stop Super Speeders Bill (Int 1153-2026, requiring intelligent speed assistance devices for vehicles with 16 or more school zone speeding violations in 12 months) for colleague sign-on.
  • CM Hanif to circulate Int 1137-2026 establishing disability business incubator centers in every borough, administered by Small Business Services and the Mayor's Office for People with Disabilities, for colleague sign-on.
  • CM Brewer's Int 1132-2026 to require Landmarks Preservation Commission to create a public online records portal for 38,000 landmark properties referred to committee for further action.
  • Department of Health and Mental Hygiene to study gambling behaviors and the impact of casino openings, per Int 1134-2026, referred to committee.
  • Council to hold administration accountable on EMS funding and staffing adequacy, following Tuesday's oversight hearing on response times, per Speaker Menin's remarks.
  • Council Homeowner Stabilization Services Initiative to expand borough-by-borough Homeowner Expo tour following the inaugural event in Newark, as part of the $5.5 million initiative commitment.
