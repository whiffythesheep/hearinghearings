(00:09:54)

...homes, whether the result of a fire, flood or structural issues. During larger incidents like multi-alarm fires or large immediate sheltering of residents, this often involves opening a nearby public school or dispatching an M bus where residents can remain out of the elements and register for services with the Red Cross, including hotel placement.

Emergency Management uses triggers and protocols to respond to requests for sheltering assistance. These were developed to ensure we respond to incidents with the need for interagency coordination based on the likelihood of a multi-agency response and the complexity of these types of incidents. While the majority of fire calls are handled locally by FDNY resources, we respond to the following: one-alarm fires in a sensitive location such as schools, houses of worship or City properties; two-alarm fires in residential properties; three-alarm fires and above; any fire with two or more fatalities; any fire where we have been requested by FDNY, Buildings, NYPD or any other agency to provide interagency coordination at the scene. These fires usually involve requests for utility shutoffs or repairs, or overcrowding and squatting conditions.

Emergency Management citywide incident coordinators will also lead on-scene meetings with relevant agencies and landlords to identify immediately needed resources such as large-scale equipment or food and water, and any need for additional public messaging.

In addition to our City resources, the Red Cross monitors public safety communications and responds to all fires that reach one alarm and above on a daily basis. The Red Cross responds to vacate situations across the City to register New Yorkers and provide them with temporary hotel placement. Additionally, if Emergency Management responds to a fire that is likely to displace residents, we will notify the Red Cross to ensure they are also responding. Once registered with them, displaced residents are placed in a hotel for two nights. Residents are then referred to HPD for longer-term case management assistance if they qualify for the HPD shelter system.

Emergencies and disasters can strike at any moment, which is why Emergency Management is proud to offer several programs to help New Yorkers with readiness and resilience. The Strengthening Communities Program offers grants to community networks to build their emergency preparedness plan and provide information to their neighbors during times of emergency. These networks were activated during winter weather and extreme heat and play a significant role in flood preparedness. The Far Rockaway Arverne nonprofit coalition, or FRANC, has been part of Strengthening Communities since the program launched in 2020. FRANC provides various community programs and youth development programs within Far Rockaway and has connections with faith-based congregations throughout the community. We will have an open application in October for additional networks throughout the City and we will work with the Council to help amplify this opportunity when applications open.

Since its launch in 2003, New York City CERT has grown to 24 divisions covering all 59 community boards and it remains a critical resource in supporting New York City during and after disasters.
