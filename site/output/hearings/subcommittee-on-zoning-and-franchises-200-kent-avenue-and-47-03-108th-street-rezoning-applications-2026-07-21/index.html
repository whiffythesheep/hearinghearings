200 Kent Avenue and 47-03 108th Street Rezoning Applications

Subcommittee on Zoning and Franchises

Chair: Farah N. Louis
July 21, 2026 · 1hr 14m · Watch on councilnyc.viebit.com ↗ · View on council.nyc.gov ↗

Meeting overview

The Subcommittee on Zoning and Franchises held hearings on three land use applications: the Flatiron NoMad Major Concessions application in Manhattan (C 260123 MCM), the 47-03 108th Street mixed use residential rezoning in Corona, Queens (C 260147 ZMQ and N 260148 ZRQ), and the 200 Kent Avenue mixed use residential rezoning in Williamsburg, Brooklyn (C 260149 ZMK and N 260150 ZRK). A fourth item, known as St. Augustine, had been listed in the City Record but was not called up for Council review and was not heard. No votes were taken; all items were laid over following the hearings.

The Flatiron NoMad application, presented by the NYC Department of Transportation alongside the Flatiron NoMad Partnership BID, seeks a major concession to formalize and expand the BID's management authority over a significantly enlarged stretch of Broadway and Fifth Avenue between 19th and 31st Streets. The existing concession area of 38,500 square feet would grow to 162,345 square feet, triggering the major concession review threshold. DOT presented the application as part of the broader Broadway Vision initiative, which has pedestrianized several blocks and expanded cycling infrastructure. Questions from CMs Wilson and Epstein focused on environmental standards for concessionaires, public seating access and signage, the proposed 50 percent maximum concession seating allowance for major concessions, pedestrian-cycling separation in the capital redesign, Citi Bike station placement, and commitments from the Midtown South Mixed Use rezoning regarding a kid-first district and additional car-free blocks. DOT confirmed that drinking fountains and water bottle filling stations are being integrated into the capital design and that public restroom provision is under inter-agency discussion, without committing to a timeline. CM Felder used the hearing to register longstanding frustrations about unused Citi Bike docking stations citywide and the absence of a mandatory helmet requirement for cyclists using city streets, a question DOT diplomatically deflected. The concession structure would allow the BID to derive revenue from commercial events, sponsorships and sub-concessions including food kiosks and outdoor dining, with revenues reinvested into maintenance and operations. Financial reporting is required quarterly and annually under the concession agreement, though DOT confirmed this is not published online.

The 108th Street rezoning in Corona, Queens, presented by Ackerman on behalf of 108 Realty Group, proposes upzoning a single block front from R6B to R7X with a C2-4 commercial overlay to facilitate a 13-story, 119-unit mixed use building on a site currently occupied by non-conforming auto-related uses. The R7X designation was selected in preference to R7A, which was used for an approval three blocks south the prior week, on the basis of the site's transit orientation, wider street context and proximity to existing higher-density residential uses on the block front. The building would be all-electric and is proposed under MIH Option 1, yielding 30 permanently affordable units at a weighted average of 60 percent AMI with 10 percent at 40 percent AMI. CM Thomas-Henry pressed on unit mix, noting that the area has families and asking whether three-bedroom units and deeper affordability under MIH Option 3 had been considered; the applicant had not considered either but agreed to explore both. The community board was not in favor of the project, and one Council member noted that no direct neighborhood engagement had occurred outside the formal ULURP process, a notable gap the applicant acknowledged. The applicant confirmed willingness to accept conditions including 30 percent MWBE hiring with quarterly reporting, bioswales and rain gardens, and to continue discussions on community facility tenancy, where initial conversations have been held with CommonPoint. The height of the building, which would be roughly twice that of surrounding structures, remains an open negotiating point, with the applicant indicating design alternatives are being developed.

The 200 Kent Avenue rezoning in Williamsburg, Brooklyn, presented by Fried Frank on behalf of 206 Kent LLC and Kent Investor LLC, proposes converting a largely vacant commercial building with an active Trader Joe's ground floor into a 143-unit mixed use residential building through a partial conversion and a nine-story addition atop the existing five-story structure. The rezoning from M1-4 to M1-4A/R7X would establish Special Mixed Use District MX-8. Between 29 and 43 units would be affordable depending on the MIH option selected, with the applicant indicating openness to Option 1 following conversations with the Council Member. The existing building was structurally designed to support a future enlargement, which simplifies construction; Trader Joe's is expected to remain open throughout. Construction is targeted for 2027 commencement with a 12-to-15-month build timeline. The applicant confirmed commitments to reach out to Evergreen, North Brooklyn Business Exchange and Greenpoint Manufacturing and Design Center to identify light manufacturing tenants for commercial space, consistent with the MX-8 district's intended flexibility. No members of the public testified on either the Williamsburg or Queens applications.

▸ Action Points
  • DOT to provide CM Felder with data on Citi Bike dock utilisation rates and connect him with the relevant team.
  • DOT to connect CM Felder with helmet distribution programme team, though Felder made clear his interest is mandatory helmet requirements, not free helmet distribution.
  • DOT to confirm explicit environmental compliance requirements for the BID and its sub-concessionaires in the Flatiron NoMad concession agreement, including recycling and composting standards.
  • DOT to include in the concession agreement requirements for the BID to report to DOT on environmental practices.
  • DOT to ensure public seating availability and signage is actively monitored and that concession seating does not encroach on public seating areas.
  • DOT to integrate public drinking fountains and water bottle filling stations into the Flatiron NoMad capital design for the southern section (21st to 27th Street).
  • DOT to continue inter-agency discussions on public restroom provision within the Flatiron NoMad corridor.
  • DOT to present capital design details for the southern section (21st to 27th Street) to Community Board 5 later in fall 2026.
  • DOT to evaluate Citi Bike station placement between 29th and 30th Street as part of the broader capital design process for that section.
  • DOT to advance planning for the kid-first district between West 22nd and West 25th Street in coordination with BID partners.
  • DOT to bring proposals for additional car-free blocks on Broadway to the community at the capital redesign workshop stage.
  • 108 Realty Group applicant to explore inclusion of three-bedroom units in the 47-03 108th Street building programme.
  • 108 Realty Group applicant to consider MIH Option 3 for deeper affordability at 47-03 108th Street, notwithstanding counsel's note that Option 3 yields a lower affordable unit percentage than Option 1.
  • 108 Realty Group applicant to review and respond to Borough President recommendations for 47-03 108th Street.
  • 108 Realty Group applicant to accept condition requiring minimum 30 percent MWBE hiring with quarterly reporting to the Borough President's office and CM Thomas-Henry's office.
  • 108 Realty Group applicant to continue discussions with CM Thomas-Henry's office regarding appropriate community facility tenant, building on initial conversations with CommonPoint.
  • 108 Realty Group applicant to share design alternatives that reduce building height with CM Thomas-Henry.
  • 108 Realty Group and fellow developer (represented by the same counsel) to coordinate on community facility uses and retail mix across the two 108th Street corridor developments.
  • 200 Kent Avenue applicant to work with Trader Joe's to reconfigure bike parking within the Trader Joe's leased premises for improved safety and accessibility.
  • 200 Kent Avenue applicant to proactively reach out to Evergreen, North Brooklyn Business Exchange and Greenpoint Manufacturing and Design Center to identify light manufacturing tenants for commercial space.
  • 200 Kent Avenue applicant to finalise MIH option selection in consultation with the local Council Member's office, with Option 1 the preferred direction.
  • 200 Kent Avenue applicant to determine applicability of 485-X tax abatement programme given the partial conversion structure of the project.
