Subcommittee on Zoning and Franchises

Meeting overview

The Subcommittee on Zoning and Franchises held its October 6, 2026 meeting to vote on five rezoning proposals and hold hearings on three additional applications. The votes and hearings covered projects across Brooklyn, the Bronx, Queens and Staten Island, collectively representing hundreds of new apartments with significant affordable housing components. All five proposals passed, though not without pointed commentary from members about the shrinking institutional authority of local Council Members to shape land use decisions in their own districts — a theme that ran through virtually every item on the agenda.

The five approved proposals were: Fort Hamilton Mews (LU 0131-2026 / LU 0132-2026) in Bay Ridge, Brooklyn, modified from R7X to R7D to soften the transition to adjacent lower-density residential streets, with MIH Option One added; Park Avenue Brooklyn (LU 0133-2026 / LU 0134-2026), a ten-site mixed-use rezoning in Bed-Stuy converting manufacturing land, with MIH Option Two removed to ensure deeper affordability; 289 Kent Avenue (LU 0139-2026 / LU 0140-2026) in Williamsburg, converting M3-1 manufacturing land to mixed use, with Option Two removed and Option Three added for the same reason; Sojourner Truth-Mapes (LU 0135-2026 / LU 0136-2026) in the Bronx, a 100% affordable 457-unit development, which passed 8-1 with CM Sanchez dissenting; and 69-67 108th Street (LU 0137-2026 / LU 0138-2026) in Forest Hills, a 59-unit project modified to remove MIH Option Two and add Option Three. CM Farias flagged for the record that the community board and local union had unresolved concerns about the Sojourner Truth-Mapes project but deferred to the local member's prerogative. Chair Louis added a closing statement urging developer Fetner Houses to make a binding labor commitment before the full Council vote.

The hearings covered three applications. The 108-05 68th Road rezoning (T2026-2620 / T2026-2621) in Forest Hills proposes a seven-story building for All My Children Daycare and Nursery School, with 33 apartments (eight affordable), 11,000 square feet of community facility space for the nursery and a youth center, and 12 voluntary parking spaces. The community board voted 35-4 in favor. The proposal generated little controversy and the hearing closed without public opposition. The 100-12 Queens Boulevard proposal (T2026-2622 / T2026-2623) was the substantive centerpiece of the day's hearings. Benenson Capital Partners is seeking to rezone a site it has owned for 70 years from R7-1/C1 to R9X/C2 to develop a 21-storey, 389-unit mixed-use building with approximately 97 permanently affordable units at MIH Option One. The project drew seven in-person public witnesses. Residents of the adjacent six-storey Fontaine co-op testified about concerns over light, air and construction impacts, though the Fontaine's managing agent and working committee representative both indicated that meaningful progress had been made toward a written agreement with the developer and that the proposed massing under the rezoning was significantly more favorable to their building than what could be built as-of-right. SEIU 32BJ and the NYC District Council of Carpenters both testified in support, citing labor commitments from Benenson. CM Schulman noted outstanding concerns about density, business displacement and stormwater flooding, while making clear she supports affordable housing production. The South Richmond Designated Open Space Amendment (T2026-2624) was a narrow technical correction by the Department of City Planning to fix mapping errors introduced by the 2023 South Richmond Zoning Relief text amendment, affecting three specific Staten Island properties. DCP confirmed no increase in FAR, height, density or development rights would result. CM Morano engaged in detailed questioning on environmental protections, property tax implications and homeowner impacts, and was broadly supportive of the correction.

The meeting featured repeated and increasingly pointed references to Charter changes that have reduced local Council Member authority over land use in their districts, particularly for community districts placed on a so-called "fast track" list. Minority Leader Carr described this as "selective disenfranchisement" and suggested a legal challenge was likely. CM Schulman echoed the concern and noted it publicly for constituents. The theme is not new, but the frequency and candor with which it surfaced across multiple unrelated agenda items suggests it is becoming a live political and legal flashpoint.
