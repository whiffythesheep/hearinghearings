Child Care Program Background checks

Committee on Health | Committee on Oversight & Investigations | Subcommittee on Early Childhood Education

Chairs: Lynn C. Schulman, Shekar Krishnan, Jennifer Gutiérrez ·
Members (6) Joann Ariola, Harvey D. Epstein, Simcha Felder, James F. Gennaro, Christopher Marte, Mercedes Narcisse
April 22, 2026 · 2hrs 6m · Watch on YouTube ↗ · View on council.nyc.gov ↗

Summary

Meeting Overview

The Health Committee, jointly with the Subcommittee on Early Childhood Education and the Committee on Oversight and Investigations, held an oversight hearing on child care background checks in the context of the Mamdani administration's ambitious push toward universal child care, including the imminent launch of 2K for two-year-olds. The hearing examined how a system that was demonstrably broken as recently as 2023 has improved, what structural problems remain, and what legislation might help. It also heard bills on unrelated health topics: organ donation leave, vaccine insurance reimbursement, and drug manufacturing costs.

The administration sent Emmy Liss from the Mayor's Office of Child Care, digital strategist Arin Yarro, and DOH Deputy Commissioner Karen Schiff. Their testimony was broadly reassuring on process improvements: median background check processing time is now around 30 days against a 45-day federal mandate, down from a catastrophic backlog in 2019. Yarro demoed a new online permitting portal, launched in March 2026, that replaces a chaotic mix of emails, PDFs and in-person visits with milestone-based workflows, real-time scheduling, document upload, chat with assigned DOH staff, and a background check dashboard. The portal has 43 applicants so far and covers new center-based providers only, with renewals planned for a future release. Schiff confirmed DOH now has 60 staff dedicated to background checks, a private fingerprinting contractor (IdentoGO) with broad hours and locations replacing the old DOI-only model, and portability for educators changing employers within the field without needing a full new check, provided they have been continuously employed and cleared within the past five years.

Council Members pressed on several gaps. CM Gutierrez raised the persistent disconnect between the official 30-day median and the three-months-plus experiences providers are actually reporting to her office, the lack of coordination between city and state systems, and the resource demands of a significant expansion. Chair Schulman questioned the capacity to handle background check volume as 2K scales from 2,000 seats this fall to every two-year-old by end of term. CM Krishnan highlighted the cost burden of fingerprinting on workers. CM Brewer asked about demographic planning and whether the City is seriously trying to reverse family out-migration. The administration's answers were generally competent but somewhat circular on the demand question: they said they would grow staff as demand grows but had not set hard staffing targets or timelines.

On legislation, DOH essentially said Int 0015-2026, which would prohibit redundant background checks, reflects what they already do in practice, and they want to work with the Council to understand what problem it is actually solving. Int 0135-2026, requiring 24-hour posting of inspection reports and direct notification to parents on closures, was met with the department noting it already posts reports overnight and currently places the obligation to post closure orders on providers rather than directly notifying parents itself, though it expressed willingness to discuss. CM Morano presented Int 0831-2026 on organ and bone marrow donation leave, arguing it removes a practical financial barrier to life-saving donation and has been refined to address earlier collective bargaining concerns. Chair Schulman also used the hearing to highlight three resolutions on vaccine policy, making a pointed reference to the federal government withholding a COVID vaccine efficacy report and arguing New York must anchor its vaccine requirements to recognized scientific bodies rather than a potentially compromised ACIP under RFK Jr.

Numbers

  • In 2019, DOH's background check backlog stretched into the tens of thousands of applications.
  • In 2023, 83% of child care providers reported staffing vacancies.
  • Lightbridge Academy in Greenpoint had capacity for 170 children but closed in August 2023 because prospective staff had been waiting nearly five months for background clearance.
  • Under federal law, background checks must be processed within 45 days.
  • DOH now has a median background check processing time of approximately 30 days, down from crisis levels in 2019.
  • DOH's median processing time has been as low as 20 days during lower-intake periods.
  • Approximately 500 applications are currently beyond the 45-day mark at any given time.
  • DOH receives approximately 3,000 background check applications per month in typical months.
  • In September, DOH received 4,200 background check applications, reflecting seasonal peaks.
  • DOH has 60 staff members dedicated solely to background check processing.
  • The new child care permitting portal launched in March 2026 and has received 43 applications to date.
  • The federal comprehensive background check includes 8 components: FBI fingerprint check, national sex offender registry, three state checks (criminal history, sex offender, child abuse registries), and three interstate checks of the same registries in any prior state of residence over five years.
  • An educator changing employers within child care, with a cleared check within five years and no break in service exceeding 180 days, can move to a new employer without a full new background check.
  • The city is launching 2K for approximately 2,000 children this fall, scaling to approximately 12,000 in fall 2027, with universal coverage for all two-year-olds targeted by the end of the mayor's first term.
  • The governor committed over $1.2 billion in new funding on day eight of the Mamdani administration to expand child care access.
  • Families with children under six are twice as likely to leave New York City compared to the general population, driven largely by child care and housing costs.
  • Int 0831-2026 would provide up to 20 days of job-protected leave for living organ donation and 5 days for bone marrow donation.
  • The Mayor's Office of Child Care and Early Childhood Education currently has 7 staff members, up from 6 at the prior update.
  • Language line provides translation in approximately 200 languages for DOH background check inquiries.
  • New York City has approximately 10,800 licensed or registered child care programs subject to federal background check requirements.

Action Points

  • DOH to provide the Council with the current number of child care workers operating in provisional status, supervised pending full clearance.
  • DOH to provide data on background check processing times for state-regulated providers and pass the question to OCFS regarding any disparities with city-regulated providers.
  • DOH to work with OCFS to share data on background check processing timelines for home-based providers regulated at the state level.
  • DOH to add ombudsman-style contact information directly within the background check portal so providers can more easily escalate problems.
  • Mayor's Office of Child Care and DOH to hold provider office hours to gather structured feedback on the new permitting portal, and to consider a formal survey of the initial 43 portal users.
  • Mayor's Office of Child Care to develop outreach and training materials for existing center-based providers ahead of the portal's expansion to renewals and amendments.
  • Mayor's Office of Child Care and DOH to develop a plan to scale background check processing capacity in line with 2K and 3K expansion targets, with staffing ratios to be shared with the Council ahead of the executive budget hearing.
  • DOH to discuss with the Council the specific problem Int 0015-2026 is intended to solve, given the department's position that it already complies with its intent.
  • DOH to work with the Council on the best mechanism for the department to provide closure information directly to families under Int 0135-2026, rather than relying solely on providers to distribute it.
  • Mayor's Office of Child Care to engage New York State on building efficiencies into the OCFS background check system for home-based providers, using provider-reported pain points as the basis for that conversation.
  • DOH to share how many IdentoGO fingerprinting locations operate within New York City and provide that information to the committee.
  • Chair Schulman to raise background check processing capacity and staffing needs at the upcoming DOH executive budget hearing.
  • Mayor's Office of Child Care to work with City Planning and demographic analysts to formalize population projections for the under-six cohort and incorporate those into multi-year child care seat planning.
  • DOH to monitor the impact of the recently released portal update requiring double-entry of date of birth and social security number, and report back on whether it reduces typo-related delays.
