Racial Equity at CUNY

Committee on Higher Education

Chair: Rita C. Joseph
June 17, 2026 · 3hrs 19m · Watch on councilnyc.viebit.com ↗ · View on council.nyc.gov ↗

Summary

Meeting Overview

The Committee on Higher Education held an oversight hearing on racial equity at CUNY, receiving testimony from Alicia Albarran, Executive Vice Chancellor and University Provost, and Larry Johnson Jr., President of Bronx Community College. The hearing covered faculty and staff diversity, hiring and recruitment practices, retention, professional development, student support programs and leadership pipelines. No legislation was considered; this was a fact-finding and accountability exercise.

CUNY presented a broadly positive picture of its diversity record. Its student body is 78% students of color, and 45% of its nearly 19,000 faculty members are from minority groups, well above the national average of roughly 28%. Over the past academic year, 61% of new full-time faculty hires were from minority groups. Senior leadership has also diversified substantially since 2019, with 62% of the 26 college presidents and deans now from minority groups. On the student side, CUNY reported it has already met its 2030 goal of closing the racial gap in graduate school enrollment from CUNY baccalaureate programs, ahead of schedule.

Chair Joseph pressed consistently on the gap between student demographics and faculty demographics, particularly the underrepresentation of Black and Hispanic faculty relative to the student body, and the higher whiteness of part-time adjunct faculty and graduate-level students. She asked pointed questions about whether CUNY has specific numeric targets for diversity. The honest answer, given repeatedly and somewhat awkwardly, is that it does not: CUNY relies on broad recruitment, diverse search committees, Chief Diversity Officer sign-off and feedback loops rather than quotas or measurable demographic goals. A council member followed up on this directly, noting that without defined goals it is hard to assess whether anything is actually improving beyond good intentions and busy committees. The provost's responses on metrics were notably vague, though she cited the COACHE biennial faculty survey and exit surveys as key tools.

Several substantive program updates emerged. The SEEK program guidelines, which have not been updated in 30 years, are apparently finally on the verge of a revision, with SEEK directors having recently completed draft changes headed for board review. CUNY is rolling out COEUS, a new centralized grant management system, which for the first time will allow the system to identify faculty struggling with grant applications and connect them with more successful peers across campuses. A research program called AI Corps was highlighted as a successful pilot within SEEK focused on STEM research mentorship. BCC's president described an unusually comprehensive wraparound services model, including a food pantry serving 2,000 families a month and an early childhood center, reflecting the acute socioeconomic pressures on the Bronx student population. Chair Joseph closed by pushing CUNY on outreach to middle schools and guidance counselors, and on the stagnation of SEEK enrollment despite growing need, receiving commitments to more strategic K-12 partnership work and a five-year enrollment growth plan.

Action Points

  • CUNY to provide the Committee with a breakdown of faculty retention rates by race and ethnicity.
  • CUNY to provide the Committee with gender breakdown of Chancellor-level and senior leadership positions.
  • CUNY to provide the Committee with specific comparative data on racial representation among full-time versus part-time adjunct faculty.
  • CUNY to share the COACHE faculty survey instrument and any available published results with the Committee.
  • CUNY to share links to annual diversity plans currently posted on college websites with the Committee.
  • CUNY to provide the Committee with data on racial representation in the new AI faculty cluster hire once hiring is completed.
  • CUNY to provide the Committee with written responses to questions about the Honors College diversity recruitment strategy, to be answered by the relevant dean.
  • CUNY to share revised SEEK program guidelines with the Committee once finalized and submitted to the board.
  • CUNY administration to confirm to the Committee whether reporting systems are on track to comply with the new 2029 federal race and ethnicity reporting requirements, including the Middle Eastern or North African category.
  • CUNY to provide the Committee with the University Advisory Council on Diversity's meeting records and any recommendations or reports it has produced.
  • CUNY to expand leadership development pipeline programs modeled on the enrollment management initiative to student affairs and other professional tracks across the system.
  • BCC to continue strengthening K-12 outreach partnerships, including with middle schools and community-based organizations, as part of the enrollment growth strategy for SEEK and related programs.
  • CUNY central office to ensure SEEK enrollment strategy and five-year plan is formally shared with the Committee.
  • CUNY to use the new COEUS grant management system to identify and mentor faculty struggling with grant applications, particularly in STEM, and to report outcomes to the Committee.
  • CUNY to invite Chair Joseph to the AI Corps SEEK research showcase event.
