August 2026 911 System Outage

Committee on Fire and Emergency Management | Committee on Technology | Committee on Public Safety | Committee on Governmental Operations, State & Federal Legislation

Chairs: Joann Ariola, Carmen De La Rosa, Oswald J. Feliz, Gale A. Brewer
September 9, 2026 · 3hrs 11m · Watch on councilnyc.viebit.com ↗ · View on council.nyc.gov ↗

Meeting overview

Four Council committees held a joint oversight hearing on September 9, 2026, examining a seven-hour partial outage of New York City's 911 system that occurred on August 18, 2026, centered on the Bronx Public Safety Answering Center (PSAC 2). Chairs De La Rosa (Technology), Ariola (Fire and Emergency Management), Feliz (Public Safety), and Brewer (Governmental Operations) led questioning. Administration witnesses included OTI Commissioner and CTO Lisa Flores, along with deputies from OTI, NYPD, and FDNY.

The basic facts established by testimony: Motorola Solutions performed a firmware upgrade to firewalls at PSAC 2 between 9:45 PM on August 17 and 4:13 AM on August 18. The upgrade was classified as low risk and therefore did not require executive-level sign-off from the interagency Change Advisory Board. The problem began at 3:13 AM but was not surfaced by any automated monitoring system. A call taker noticed audio problems around 6:00 AM. The issue was not understood to be affecting 911 calls specifically until approximately 9:30 AM. Full remediation, achieved by rerouting all traffic through PSAC 1 in Brooklyn, was completed at 10:19 AM. Of 5,489 total calls during the outage window, 1,696 encountered problems; 742 of those were 911 calls; 319 came from unique phone numbers; 206 of those callers eventually reached an operator; 113 did not. The public was not notified during the outage. The existence of the outage became public through press reports.

The hearing's sharpest lines of questioning focused on three related failures: the failure of automated monitoring to detect the problem; the failure to reroute calls automatically to the redundant Brooklyn center; and the failure to notify the public. On monitoring, Flores acknowledged that no automated alarm caught the issue and that this needs to be fixed, but declined to give specifics citing security concerns. On redundancy, she and her colleagues explained that the cutover between PSACs is not automatic and that the intermittent nature of the failure (70% of calls were still getting through) complicated diagnosis. Council members, particularly Wong, were unpersuaded by this explanation, arguing that any degradation in a life-safety system should trigger an immediate failover. On public notification, the administration's position — that there was only a 45-minute window between knowing 911 calls were affected and restoring service, and that notifying the public could have deterred people from calling — was repeatedly and forcefully rejected by nearly every council member present. CM Feliz drew the key distinction: failing to notify the public is not a technical glitch, it is a human choice. CM Ariola pointed to Notify NYC as an existing tool that could have been used instantly. The administration acknowledged the criticism and committed to reviewing public notification protocols, but offered no timeline.

The hearing also surfaced a broader governance problem: multiple agencies share responsibility for PSAC operations, and no single named official is accountable when the system fails. Council Member Morano asked pointedly who owns the system when it breaks. He received no clean answer. The question of Motorola's liability went similarly unresolved; the administration repeatedly said it would be addressed after the root cause analysis is complete. Morano also raised the uncomfortable point that Motorola is effectively grading its own homework in that analysis. Next Generation 911, originally due in 2018 and since delayed repeatedly, is now scheduled for 2027; Flores said she intends to scrutinize that timeline carefully. On staffing, NYPD reported meaningful improvement in 911 operator headcount, with a target of largely voluntary overtime within three to six months. The sole public witnesses were an advocate for making 911 operationally independent of NYPD to improve crisis response routing, and two individuals testifying on unrelated matters concerning directed-energy weapons who appeared to have wandered somewhat off-topic.

▸ Numbers
  • The 911 system receives over 9 million calls per year.
  • Total typical daily call volume across both PSACs is approximately 25,000 calls.
  • The outage window ran from 3:13 AM to 10:19 AM on August 18, 2026, approximately seven hours.
  • Of 5,489 total calls to emergency services during the outage window, approximately 3,800 (70%) were handled successfully without issue.
  • 1,696 calls encountered problems during the outage.
  • 742 of the affected calls were 911 calls from members of the public.
  • 319 unique phone numbers were associated with those 742 affected 911 calls.
  • 206 of the 319 unique callers eventually connected with an operator.
  • 113 unique callers never reached emergency services.
  • NYPD made 526 callback calls in the 24 hours following the outage using numbers supplied by the vendor.
  • NYPD made voice contact with 32 individuals in that 24-hour callback effort.
  • A separate text-to-911 outage earlier in 2026 lasted approximately 110 minutes and affected 12 callers.
  • The firmware change process began at 9:45 PM on August 17 and was completed at 4:13 AM on August 18.
  • The first call taker notification of audio problems occurred shortly before 6:00 AM; the first support ticket was opened at 6:40 AM.
  • The operational bridge call involving all agencies and vendors began at 8:54 AM.
  • NYPD currently employs 1,370 911 operators, up from 1,250 the previous year, a 10% increase.
  • Supervisory and principal headcount rose from 146 to 625 year over year.
  • A class of 80 new operators is currently in training; a further 100 hires are planned for October.
  • Year-to-date overtime for 911 operators totals 330,000 hours, equivalent to approximately 32 hours per person per month.
  • In calendar year 2025, 2,800 system changes were executed without service impact.
  • Motorola's contract value has grown from approximately $20 million to around $80 million.
  • Next Generation 911 implementation began in 2020 and is now scheduled for completion in 2027, having previously been expected to complete in summer 2026 and originally targeted for 2018.
  • The FY27 budget includes six staff lines for the newly established Office of Algorithmic Accountability.
  • From the start of the outage at 3:06 AM, 322 calls with one-way audio were presented to 83 unique call takers before the problem was internally flagged.
▸ Action Points
  • OTI to complete root cause analysis covering technical architecture, testing protocols, monitoring systems, incident response decisions, vendor performance, communications, and lines of accountability.
  • OTI to develop and publish a formal public notification policy specifying criteria, timing, mechanism, and responsible party for notifying New Yorkers during 911 outages.
  • OTI to work with NYC Emergency Management to define what a Notify NYC alert for a 911 outage would look like and when it would be triggered.
  • OTI to review and revise the risk classification criteria used by Motorola and the Change Advisory Board, including whether any change to a life-safety system can appropriately be classified as low risk.
  • OTI to assess whether automated monitoring can be upgraded to detect call audio failures and trigger alerts before the problem reaches call takers.
  • OTI to assess whether automatic failover between PSAC 1 and PSAC 2 is technically feasible and operationally appropriate.
  • OTI to conduct end-to-end testing protocols for future system changes, covering the full call lifecycle from origination through recording.
  • OTI to identify whether Motorola has any financial liability under its contract for the August 18 outage, including review of SLA terms, indemnification provisions, and warranties.
  • OTI to clarify the single named City official accountable for the integrity of the 911 technology system.
  • OTI to review governance structure of the PSAC Steering Committee, including whether the number of agencies involved impedes clear accountability.
  • OTI to provide the Council with a timeline and action plan upon completion of the root cause analysis, with specific deadlines and measurable milestones.
  • OTI to follow up with the Council on outstanding contract details related to Motorola liability and warranty provisions.
  • OTI to respond to the Technology Committee's April 28 letter on data privacy, facial recognition, and biometric data governance.
  • OTI to clarify responsibility for notifying NICE (emergency communications) during outages and build that into formal protocol.
  • OTI to investigate whether any of the 113 callers who never reached emergency services suffered harm and to report findings to the Council.
  • OTI to examine whether 988 calls that escalated to 911 were affected by the outage and report findings.
  • OTI to look into what other US cities are doing for 911 redundancy, backup routing, and crisis call handling, and report back to the Council.
  • OTI to review the Next Generation 911 schedule, assess reliability of the 2027 completion date, and report to the Council.
  • NYPD to report back to the Council on the nature of the 113 unserviced emergency calls as analysis is completed.
  • NYPD to honor the call taker who first flagged the audio problem with a commander's day.
  • NYPD to provide Council Members with advance notification protocols when future 911 incidents occur, so members can assist constituents.
  • OTI to have the Office of Algorithmic Accountability fully staffed and operational by end of 2026.
  • Chairs to send a joint committee letter to the administration formalizing outstanding questions not answered at the hearing.
