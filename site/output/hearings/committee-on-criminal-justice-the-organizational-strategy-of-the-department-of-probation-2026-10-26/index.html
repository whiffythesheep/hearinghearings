The Organizational Strategy of the Department of Probation

Committee on Criminal Justice

Chair: Selvena N. Brooks-Powers
October 26, 2026 · 2hrs 17m · Watch on councilnyc.viebit.com ↗ · View on council.nyc.gov ↗

Meeting overview

The Committee on Criminal Justice held an oversight hearing on the organizational strategy of the Department of Probation, with Commissioner Sharon Goodwin and her senior leadership team providing testimony. The Committee also took up Int 1104-2026, a bill sponsored by Chair Brooks-Powers that would require the Department to provide youth accused of crimes with written notices explaining the criminal legal process.

Commissioner Goodwin, a forty-year veteran of the agency who has held nearly every position in the probation officer title series, framed the Department's strategy around the phrase "what works" and evidence-based practice. She outlined three priorities since her appointment: reducing a vacancy rate that had been the highest of any city agency at 23 percent; right-sizing caseloads according to risk level rather than assigning officers a mixed portfolio of low, medium, and high-risk clients indiscriminately; and rebuilding relationships with Family Court judges, community-based organizations, and neighborhood stakeholder groups. The reinstatement of the Probation Officer Trainee title, which allows the Department to hire college graduates without yet having casework experience, has been a central recruitment tool. The training academy has been shortened from approximately five months to 3.5 months while maintaining state compliance.

Committee members pressed on several substantive concerns. CM Morano raised the question of whether high-risk individuals had received insufficient supervision under the previous mixed-caseload system, and whether this correlated with a reported 50 percent increase in juvenile rearrests. The Department acknowledged the caseload problem had persisted for roughly three years but said it was unaware of specific violent crimes attributable to it; the Deputy Commissioner for Juvenile Operations noted that rearrest rates have begun declining since the new Commissioner's arrival. The adjustment and diversion rate for juvenile cases drew pointed scrutiny: of approximately 9,000 cases that came through intake in fiscal year 2026, only around 1,400 were adjusted, implying a rate well below the historical high of 41 percent. The Deputy Commissioner for Juvenile Operations acknowledged this was not where the Department wanted to be and committed to a target adjustment rate of 20 percent for the coming year, with retraining of all juvenile operations staff planned. Early termination of probation also came up, with the Chair noting that courts are more likely to grant motions when the Department itself files them. The Commissioner confirmed this practice has been revived and that there is no blanket objection from the Office of General Counsel to early discharge motions, contrary to what apparently occurred under the previous administration.

On Int 1104-2026, the Department expressed general support, noting that providing youth and families with a notice of rights and information about the criminal legal process already largely matches current intake practice in Family Court. However, the Department flagged some technical concerns around alignment with the Family Court Act and indicated it would work with the Council to refine the bill's details. The hearing also touched on data infrastructure: the Department is working with CUNY's Institute for State and Local Governance to develop a metrics framework for evaluating community-based programs, and is building an internal analytics function to improve data-driven decision-making. These efforts are still in early stages, which left several committee questions about program effectiveness and rearrest outcomes unanswered with anything more than anecdote.
