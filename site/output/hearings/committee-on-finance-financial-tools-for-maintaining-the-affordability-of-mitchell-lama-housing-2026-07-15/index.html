Financial Tools for Maintaining the Affordability of Mitchell-Lama Housing

Committee on Finance | Committee on Housing and Buildings

Chairs: Linda Lee, Pierina Ana Sanchez
July 15, 2026 · 3hrs 22m · Watch on councilnyc.viebit.com ↗ · View on council.nyc.gov ↗

Summary

Meeting Overview

The Finance Committee, chaired by Linda Lee and held jointly with the Housing and Buildings Committee chaired by Pierina Ana Sanchez, convened an oversight hearing on financial tools for maintaining the affordability of Mitchell-Lama housing, alongside consideration of Res 0083-2026, which calls on the State Legislature to pass A.6432/S.4236 strengthening protections for Mitchell-Lama residents. Speaker Julie Menin and roughly a dozen council members participated. The immediate catalyst was a proposed cumulative 30% rent increase at Tracy Towers in the Bronx, but the hearing ranged across the entire City-supervised Mitchell-Lama portfolio of roughly 90,000 apartments in 134 developments.

HPD First Deputy Commissioner Adam Phillips led the agency testimony, joined by colleagues from housing supervision and asset management. HPD's core argument was structural: Mitchell-Lama rents and carrying charges are not set by the Rent Guidelines Board but by what is needed to balance each development's budget; state law requires HPD to approve increases sufficient to cover costs; decades of deferred maintenance combined with skyrocketing insurance, labor and utility costs have produced a wave of large, infrequent increases rather than steady annual ones; and the portfolio faces billions in capital needs over the next decade. Phillips acknowledged Tracy Towers is not exceptional — 37 developments saw increases averaging roughly 26-29% in 2025 alone, and five more have already filed for increases in 2026. HPD's stated toolkit includes the recently reduced shelter rent tax (from 10% to 5% of shelter rent), SCRIE enrollment (about 6,000 households currently enrolled, with roughly 5,500 Section 8 vouchers also in the portfolio), proposed J-51 reauthorization, $1.32 billion in total preservation budget for FY2027, and exploratory land sales on underutilized Mitchell-Lama parcels to generate capital. HPD also confirmed Tracy Towers has now closed on phase one of its construction financing.

Council members across the board expressed frustration with what they characterised as a reactive rather than proactive oversight model. The recurring theme was that gradual annual increases of 1-2% would be far less disruptive than decade-long freezes followed by 30-50% jumps, and that HPD should be pushing boards toward regular increases rather than waiting for a crisis. CM Restler pressed hard on Tracy Towers specifically, noting a nearly 50% jump in security costs in two years that he said should have been flagged in routine financial reviews, and questioned whether HPD had ever actually rejected or meaningfully modified a proposed increase. CM Brewer raised the specific cases of Clinton Towers and 110 West End Avenue and proposed a roundtable involving city, state and council members with Mitchell-Lama portfolios. CM Marte questioned why city capital is structured as loans rather than grants, asked about getting shelter rent tax to zero, and pushed for forgivable loan structures. CM Feliz asked about cross-portfolio accountability for management companies with patterns of neglect. CM Avilés noted the resolution being heard calls for independent state oversight. A recurring concern across members was that construction funded years ago — including a 2016 loan for Tracy Towers elevators — has not produced lasting improvements, and that monthly requisition sign-offs may not ensure quality or durability of completed work.

The hearing produced no formal votes or enacted legislation, but several substantive commitments were extracted from HPD, including provision of comprehensive rent-increase histories, per-development capital needs data, violation profiles, and the list of developments with pending increase applications. There was broad consensus that a roundtable convening city and state agencies, council members and advocates is needed. The hearing also clarified several structural issues that have policy implications: the statutory bar on income-tiered surcharges within Mitchell-Lama means cross-subsidisation is unavailable; SCRIE income eligibility was raised at state level to $75,000 but requires parallel local action; Article 5 co-ops like Penn South are eligible for city capital preservation financing but apparently did not know it; and 65 developments citywide have already left the Mitchell-Lama program, with exits triggered when balloon loans come due.

Numbers

  • Approximately 90,000 Mitchell-Lama apartments across 134 developments in New York City, roughly half supervised by the city through HPD and half by the state.
  • Two thirds of the city Mitchell-Lama portfolio are co-ops; one third are rentals.
  • 269 Mitchell-Lama developments containing more than 105,000 apartments have been built statewide since the program began in 1955.
  • 65 Mitchell-Lama developments have left the program citywide since 1955.
  • 37 Mitchell-Lama developments saw rent or carrying charge increases in 2025, averaging approximately 26-29% over multiple years.
  • 25 of those 37 developments involved a cumulative increase of 20% or greater; 7 involved a 20% or greater increase in 2025 alone.
  • 5 Mitchell-Lama developments have filed for and received rate increases so far in 2026.
  • Developments with pending increase applications read into the record include: Washington Square Southeast (15/10/5%), Sam Burt (15/7/7%), Trinity House (7/7/7%), First Atlantic Terminal (2.5/2.5%), Jefferson Towers (10/6%), Tracy Towers (approximately 30%), Masaryk Towers (increase filed), Rupert House (15/10/5%), and Lincoln Amsterdam (30/10%, no increase in 25 years).
  • The State Comptroller's March 2026 audit found hazardous or unsafe conditions at 12 of 15 Mitchell-Lama developments examined statewide, including all 10 New York City developments sampled, and identified approximately $2.3 million in misspent or inadequately documented funds.
  • HPD's total preservation budget for FY2027 is $1.32 billion, of which Mitchell-Lama developments will receive a share; HPD expects to preserve approximately 2,800 Mitchell-Lama units in FY2027 and FY2028 combined.
  • The city's five-year capital commitment plan for preservation totals $4.9 billion, with a $500 million increase projected for FY2031; Mitchell-Lama share is not broken out separately.
  • The FY2025 New York State budget reduced the shelter rent tax cap from 10% to 5% of annual shelter rent for Mitchell-Lama developments; every city-supervised Mitchell-Lama is now paying at the 5% rate.
  • Approximately $47.5 million in property tax and municipal charge arrears are outstanding across city-supervised Mitchell-Lama developments.
  • The city-supervised Mitchell-Lama portfolio has approximately 6,000 households enrolled in SCRIE and 5,500 Section 8 vouchers, together covering a significant share of the roughly 8,000 households the Department of Finance estimates are income-eligible for SCRIE.
  • Citywide, approximately 158,214 households are estimated to be eligible for SCRIE or DRIE; only 67,132 received the benefit in 2023.
  • SCRIE income eligibility was raised at state level from $50,000 to $75,000; local legislative action is still required to implement the higher threshold in city-supervised developments.
  • 12 Mitchell-Lama developments are currently in HPD's capital pipeline preparing for construction loan closing.
  • 48 city-supervised Mitchell-Lama developments have completed an Integrated Physical Needs Assessment since 2020; 6 more are currently in the process.
  • HPD has conducted 25 SCRIE outreach workshops over the past year across the Mitchell-Lama portfolio.
  • Tracy Towers security costs rose from approximately $1.8 million in FY2025 to $2.8 million projected for FY2027, a roughly 50% increase representing approximately $267 per unit per month.
  • Tracy Towers phase one construction financing closed in late June 2026; phase two is expected to close by end of calendar year 2027, with a scope including elevator replacement, facade work, plaza repairs, security upgrades and playground improvements financed by approximately $40 million in city capital loans with no principal or interest payments until balloon maturity.
  • A 2016 loan for Tracy Towers of approximately $40 million covered elevator upgrades, boiler, roof and other repairs; residents report elevators remain unreliable and the roof continues to leak.
  • Kingsbridge Arms has over $1 million in debt and relies on additional borrowing to cover basic operating expenses.
  • Mitchell-Lamas are exempt from Local Law 97 carbon emissions penalties until 2035.
  • Penn South in CM Epstein's district comprises nearly 3,000 units and is an Article 5 co-op previously believed by its board to be ineligible for city capital preservation financing.

Action Points

  • HPD to provide the Finance and Housing committees with a comprehensive list of all city-supervised Mitchell-Lama developments and their rent and carrying charge increases over the past 10 years, plus all pending proposed increases, within days of the hearing.
  • HPD to provide both committees with a development-by-development assessment of the financial condition of all city-supervised Mitchell-Lama developments, including which are in financial stress and which face significant increases within the next five years.
  • HPD to provide both committees with a breakdown of open violations across the Mitchell-Lama portfolio.
  • HPD to provide both committees with a per-development capital needs assessment for all city-supervised Mitchell-Lama developments, including cost estimates where available.
  • HPD to share its formal response to the State Comptroller's March 2026 audit with relevant council members, particularly those with affected developments in their districts.
  • HPD to provide a breakdown of city capital versus private capital (including HDC first mortgage) for Tracy Towers and, where available, other Mitchell-Lama developments, to clarify what portion of rent increases is attributable to debt service.
  • HPD to provide monthly construction requisition and sign-off documents from the 2016 Tracy Towers loan to council members, in coordination with HDC, to allow oversight of whether contracted work was completed to standard.
  • HPD to bring the Tracy Towers property management firm in for a detailed review of operating costs, including the security cost increase, with a view to identifying reductions before the proposed rent increase takes effect.
  • HPD to investigate and report to CM Feliz on the two South Bronx Mitchell-Lama developments (Men's Plaza and adjacent property) managed by the same company showing patterns of neglect, including convening a meeting with the management firm.
  • HPD to work with both committee chairs to enact locally the increase in the SCRIE income eligibility threshold from $50,000 to $75,000, consistent with the state-level change.
  • HPD to provide both committees with a projection of how many additional Mitchell-Lama households would become SCRIE-eligible under the $75,000 income threshold.
  • HPD to provide a roll-up figure for the total capital cost of needs assessments completed across the 48 city-supervised Mitchell-Lama developments assessed since 2020.
  • HPD to investigate and report on the status of project manager assignment for Jamie Towers, which has $8 million in allocated city and state capital but has not advanced through three consecutive closing cycles.
  • HPD to look into the 1950 Hutchinson Parkway Apartments project timeline and confirm its position in the capital pipeline to CM Restler.
  • HPD to provide council members with referrals to nonprofit legal service providers that can assist Mitchell-Lama co-op boards dealing with shareholder arrears and related governance challenges.
  • HPD to take a more proactive approach to recommending gradual annual rent and carrying charge increases to Mitchell-Lama boards before financial distress develops, including outreach to council members as partners in encouraging boards to act.
  • HPD to host a roundtable convening city and state agencies, council members and relevant advocates, including limited-equity co-ops such as Amalgamated, to develop a coordinated Mitchell-Lama 2.0 strategy; HPD committed to provide coffee.
  • HPD to confirm to CM Epstein that Penn South and other Article 5 co-ops are eligible for city capital preservation financing and to engage with Penn South on a loan application.
  • HPD to provide both committees with the Local Law 97 penalty schedule applicable to Mitchell-Lama developments after the 2035 exemption expires.
  • HPD to clarify whether any Mitchell-Lama development will pay less than 5% shelter rent tax based on prior Board of Estimate resolutions, including a comparison of exemption values against the cap for each development.
  • Council to pursue local legislation to implement the SCRIE income threshold increase from $50,000 to $75,000, as requested by HPD.
