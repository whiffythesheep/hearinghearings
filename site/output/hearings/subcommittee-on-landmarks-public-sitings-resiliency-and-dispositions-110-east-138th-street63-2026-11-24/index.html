110 East 138th Street/63 Exterior Street Land Use Applications

Subcommittee on Landmarks, Public Sitings, Resiliency and Dispositions

Chair: Christopher Marte
November 24, 2026 · 1hr 5m · Watch on councilnyc.viebit.com ↗ · View on council.nyc.gov ↗

Meeting overview

The Subcommittee on Landmarks, Public Sitings, Resiliency and Dispositions, chaired by CM Christopher Marte, held hearings on three distinct proposals: a major affordable housing development on the South Bronx waterfront, and two school siting applications in Long Island City, Queens. Items were laid over after testimony; no votes were taken.

The Bronx waterfront items (LU 0161 through LU 0163) concern a proposal by Phipps Houses and Douglas Development to build two mixed-use buildings at 110 East 138th Street and 63 Exterior Street in the Mott Haven/Port Morris waterfront area, known as Madison Landing. The project requires a special permit to modify height and bulk rules, an Urban Development Action Area designation and disposition of city-owned land, and a waterfront public access authorization. The local Council Member offered strong support, noting the project is 100% affordable in a waterfront zone where previous developments used no affordability subsidies. The development team presented a detailed plan including 537 income-restricted units, Passive House design, geothermal and solar energy, 25,000 square feet of shore public walkway, a dog run, a senior shuttle service, and social services space. Rents will be capped at 80% AMI with average incomes below 50% AMI. Subcommittee members pressed on the studio-heavy unit mix in the senior building, the enforceability of waterfront access commitments, the frequency of the proposed senior shuttle, and the retail rent structure. One public witness raised concerns about language in the waterfront permit application referencing market-rate housing, Phipps Houses' management record at Lambert Houses including a federal audit request, prior misuse of HDFC funds, and unresolved environmental contamination questions. The witness called for a legally binding permanent affordability agreement and a community oversight committee.

The two school siting applications (LU 0164 and LU 0165) cover sites in Long Island City: a 972-seat intermediate school at 43-58 11th Street for Community School District 30 (the Schuman site), and a 676-seat high school with a District 75 program at 47-51 33rd Street for Community School District 24. Both schools are anticipated to open in 2031. The high school presentation was largely procedural, with the local Council Member securing on-record confirmation that the facility will serve grades 7 to 12 permanently and demanding a detailed construction timeline given a history of broken promises to constituents. The more contentious matter was the intermediate school site. The local Council Member revealed that the Schuman site had been publicly committed as an elementary school under the OneNYC planning process as far back as 2020, and that the SCA changed its designation to a middle school only in the last few months, without notifying PTAs, the two affected elementary schools, or the existing middle school. The SCA's explanation was that physical constraints at the competing Plaques site made a full-size gym impossible for a middle school, so the uses were swapped, allowing the Plaques elementary school to grow to approximately 864 seats. The Council Member was visibly and vocally furious, pointing out that Long Island City has a shortage of over 300 kindergarten seats right now, that apartments in the area are built in three years not ten as planners claim, and that the city's plan to wait until new housing is fully built by 2035 before siting additional schools is both factually wrong and a betrayal of commitments made to residents who have absorbed an enormous share of the city's new housing construction. The Council Member put on record a demand for a school at 44-59 45th Avenue and warned the administration that community members are paying close attention.

▸ Numbers
  • 537 total income-restricted housing units proposed across two buildings at 110 East 138th Street/63 Exterior Street.
  • 183 units designated for affordable senior housing in the north building.
  • 350 units of affordable family housing in the south building.
  • Maximum rents set at 80% of Area Median Income; average incomes expected to be below 50% AMI.
  • A one-bedroom unit at 50% AMI will cost $1,369 per month in rent under 2026 AMI levels.
  • Over 25,000 square feet of shore public walkway space to be created at the Bronx waterfront site.
  • An additional 8,000 square feet of public open space included at the Bronx site.
  • Buildings raised to 15.4 feet above base flood elevation, more than four feet above the 100-year flood elevation requirement of 11 feet.
  • Approximately 2,000 square feet of retail space included in the south building, intended for a daily goods retailer.
  • Phipps Houses received a management score of 59 out of 100 at Lambert Houses, triggering a request for a federal audit.
  • Lambert Houses used more than $83,000 of its own funds to cover expenses for a Phipps-associated company without HDFC approval, per a 2024 audit.
  • Proposed intermediate school at 43-58 11th Street: approximately 972 seats, serving grades 6 to 8, site approximately 47,255 square feet (1.0 acres), anticipated opening 2031.
  • Proposed high school at 47-51 33rd Street: approximately 676 seats, serving grades 9 to 12 with District 75 program, site approximately 30,000 square feet (0.84 acres), anticipated opening 2031.
  • Intermediate school will include 30 general classrooms, 4 special education classrooms, 8 District 75 classrooms, and an outdoor play yard of approximately 14,000 square feet.
  • High school will include 14 general classrooms, 8 District 75 classrooms, a theater, and acting rehearsal space.
  • Design process for each school is approximately 13 months; bidding approximately 3 to 4 months; demolition to begin during design phase.
  • District 30 in Long Island City has a current shortage of over 300 kindergarten seats.
  • The city committed to providing 1,300 new elementary school seats in Long Island City; the reclassification of the Schuman site to a middle school leaves roughly half that commitment unmet.
  • Long Island City is projected to generate over 14,000 new housing units; city planners project full build-out by 2035.
  • The Plaques elementary school site was originally planned for 600 seats; following the swap it is now planned for approximately 864 seats.
  • LIC has built more apartments in the last decade than the City of Los Angeles, per the local Council Member.
▸ Action Points
  • Phipps Houses and HPD to provide legally binding documentation confirming that all 537 units remain permanently affordable even if the property changes ownership, as requested by the Bronx Borough President.
  • HPD and Phipps Houses to establish and maintain a Community Advisory Board throughout construction and into building management.
  • Development team to continue coordinating with NYC DOT and NYS DOT throughout the construction phase on pedestrian signal timing, sidewalk widening, and bike infrastructure along Exterior Street and East 138th Street.
  • Development team to finalise and publish the senior shuttle route schedule and operating hours closer to building opening.
  • Phipps Houses to give serious consideration to below-market retail rents for the 2,000-square-foot commercial space, prioritising local small businesses, as requested by CM Thomas-Henry.
  • HPD to investigate the status of the federal audit of Phipps Houses' management at Lambert Houses and report findings to the subcommittee.
  • HPD to confirm that Madison Landing's environmental remediation plans will be reviewed and approved by the Department of Health and the Department of Environmental Conservation before the Council takes a final vote.
  • HPD to consider establishing a community and tenant oversight committee for the Madison Landing project, as requested by the public witness.
  • SCA to provide the local Council Member's office with the exact month the decision was made to change the 11th Street Schuman site from an elementary school to a middle school.
  • SCA to submit a detailed construction timeline for both the 11th Street and 33rd Street school projects to the local Council Member, covering design authorization, bidding, demolition, construction start, and projected opening dates.
  • SCA to provide a written response on how the remaining elementary school seat shortfall in District 30 will be addressed, including the status of the DOT site and the Newtown Basin site.
  • SCA and DOE to engage DOT on a timeline for DOT to vacate and transfer the site at 44-59 45th Avenue for school development.
  • Local Council Member's office to hold a quarterly community oversight town hall with SCA, DOE, DOT, and other relevant agencies on school seat delivery commitments in Long Island City.
  • SCA to notify PTAs of PS 78, PS 384, and Hunters Point Middle School formally about the change in planned use at the Schuman site and to engage them in the design and programming process going forward.
