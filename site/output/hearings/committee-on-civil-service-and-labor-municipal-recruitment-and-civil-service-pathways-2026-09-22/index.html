Municipal Recruitment and Civil Service Pathways

Committee on Civil Service and Labor

Chair: Shirley Aldebol
September 22, 2026 · 1hr 59m · Watch on councilnyc.viebit.com ↗ · View on council.nyc.gov ↗

Meeting overview

The Council's Committee on Civil Service and Labor, chaired by CM Shirley Aldebol, held an oversight hearing on municipal recruitment and civil service pathways. The primary witness was Katrina Porter, Deputy Commissioner and Chief Human Capital Officer at DCAS, accompanied by a colleague identified as Katie, Director of Workforce Operations. The hearing examined the full range of DCAS recruitment programs — the Civil Service Pathways Fellowship, Public Service Corps, Summer Internship Program, Urban Fellows Program, and the 55-A disability hiring program — alongside civil service exam administration, outreach efforts, and the persistent vacancy problem across City agencies.

The core tension throughout was familiar to anyone who follows NYC government: the City is making genuine incremental progress on a system that arguably needs structural overhaul. DCAS presented a reasonably impressive array of programs and metrics — doubling the Pathways Fellowship to 400 fellows, free exams for first-time takers and high schoolers under Local Law 57 of 2025, exam notices now translatable into any language, social media engagement up dramatically, 308 outreach events in FY2025, and median exam-to-list time down to 101 days from 158. Council members probed the underlying weaknesses: a 5% citywide vacancy rate with some agencies like the Human Rights Commission at nearly 20%; hiring timelines that can exceed a year; non-competitive salaries; agencies operating in silos with no centralized accountability for end-to-end hiring; and a near-total inability to connect recruitment inputs (social media impressions, outreach events) to hiring outputs. CM Morano pointedly noted that 400,000 social media impressions mean nothing if you cannot show how many people got jobs. CM Restler pressed for a global accountability framework measuring the entire lifecycle from exam posting to hire, and for salary reviews focused on hard-to-recruit technical positions. CM Hudson raised the concrete consequences of vacancies, citing the Legionnaires' disease outbreak and DOB inspector shortages, and got an honest answer from DCAS: they would not know whether DOHMH had requested emergency hiring support because agency-level hiring is decentralized and largely invisible to DCAS unless agencies ask for help.

Four public witnesses from Citizens, a former DCAS internal reviewer, Abundance New York, and Citizens Union gave the most substantive testimony of the hearing. Their collective message was that the City is tinkering at the margins of a Byzantine system that excludes qualified candidates not through malice but through opacity, jargon, and structural delay. A former DOEP hiring manager described reverse-engineering civil service rules on the job with no training, sitting through the rule-of-three process to staff a team serving homeless families. A former DCAS employee who led a 2019 citywide hiring assessment — work that was apparently never made public — noted that most City employees who took a civil service exam before being hired already had a friend or family member who worked for the City, which is a structural equity failure hiding in plain sight. The public witnesses collectively pushed for: plain language standards across all civil service materials; banded scoring to replace the rule of three; removing exam requirements for licensed roles where state licensing already functions as a merit filter; publishing agency-level time-to-hire data; better candidate tracking mechanisms across agencies; mandatory civil service training for all hiring managers; and a mayoral-term workforce planning process analogous to capital planning. The chair agreed with most of this and noted she had been opposed to the rule of one-in-three for some time, setting up what may be the committee's legislative agenda going forward.

The hearing revealed a governance gap that runs through all of this: DCAS can administer exams and run fellowship programs, but it cannot compel agencies to hire, cannot see what happens after a list is certified, cannot track youth apprenticeship data because another office runs that program, and cannot fix salary non-competitiveness because that is OMB's domain. CM Restler made the pointed comparison to the City's centralized response to sexual harassment reporting — when City Hall mandated data sharing, it worked — and argued that the same political will is needed here. That argument has been made before about many NYC government coordination failures, but it is the right one.

▸ Numbers
  • Citywide municipal vacancy rate: approximately 5% overall.
  • Department of Social Services vacancy rate: approximately 15%.
  • Human Rights Commission vacancy rate: approximately 20%.
  • DCAS civil service exams administered per year: approximately 200 on average.
  • Civil Service Pathways Fellowship expanded to 400 fellows in August 2026, up from 200 previously.
  • Approximately 75-76% of Civil Service Pathways Fellowship completers transition to full-time City employment.
  • Of those who transition from the Fellowship, 33% have reached permanent civil service status.
  • Urban Fellows Program retention: 77% of the 2024-2025 cohort stayed in City government; 38% of the 2025-2026 cohort stayed, with some still in process.
  • Summer Internship Program: over 900 interns hired across 27 agencies in the most recent year.
  • Public Service Corps: 106 interns across 7 agencies in FY2025; 122 interns across 13 agencies in FY2026.
  • DCAS conducted 308 outreach events in FY2025 that counted toward Office of Citywide Recruitment targets; 178 met Civil Service Ambassador Program requirements specifically, up from 95 qualifying events in FY2024.
  • Of 170 Civil Service Ambassador qualifying events in FY2025 referenced in the Council briefing paper, only 7 were held on Staten Island.
  • Median time from exam administration to list establishment: 101 days, down from 158 days previously; target is under 120 days for Police Officer exams.
  • Of 208 civil service exams open for filing in FY2025, 57 were completed in fewer than 100 days.
  • Civil service exam applications received: 119,117 in FY2026 versus approximately 166,000 in FY2025, with the decline attributed to the mix of exams offered rather than declining interest.
  • Approximately 20,000 positions were filled through the NYC Jobs website in FY2025.
  • The NYC Jobs Newsletter reaches more than 250,000 New Yorkers monthly.
  • DCAS social media cumulative reach: over 460,000 impressions in the most recent month analyzed, representing a 251% year-over-year increase; engagement up 329%; video views up 408%.
  • Approximately 30% of civil service exam applicants request a fee waiver of some kind.
  • Since implementation of Local Law 57 of 2025, approximately 2,000 individuals have requested first-time test taker fee waivers and 57 high school students have requested fee waivers.
  • DCAS has reviewed approximately 116 civil service titles for minimum qualification requirements, resulting in amendments to 69 titles, impacting an estimated 53,000 individual positions.
  • Civil service exam lists are valid for up to four years; exams are typically administered on a two-to-three-year cycle, except high-need titles like Police Officer, which are examined monthly.
  • The 2019 DCAS citywide hiring assessment involved conversations with over 10,000 prospective employees and current City staff; its findings were never made public.
▸ Action Points
  • DCAS to provide data on how many of the Staten Island Civil Service Ambassador events resulted in actual hires.
  • DCAS to provide a concrete number on how many job applications submitted through the NYC Jobs website were for positions agencies did not yet have approval or funding to fill.
  • DCAS to follow up with CM Morano on how many hires can be directly attributed to social media recruitment campaigns.
  • DCAS to check and report back on whether DOHMH requested emergency recruitment support from the Office of Citywide Recruitment during the Legionnaires' disease outbreaks on the Upper East Side and in the South Bronx.
  • DCAS to provide data on total recruitment events conducted in partnership with the Department of Veterans Services, including veteran-specific outreach.
  • DCAS to provide a breakdown of which civil service exams the approximately 2,000 first-time test taker fee waivers and 57 high school fee waivers were applied to.
  • DCAS to report on exact fee waivers issued in the last fiscal year by category.
  • DCAS to provide data on which trade schools it visited in 2025 and whether those schools were selected to align with specific upcoming exam titles.
  • DCAS to consider developing exit surveys or other tracking mechanisms for Civil Service Pathways Fellows who complete the program but do not enter full-time City employment, and report back to the committee.
  • DCAS to follow up with the Mayor's Office of Talent and Department of Education to obtain enrollment data for the Youth Apprenticeship Program and share with the committee.
  • DCAS to explore, in coordination with sister agencies overseeing juvenile justice facilities, providing justice-involved youth with referrals to CUNY courses and civil service career readiness materials.
  • DCAS to provide data on CUNY-specific civil service outreach events and career and technical education schools visited, including counts by school.
  • DCAS to revisit and improve the methodology for tracking how applicants first learned about civil service jobs and exam opportunities, given acknowledged unreliability of current application-question data.
  • DCAS to share with the committee a roadmap for building a global accountability framework tracking the full hiring lifecycle from exam posting through agency onboarding, including agency-level time-to-hire data.
  • Public witnesses from Citizens, Citizens Union, and Abundance New York to share their joint spreadsheet of actionable civil service reform recommendations with the committee for use in developing potential legislation.
  • Committee to pursue follow-up on whether OMB posting authority approvals can be better aligned with existing agency headcount authorizations to reduce redundant approval steps.
